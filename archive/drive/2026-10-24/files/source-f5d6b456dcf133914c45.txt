MAJOR OPEN PROBLEMS
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

This document lists the current research frontier in dependency order.

The program's main unresolved problem is now upstream:

    derive a physically natural situated Perspective and its consequential
    continuation from the local structure of universal quantum possibility.

Everything downstream becomes more precise once that problem is solved.

The open problems below are grouped by layer rather than by historical origin.


1. PHYSICAL LOCAL STRUCTURE

The first problem is to identify the correct mathematical carrier of intrinsic local structure in quantum reality.

The current schematic notation is:

    (Omega_phys, tau_phys),

where tau_phys stands for physical neighborhood, continuity, accessibility, or local geometry.

Ordinary topology may be insufficient.

Candidate richer structures include:

- projective Hilbert geometry;
- operator topologies;
- causal structure;
- local operator algebras;
- process-composition structure;
- boundary/gluing structure;
- decoherence geometry;
- sheaf-like locality;
- quantum information geometry.

Research question:

    What structure does quantum theory supply that is intrinsic enough
    to ground physically meaningful neighborhoods without analyst choice?


2. CANONICALITY

The local structure must be stable under physically irrelevant redescription.

Important transformations include:

- basis changes;
- gauge transformations;
- coordinate changes;
- equivalent process representations;
- alternative tensor-network descriptions;
- admissible spacetime foliations;
- relabelings.

Research question:

    Which quantities are invariant or covariant under the equivalence
    transformations that leave the physical situation unchanged?


3. PERSPECTIVE DERIVATION

A Perspective should be a physically situated structure inside the universal quantum object.

Candidate ingredients include:

- bounded causal access;
- stable records;
- local support;
- subsystem structure;
- environmental correlations;
- current compositional boundary;
- available interactions.

Research question:

    What physical criterion individuates a Perspective without simply declaring
    an observer or subsystem by hand?


4. PERSPECTIVE BOUNDARY

Even if a Perspective is local, its boundary may be fuzzy, dynamical, or scale-dependent.

Possible physical criteria include:

- interaction strength;
- causal separation;
- predictive closure;
- informational bottlenecks;
- decoherence boundaries;
- control interfaces;
- local algebraic inclusion.

Research question:

    When do several degrees of freedom form one situated Perspective rather
    than several overlapping or nested Perspectives?


5. RECORD FRONTIER

The current picture treats the present as a record-conditioned frontier rather than a universal cosmic now.

Research questions:

- Which records define the physically accessible past?
- What makes a record stable?
- How is present support separated from merely latent environmental correlation?
- Can the frontier be defined covariantly?
- How does it evolve as new records form?


6. RELATIVISTIC FORMULATION

A final theory should not require a preferred global foliation.

Possible settings include:

- causal diamonds;
- algebraic QFT;
- covariant histories;
- world-tube boundaries;
- general-boundary formulations;
- process networks.

Research question:

    Can the Perspective/present construction be stated locally and covariantly
    in relativistic quantum field theory?


7. QUANTUM HISTORY STRUCTURE

The project needs a way to represent compatible histories without assuming one underlying classical trajectory.

Research questions:

- Which histories are physically meaningful relative to a Perspective?
- How should coherent alternatives be grouped?
- How does decoherence create approximately classical history structure?
- How should incompatible consistent families be handled?
- What is the relation between history structure and local quantum process structure?


8. TRAJECTORY DIRECTION

In semiclassical regimes, phase gradients and stationary-action paths provide momentum-like trajectory direction.

The general quantum notion is unclear.

Research questions:

- What is trajectory direction outside canonical semiclassical mechanics?
- Is it a tangent object, process orientation, current, causal direction, or boundary relation?
- How should it be defined for QFT or indefinite causal structure?


9. REMOTE FOCUS

Focus represents a remote condition toward or from which histories are resolved.

Candidate mathematical forms include:

- effect operators;
- boundary conditions;
- postselection-like probes;
- asymptotic conditions;
- families of compatible futures.

Research question:

    Can focus be represented physically without assuming that the remote
    condition is actually measured or ontologically selected?


10. FOCUS-RELATIVE HISTORY BUNDLES

A present support plus a remote focus can correspond to several coherent history contributions.

Research questions:

- When does a focus define a stable bundle?
- How should interference inside the bundle be represented?
- When does the bundle admit a quasiclassical trajectory approximation?
- Does a focused decoherence functional provide the right tool?


11. QUANTUM UNCERTAINTY AND PERSPECTIVAL RESOLUTION

Different ways of resolving the universal structure may correspond to noncommuting observables or incompatible process questions.

Research question:

    Can perspectival resolution provide a useful unifying interpretation of
    quantum incompatibility without pretending that uncertainty is caused by
    perspective itself?


12. RESIDUAL CONTINUATION

Once Perspective is physically identified, the program needs the conditional process that remains available from it.

Research questions:

- Is the residual object a state, process tensor, comb, local algebra state, history functional, or something richer?
- How is it conditioned on records?
- What memory must it retain?
- How does it compose across boundaries?


13. CONSEQUENCE EXTRACTION

The central downstream map is schematically:

    Gamma_P = K_P(Omega_phys).

K_P should preserve differences that genuinely alter continuation while discarding redundant physical detail.

Research question:

    What makes consequence extraction a natural factorization of the physical
    structure rather than an analyst-selected feature map?


14. EXACT SUFFICIENCY

In finite classical systems, causal states and predictive state representations provide exact sufficient representations.

The quantum/process analogue is less settled for the project's purposes.

Research question:

    Under what conditions does a compact exact sufficient state exist for
    a situated quantum continuation?


15. APPROXIMATE SUFFICIENCY

Real systems require approximation.

Research questions:

- What physically meaningful distortion measure should be used?
- Is predictive rate-distortion appropriate?
- How should compression preserve rare but highly amplifiable distinctions?
- How should multiscale approximations relate?


16. OPERATIONAL DISTANCE

Operational process discrimination supplies candidate metrics.

The open issue is how these metrics relate to the intrinsic topology-first physical structure.

Research question:

    Are discrimination metrics fundamental geometry, local probes of a deeper
    geometry, or context-dependent approximations?


17. EFFECTIVE CONSEQUENCE GEOMETRY

Gamma_P may possess:

- topology;
- metric;
- orientation;
- measure;
- causal order;
- process composition;
- stratification.

Research question:

    What is the minimal geometric structure required to describe consequential
    continuation without imposing unnecessary coordinates?


18. GLOBAL VERSUS LOCAL GEOMETRY

Local quantum information geometry may be smooth while global continuation is branching, disconnected, or singular.

Research questions:

- How should local and global structure be combined?
- What replaces tangent geometry at branch points?
- Can metric entropy and local Fisher-like geometry coexist in one framework?


19. BORN WEIGHT

Born weighting belongs downstream of the fine-grained coherent quantum structure.

Research questions:

- At what stage are alternatives decoherent enough for additive Born probability?
- How should Born weight enter consequence volume?
- Should support and weight remain separate outputs?
- How are extremely low-weight but structurally broad continuations represented?


20. HORIZON

Consequence depends on continuation depth.

Research questions:

- Can a canonical horizon-independent object be reconstructed from the full filtration?
- How should delayed consequence and recombination be represented?
- Which asymptotic properties are stable?


21. MULTISCALE STRUCTURE

The same system supports descriptions at:

- quantum;
- molecular;
- cellular;
- organismic;
- social;
- ecological scales.

Research question:

    Which coarse variables are naturally selected by consequence-preserving
    structure, and how do higher-level patterns inherit reality from lower-level dynamics?


22. PATTERN REALITY

A higher-level pattern should be robust enough not to depend on one arbitrary encoding.

Research questions:

- What family of admissible descriptions should robustness range over?
- How much invariance is sufficient for objective emergent patternhood?
- How should predictive closure and intervention relevance be combined?


23. AGENCY REALIZATION

The program has a structural model of agency as endogenous policy-sensitive deformation.

Research questions:

- What is the minimal physical criterion for action?
- How should policy be identified in a distributed system?
- When does feedback become genuinely endogenous control?
- How should self-modification preserve agent continuity?


24. GENERATIVITY

Generativity is the capacity to reach states that expose new consequential structure.

Research questions:

- How should new capability be distinguished from simple amplification?
- Can recursive generativity be measured?
- How should tools, learning, reproduction, and institution-building be represented in a common formalism?


25. LUSHNESS CARRIER

Before defining one scalar, the program needs the correct structured carrier.

Possible components include:

- effective dimension;
- metric entropy;
- persistence profile;
- reachability;
- weighted geometry;
- generative structure;
- redundancy.

Research question:

    What mathematical object best represents the richness of nonredundant
    consequential possibility?


26. LUSHNESS SCALARIZATION

A scalar may be useful in restricted regimes.

Candidates include:

- log determinant;
- metric entropy;
- effective dimension;
- integrated horizon profiles.

Research question:

    Under what assumptions does a scalar preserve enough of the underlying
    geometry to be meaningful?


27. RANDOM MEMORY

Permanent random memory is a difficult benchmark.

Research question:

    If random distinctions remain perfectly readable and causally available,
    how much Lushness should they contribute relative to structured generative memory?

The answer should emerge from the geometry rather than from intuition.


28. GAS AND TURBULENCE

Large physical systems can contain huge state spaces and coherent structures.

Research question:

    After consequence-preserving compression, how much nonredundant continuation
    remains in gases, turbulence, and other complex dynamical media?


29. QUANTUM-GRAVITY FLOOR

The current physical program does not assume a Planck-scale consequence threshold.

Research questions:

- Does quantum gravity introduce a natural minimum distinction?
- Are there sub-quantum continuation structures?
- Is the present quantum topology only effective?


30. VALUER DEMARCATION

A valuer is a process for which continuation differences matter endogenously.

Research questions:

- What is the weakest sufficient physical criterion?
- Is valuerhood graded or categorical?
- How much self-maintenance is required?
- Is explicit future modeling necessary?
- How much policy flexibility is required?


31. CONSCIOUSNESS AND VALUERHOOD

The current conjecture treats consciousness as potentially sufficient for valuerhood.

Research questions:

- Which consciousness architectures instantiate endogenous orientation?
- Is phenomenal valence required?
- Which nonconscious systems qualify as valuers?
- Can consciousness and agency dissociate strongly?


32. VALUER INDIVIDUATION

Research questions:

- When is a colony one valuer or many?
- Can overlapping valuers exist?
- Can a distributed institution be a valuer?
- How should split or fused systems be counted?


33. STANDING

Valuerhood does not by itself settle moral standing.

Research questions:

- Which valuers have claims on others?
- Does consciousness change standing?
- How should intensity, duration, or phenomenal valence matter?
- Can nonconscious valuers have standing?


34. REDUNDANT CONSCIOUS COPIES

Physical consequence geometry may treat copied structures as redundant.

Moral intuition may still treat distinct conscious loci as separately significant.

Research question:

    How should redundancy in field geometry coexist with numerical multiplicity
    of phenomenal subjects?


35. MULTI-VALUER COMPATIBILITY IN REAL SYSTEMS

Finite May/Robust mathematics is established.

Research question:

    What physical requirements correspond to real valuer continuation, and how
    should they be inferred rather than simply registered?


36. NON-CAPTURE

The project needs a noncircular definition of independent situated valuation.

Research questions:

- What makes one valuer independent of a larger controller?
- Is separate policy formation required?
- Is recoverable exit sufficient?
- Can a dominant system manufacture fake plurality?


37. GENERATIVE INTEGRATION

Integration can reduce lower-level independence while producing a new higher-level agent or valuer.

Research question:

    When is capture destructive, and when is integration genuinely generative?


38. TERMINAL SOLITUDE

Terminal solitude is structurally clear as absence and non-regenerability of independent valuation.

Research questions:

- What is the correct scope?
- What counts as independent?
- Can a single integrated system contain genuinely independent internal valuers?
- Is terminal solitude always normatively bad?


39. AGGREGATION

Multiple valuers may have incomparable stakes.

Research questions:

- Is there any canonical aggregation rule?
- When are claims fungible?
- Which comparisons should remain partial?
- Can compensation cross identity boundaries?


40. AUTHORITY

Even a correct optimization procedure does not answer who may impose it.

Research questions:

- Who defines the valuer register?
- Who sets horizons and repair classes?
- Who verifies threats?
- Who may contain a dangerous agent?
- How is the authority itself prevented from capture?


41. POWER ASYMMETRY

Reciprocal compatibility can fail when one agent controls the board.

Research questions:

- What structural protections remain under extreme asymmetry?
- How can weaker valuers retain correction or appeal?
- Can defensive containment be formally bounded?


42. NORMATIVE BRIDGE

Gradient Ethics uses the premise that affected valuers' endogenous continuation gradients provide reasons.

Research questions:

- What is the exact strength of this reason relation?
- Is it defeasible?
- How does it interact with rights, virtue, and consequential comparison?
- Can some part of the bridge be derived from agency or rational consistency?


43. PHENOMENAL COUPLING

Let

    kappa
        subseteq
    P_phys x E

represent physical/phenomenal correspondence.

Research questions:

- Is kappa identity, emergence, dual aspect, or psychophysical law?
- Why does one organization have one phenomenal character?
- What binds one subject?


44. EXPERIENCED TIME

Physical records and temporoperceptual integration explain much of the structure of an experienced present.

Research question:

    What remains to be explained about the phenomenal sense of passage once
    memory, anticipation, and local physical succession are fully modeled?


45. BRANCHING SUBJECT IDENTITY

Research questions:

- Does a subject branch?
- Do branch successors become distinct subjects?
- How does Born measure relate to first-person expectation?
- Is phenomenal actuality merely self-location or something stronger?


46. SOUL / STRONG IDENTITY

Research question:

    What world-supported identity relation makes changing sections belong to
    one creature across deep transformation, copying, fission, or re-embodiment?


47. DIVINE CONSCIOUSNESS

The theological architecture distinguishes manifest completion from awareness.

Research question:

    Does the global manifest structure instantiate one phenomenal subject,
    and if so, what grounds that relation?


48. DIVINE WILL

Research question:

    When does complete structural order amount to intentional will rather
    than impersonal lawfulness?


49. DIVINE GOODNESS AND THEODICY

A fully branching actual universe can contain horrific continuations.

Research questions:

- How is divine goodness compatible with them?
- Can global generativity compensate local irreversible suffering?
- What would non-fungibility imply for theodicy?


50. PRIORITY ORDER

The present dependency order is approximately:

    physical local structure
        -> Perspective
        -> record-conditioned present
        -> quantum history / focus / trajectory
        -> residual continuation
        -> consequence extraction
        -> effective geometry
        -> Lushness
        -> agency / valuer realization
        -> multi-valuer compatibility in physical systems
        -> standing / aggregation / authority
        -> consciousness correspondence
        -> metaphysical and theological completion.

Several downstream branches can be studied in parallel.

The upstream physical problems have the greatest leverage because they determine which later abstractions are genuinely inherited from reality.
