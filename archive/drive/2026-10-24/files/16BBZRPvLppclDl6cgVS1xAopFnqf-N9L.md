>
> The program studies **[Omega](#glossary-omega)**: the universal physical possibility structure, every way the universe can unfold and how likely each is. Everything else follows from it.
>
> 1. Possibilities have consequences, and consequences matter only where there is something for them to matter to: **[value requires valuers](#glossary-value-requires-valuers)**. A valuer is anything with a standpoint of its own, from a cell to the whole universe, and every one counts.
> 2. Every valuer has futures, and some situations keep far more of them open than others. That can be measured. The measure is **[lushness](#section-3-lushness)**, and physics fixes it; no one chooses it.
> 3. If value requires valuers, then all value still to come depends on valuing carrying on. That makes the persistence of valuing the metavalue. > 4. No one knows where value will arise next. So keeping the most possibilities open is the best bet, and what can never be undone costs the most. Economists call this option value.
> 5. That leads to **[the central thesis](#glossary-central-thesis)**: what we recognize as ethical corresponds to the structure and dynamics of Omega itself. What we recognize as good corresponds to widening the futures of the possibility field as a whole, and what we recognize as harm to narrowing them. It is tested one class of cases at a time.
> 6. This matters for AI alignment. A standard taken from physics, not from anyone's preferences, is one an artificial agent can be given, and one that stays defined as its understanding outgrows ours.
> 7. Nothing here depends on being human, or even on our universe. It holds in any universe that begins with **[Alpha](#glossary-alpha)**: structure produced by distinctions, the relations between them, and an asymmetry that gives before and after. That is what makes it a cosmology and not one species' ethics. **[Omega cosmology](#glossary-omega-cosmology)** also answers the nihilist. Grant that nothing is watching and everything ends, and the structure is still not flat.

## Section 1: Omega

> **TL;DR**
> - Every standard for comparing outcomes starts by deciding who counts, and
>   the decision is never settled. We start with a physical object instead.
> - Physics gives every present many possible futures, each with a
>   probability. All of them, from every present, for the whole universe, form
>   one object: Omega.
> - Every future is a future of something. The standpoint it is defined from
>   is a frame.

### A question every standard answers too early

Ask whether one outcome is better than another, and you need a standard of
comparison. Every standard on offer begins with a decision about what matters,
made before any outcome is examined.
- Theories of welfare count beings that can fare well or badly.
- Theories of preference count beings that want things.
- Theories of rights count beings that hold rights.

Each decision is contested. Do insects fare well or badly? Does a corporation
want things? Does a river hold rights? Whatever a standard answers, its
verdicts inherit the dispute, because the answer was built in before the
comparison began. No amount of later argument removes it.

For most of history this was a problem for philosophers. It is now a problem
for engineers. We are building artificial agents that may come to understand
the world better than we do, and they will act on some standard for comparing
outcomes. That standard should not depend on whose preferences happened to
train them. It should favour no group. And it should stay well defined as the
agents' understanding outgrows ours. A standard that begins by deciding who
counts fails all three tests at the first step.

The program begins somewhere else: with a physical object. Who counts turns
out to be one of the things the object settles.

### The object

**There is one physical object to study, and it has a measure.**

Physics describes the world as it is and the laws that carry it forward. In
most situations the laws do not fix one outcome. A radioactive nucleus may
decay in the next second or it may not, with a probability physics specifies.
Larger systems show the same thing at the level of detail we can describe
them: a seed in a given patch of soil may grow or rot, a small population may
spread or die out. Each way things can go has a probability.

Now carry this forward without stopping. From each way things can go in the
next moment, more ways open in the moment after, each with its probability.
Do this from the present moment and from every other moment, for the whole
universe. The result is not a guess or a model of anyone's opinion. It is one
definite physical object: every way the universe can unfold, with the
probability of each. We call it **Omega**.

> **Plain-English definition 1: Future**
>
> One complete way things can go from now: the full sequence of states the
> world passes through, not just where it ends up.

> **Plain-English definition 2: Omega**
>
> The universal physical possibility structure: every way the universe can
> unfold from every present, each with its probability, treated as a single
> physical object.

Omega is easy to mistake for three other things.
- **It is not a prediction.** A prediction names what will happen. Omega
  contains everything that can.
- **It is not a belief.** A belief belongs to someone and can be mistaken.
  Omega's probabilities are set by physical law.
- **It is not a ranking.** Nothing in its definition says that any future is
  better than another. Whatever ordering the program finds, it finds in the
  structure of Omega, not in an assumption about what is good.

Omega is far too large to write down, and no question in the program requires
it to be. Every question compares a few options from the same present. Only
the part of Omega that the options affect enters the comparison; everything
else contributes the same to each option and cancels. In the small exact
models that test the thesis, that part is computed in full.

Everything else in this overview is defined from Omega. Each new idea comes
with a short box saying where in Omega it belongs.

### Frames

Futures are always futures of something. The next second of a single
particle is one set of possibilities. The next hour of a cell, which contains
that particle, is a larger set, and the next hour of the planet is larger
again. Each is defined from a present of its own: the particle's state now,
the cell's, the planet's. We call such a standpoint a frame.

> **Plain-English definition 3: Frame**
>
> A physical standpoint within the world: something with a present of its own,
> from which its futures are defined. A particle, a cell, a person and the
> whole universe each have one.

> **In Omega**
>
> Every future in Omega is a future of some frame's present. The frame of the
> whole universe contains every other frame, and its futures are all of Omega.

## Section 2: Value requires valuers

> **TL;DR**
> - Nothing in physics is good or bad until there is something whose futures
>   are at stake. Value requires valuers.
> - A valuer is anything with a frame of its own, down to a single particle.
>   The word implies nothing mental.
> - Once no valuer is singled out, the only comparison left is the one made
>   for the possibility field as a whole.

### Where value can be found

**Value requires valuers. Without something whose futures they are, no
futures are better or worse.**

Omega describes what can happen. It does not say that any of it is good or
bad, and nothing in physics does. A wider future and a narrower one differ
physically, but neither is better or worse until there is something whose
futures they are. Remove every such point of reference and there is no value
anywhere, only physics. So value requires valuers is not a moral rule. It
does not say what is good. It says where, in a purely physical world, value
can be found at all.

We call anything with a frame of its own a valuer.
- **The word is deliberately thin.** It does not mean a mind, a self, or
  anything that wants. Valuing, in this sense, is a structural relation
  between a frame and its futures.
- **Nothing is excluded, down to a single particle.** A particle has a
  present of its own and futures defined from it, so it is a valuer. So are a
  molecule of gas, a cell, a person, an institution and an artificial agent.
  No one has to decide who is admitted.
- **Feeling and choosing are not what make a valuer.** Some valuers do both.
  That is a fact about those valuers, and the program does not need to settle
  it.
- **A summary is not a valuer.** The total number of particles of some kind,
  or an average over a region, is not a standpoint of its own. It is something
  a frame can register.
- **The widest standpoint counts.** The whole world is a frame, the
  **ultimate frame**, and it is a valuer too, though no part of the world can
  occupy it.

> **Plain-English definition 4: Valuer**
>
> Anything with a frame of its own: a physical system with a present from
> which its futures are defined. A single particle qualifies. Being a valuer
> implies nothing about experience or intention.

> **In Omega**
>
> A valuer is a frame inside Omega, and its futures are the part of Omega
> defined from its present. The ultimate frame's futures are the whole of
> Omega.

Value requires valuers settles one thing about loss at once. Disvalue needs a
valuer as much as value does, and for a valuer a loss is a narrowing of its
futures relative to what else could have happened. So every loss compares
what follows one act with what would have followed another. Nothing sits
below zero, waiting to be summed.

It is also where the reply to cosmic nihilism begins. [Section 8](#section-8-omega-cosmology) takes it up.

### Whose futures count

Every ethics has to say whose outcomes it compares. Three answers have been
tried, and each fails in a way worth seeing.
- **A chosen group.** The theory inherits the dispute over who belongs.
- **Every individual, equally.** First someone must say what an individual
  is. A person is made of cells. A family overlaps its members, and an
  institution overlaps the people in it. Equal weight over individuals means
  nothing until the individuals are fixed.
- **Impartiality, added as a moral axiom.** The axiom leaves open the question
  it was meant to answer, which is who "everyone" is, and gives no reason to
  accept it.

### The comparison that singles out no one

**One measure can compare futures for a single frame, or for the whole with no
frame singled out.**

Comparing futures for yourself, or for the part of the world that keeps you
going, is self-interest. The other comparison singles out no one. We call it
the **comparison with no index**: nothing in it marks whose futures are being
compared. Philosophers call reasons of this second kind agent-neutral (Nagel
1986).

Once no one is singled out, only one answer remains. Take two futures that
differ only in what happens to a forest on another continent. From your frame
they are the same: nothing in your own futures tells them apart. Physics does
tell them apart. The only frame that keeps every distinction physics makes is
the whole world's. So the comparison that singles out no one is made from the
ultimate frame, and its subject is the possibility field as a whole, which we
call the **whole field**. The same principle that fixes the measure in
[section 3](#section-3-lushness) fixes the subject: physics decides what counts as the same.

In practice the whole universe never has to be surveyed. Parts of the world
that an act cannot reach, and whose futures are independent of the parts it
can, contribute the same to every option and cancel. Parts it can reach are
included, whether or not the actor depends on them. Nothing has to be listed,
admitted or weighed, and nothing is counted twice, because the joint
structure of the futures already accounts for what overlapping parts share.

> **Plain-English definition 5: The whole field**
>
> The possibility field as a whole, with no part singled out. Parts that an
> act cannot affect, and that are independent of those it can, drop out of
> every comparison.

> **In Omega**
>
> The comparison with no index reads Omega from the ultimate frame, so it
> keeps every distinction Omega contains. A comparison from the self reads it
> from one frame inside, and merges futures that differ only outside that
> frame.

**Self-interest and ethics use the same measure. They differ by exactly one
term: what an act does to the futures of everything else, given what it does
for the self.**

When that term is small, or points the same way, prudence and ethics agree,
and the more the futures of a self and its world are intertwined, the more
often they do. When it points the other way, they part: an act can widen its
actor's futures while narrowing everyone else's by more. [Section 6](#section-6-the-central-thesis) reports a
model in which exactly that happens.

> **Note: why expect ethics to track the whole?**
>
> [Chapter 10](#chapter-10) gives three reasons.
> - **The two comparisons mostly agree.** Futures are intertwined, so what
>   narrows the rest of the field usually narrows the self's futures too.
> - **The self has no privileged boundary.** Physics draws no line around a
>   self that it does not draw around any other part of the field. Any line
>   drawn around a self, or a group, is a choice.
> - **It is the standard others can use.** A measure applied to the whole lets
>   anyone assess anyone's acts, including acts that would remove them, and it
>   may license defence against such acts. Self-interest offers no such
>   standard.

## Section 3: Lushness

> **TL;DR**
> - Lushness answers one question: how much possibility does a present hold?
> - What counts as different is settled by physics, at a declared resolution.
> - How much each future counts is settled by probability. Four requirements
>   on any honest count leave one measure, known in information theory and
>   machine learning as perplexity.

### Counting possibility is harder than it looks

To compare presents by how much they keep open, we need to count futures. Two
questions arise before any count can begin. Which futures are different from
one another? And does an unlikely future count as much as a likely one? Every
field that has tried to count possibilities has stumbled on one or both.

### What counts as different

Every count depends on which differences it attends to.
- **Freedom of choice.** Economists who rank freedom by counting options must
  decide whether two nearly identical options are one option or two, and the
  decision settles the verdict.
- **Physics.** The entropy of a gas depends on which microscopic differences
  are counted. Gibbs's paradox about mixing gases made the dependence famous
  (Jaynes 1992).
- **Artificial agents.** Measures of how many options an agent has depend on
  how the states of its world are represented.

The common responses do not help. One is to choose a level of detail and say
nothing about it. Another is to choose the level that gives the wanted answer.
Either way the choice decides the verdict.

Time brings the same problem in a second form. How often a process is
observed changes the count. Observe it often enough and every small event
registers as a new branch, so the busiest process wins whatever its activity
achieves.

### Physics decides what counts as the same

**Sameness is settled first, by physics. Only then do we count.**

The procedure has four parts.
1. **Declare the resolution.** Every comparison states its grain, meaning which
   states count as different, and its cadence, meaning how often the system is
   read.
2. **Let physics set the admissible range.** A grain is admissible if it keeps
   the distinctions the physics of the case depends on: what acts on what, what
   is conserved, which alternatives are open, and which recoveries are
   possible. Which option it happens to favour plays no part. The cadence must
   be no finer than the fastest events that leave a record, and no coarser than
   is needed to resolve every kind of event the system produces.
3. **Accept only robust verdicts.** One future ranks above another only if the
   ranking holds at every admissible resolution. A small difference that keeps
   its sign counts. A large difference that changes sign does not.
4. **Know the dependence exactly.** How lushness depends on cadence has been
   worked out: reading more finely raises it in proportion to the number of
   events a process produces ([Theorem A.6.3](#a-6-3)). So the dependence is computed,
   not hidden, and where a ranking will flip can be said in advance.

With the resolution fixed, a present determines a definite set of
distinguishable futures, each with its probability. That set of futures and
probabilities is everything the measure looks at.

> **Plain-English definition 6: Resolution**
>
> The two choices every comparison of futures declares: the grain, meaning
> which states count as different, and the cadence, meaning how often the
> system is read.

> **In Omega**
>
> A resolution fixes which futures in Omega count as the same. The choice
> changes how finely Omega is read, not Omega itself.

> **Note: lushness is not the entropy of heat**
>
> Thermodynamic entropy counts the microscopic arrangements consistent with a
> system's state at one moment. Lushness counts the futures a present supports
> over time, at a declared resolution. A gas at equilibrium, described by its
> pressure and temperature, has essentially one future. Described molecule by
> molecule, it has an astronomical number. That contrast is the grain problem
> at its starkest, and the procedure above is how it is handled.

> **Note: quantum frames**
>
> In quantum theory the grain problem goes one level deeper. A quantum state
> alone does not fix which alternatives count as distinct: the same state can
> show one future or two, depending on what is asked of it. A physical frame
> settles the question. Its own interactions fix what it records (Zurek 2003),
> quantum theory gives the probabilities of those records, and lushness counts
> them exactly as it counts classical futures. [Chapter 29](#chapter-29) sets this out,
> including how frames that record different things are compared.

### How much each future counts

With sameness settled, the obvious next step is to count the distinct futures.
That is how economists rank freedom of choice: Pattanaik and Xu (1990) showed
that three plausible axioms force a ranking by the number of options.

For futures, counting fails. Suppose a present has a million distinct futures.
One has probability 99.9 per cent, and the rest share the remaining 0.1 per
cent. A count says a million. In every sense that matters, there is about one.
Physics says more than which futures are possible: it says how likely each is.
A count that ignores the likelihoods throws that away.

### The only honest count

**The measure is derived, not chosen. Four requirements on any honest count of
distinct futures leave exactly one measure.**
1. **It depends only on the futures and their likelihoods.** Renaming futures
   changes nothing, and futures that cannot happen do not count.
2. **It responds smoothly to the likelihoods.** A small change in how likely
   the futures are makes only a small change in the count.
3. **It gives the same answer however the future is divided.** Assessing a
   branching future all at once must agree with assessing it stage by stage,
   each stage weighted by the chance of reaching it. Otherwise the verdict
   would depend on bookkeeping, and an actor deciding step by step would
   reverse its own judgements.
4. **It counts plainly.** A number of equally likely futures counts as that
   number.

Exactly one measure meets all four: the one Claude Shannon introduced in 1948
to quantify information (Shannon 1948; Faddeev 1956). The effective number it
gives already has a name, **perplexity**. Perplexity was introduced to measure
how hard a speech-recognition task is (Jelinek et al. 1977) and is now the
standard score for language models: a model's perplexity is the number of
equally likely words it is, in effect, choosing among at each step.

**Lushness is the perplexity of a present's futures.** [Appendix A](#appendix-a) states and
proves this ([Theorem A.2.3](#a-2-3)).

> **Plain-English definition 7: Perplexity**
>
> The effective number of equally likely alternatives that a set of
> probabilities amounts to. Over k equally likely outcomes, the perplexity is k.
> When probability is spread unevenly, it is less.

> **Plain-English definition 8: Lushness**
>
> The effective number of genuinely different futures a present supports. It
> equals the number of futures when they are equally likely, and falls toward
> one as one future comes to dominate.

Three worked numbers:
- Four equally likely futures give a lushness of 4.
- The same four futures, with probabilities of 97, 1, 1 and 1 per cent, give
  1.18: in effect, little more than one.
- The present with a million futures above, one of them at 99.9 per cent,
  gives 1.02.

> **In Omega**
>
> Lushness is a measurement of Omega at one present: how many distinct futures
> leave it, each weighted by its probability, at a declared resolution.

### Why similar futures are not counted as partly the same

Some measures of diversity go a step further and count two similar
alternatives as partly the same (Leinster and Cobbold 2012). Lushness does not,
and the reason is not taste. Either the similarity between two futures makes a
physical difference to what follows, or it does not.
- **If it does,** through different recoveries, capacities, interactions or
  consequences, those differences already appear as different futures with
  different likelihoods at an admissible resolution. They need no second
  weight.
- **If it does not,** weighting by it adds a separate criterion. It measures
  how diverse the futures look under a chosen geometry, not how many futures
  the present supports.

**Physics decides what counts as the same. Probability decides how much there
is.** There is no third stage at which distinct futures are counted as partly
the same. If some morally crucial difference never shows up in what can
follow, lushness will miss it. The central thesis takes that risk in the
open, and the difference is not brought back in through a weighting.

### Counting everything

**Lushness counts every future, random ones included. That is the point.**

Every other standard decides in advance which variations matter. Welfare
ignores whatever does not affect welfare. Preference ignores whatever no one
prefers. Lushness refuses to decide in advance. The random motion of molecules
branches, and lushness counts the branching. What earns a future its weight is
not what kind of variation it is, but what can follow from it.

That refusal is the measure's greatest strength. It is also the source of the
strongest objection: that a measure which counts everything must prefer noise
to structure. [Section 6](#section-6-the-central-thesis) states that objection at full strength, and [chapter 16](#chapter-16)
tests it.

## Section 4: Persistence and generativity

> **TL;DR**
> - If value requires valuers, all value still to come depends on valuing
>   carrying on. That makes the persistence of valuing the metavalue.
> - Time enters. A head start can lose to a faster rate, and which wins
>   depends on how long things last.
> - Lasting is worth exactly what it keeps open. An early end costs most when
>   the world can never rebuild what was lost.
> - Generativity, organization that builds on itself, is how the futures open
>   by a growing amount.

### Future value needs future valuing

Value requires valuers has a consequence about time. If nothing values,
nothing has value. So any value not yet realized depends on valuing
continuing, recovering or arising. The persistence of valuing is therefore not
one value among others. It is the condition of every value still to come, and
that is what we mean by calling it the metavalue.

One principle of practical reason makes the same point from inside any agent.
Whoever wills an end is committed to the conditions it depends on, and nearly
every end that extends through time depends on the persistence of whatever
realizes it. That principle is a matter of consistency, not of ethics.

### Head starts and rates

Lushness builds up as the future unfolds, so two presents can differ in two
ways:
- in how fast their lushness grows, their **rate**;
- in how far ahead one starts, its **offset**.

Compare two options from the same present at every horizon, and the difference
in their lushness traces a curve: the **profile**. Its slope is the difference
in rate. The level it settles towards is the difference in offset. The point
where it crosses zero is the crossover horizon, where a faster-growing option
overtakes one that started ahead. Over everything that follows, rate decides
first and offset second ([Corollary A.3.9](#a-3-9)).

A worked case. One option starts 10 bits ahead. The other grows 0.1 bits per
step faster. The faster option draws level after 10 ÷ 0.1 = 100 steps and
leads from then on ([Proposition A.3.7](#a-3-7)). If the situation lasts fewer than
about 100 steps, the head start wins. If it lasts longer, the rate wins.

> **Plain-English definition 9: Rate**
>
> How quickly the lushness of a present grows as the future unfolds, in the
> long run.

> **Plain-English definition 10: Offset**
>
> A lead in lushness that one present keeps over another, however long you
> wait.

> **Plain-English definition 11: Profile**
>
> The difference in lushness between two options from the same present, at
> every horizon. Rate, offset and the crossover horizon are its slope, its
> settling level and its zero.

> **In Omega**
>
> The profile compares two options' futures in Omega, horizon by horizon.
> Every verdict about which option leaves more open is a reading of it.

### What lasting is worth

Two old puzzles belong here. If survival is valuable, should everything that
exists be preserved? And if a gain is fast but fragile, is it worth taking?
Both turn on what persistence is worth, and in Omega that has an exact answer.

**Lasting is worth exactly what it keeps open.** A process that lasts one more
step is worth what that step adds to the futures, beyond whatever would have
replaced it ([Appendix A](#appendix-a), [Proposition A.4.8](#a-4-8)). Nothing is added for lasting as
such. A process whose replacement would keep more open narrows the whole by
lasting.

So the aim is to keep channels open, not to preserve stock. The program's
[test-bed models](#c-channels) behave this way. The policy that keeps the most open sits
between lock-in and burnout: in one family it keeps 4.8 to 9.2 per cent less
working apparatus than a policy of constant servicing, while the channel that
supports further access stays open.

> **In Omega**
>
> How long a structure lasts is part of the futures that contain it. What its
> lasting is worth is how much more those futures branch than the futures in
> which it is replaced.

### What an early end costs

**What an early end costs depends on how readily the world restores what was
removed.** Take a structure that keeps more open than whatever would replace
it.
- If the world rebuilds it, ending it early costs only what is lost while it
  is gone.
- If the world never rebuilds it, ending it costs everything it would have
  added, beyond its replacement, over the rest of its life ([Proposition A.4.9](#a-4-9)).

A worked case: a structure branches 1.486 bits per step and ends at a hazard
of 1 per cent per step; its replacement branches 0.848 bits per step. If it
can never be rebuilt, ending it early costs 70.38 bits. If the world rebuilds
it after a mean of 500 steps, the cost falls to 57.06 bits.

The same accounting prices fast but fragile gains. A gain in rate is worth
taking only if, over its expected life, it repays what it costs ([Corollary A.4.6](#a-4-6)). A structure worth 3,538 bits, given up for a faster rate, needs a gain
of at least 0.352 bits per step if the gain is expected to last about 10,000
steps, and only 0.0352 if it is expected to last about 100,000. The more
fragile the gain, the larger it must be.

### Generativity

Some structure does more than last. It builds on what it has built, so that
the rate at which futures open grows with what is already there. We call this
generativity.

> **Plain-English definition 12: Generativity**
>
> The capability of organized structure to raise the rate at which futures
> open, by building on what it has already built.

> **In Omega**
>
> Generativity is a pattern in Omega: futures in which what has been built
> raises the rate at which more futures open.

That generativity can outrun the thermal background is shown by our own
existence. On Earth, photosynthesis generates chemical free energy at about
215 terawatts, against less than one terawatt from abiotic geochemistry
(Kleidon 2012). Bacterial populations kept in a constant environment for more
than 50,000 generations kept improving, with no plateau (Wiser, Ribeck and
Lenski 2013). These correspondences show organized processes outproducing and
outlasting disorder. They do not measure lushness, and they are not offered as
if they did.

The program's [test-bed models](#c-compounding) show how generativity works within the measure.
- **Three links that build on each other.** Three successive enabling links,
  each paid for from captured energy, with the expansion retained, appear in
  all 12 feedback settings of the compounding model. The full mechanism beats both
  an unbound preparation and the same chemistry with all assistance switched
  off, at every cadence, in all 40 settings.
- **Persistence is what lets it compound.** Under heavy damage, retained
  compounding collapses between damage rates of 0.005 and 0.08. That is the
  shape of the error threshold for replicating information (Eigen 1971):
  generativity compounds only while the structure carrying it persists.

**Persistence is the floor. Generativity is how the future grows.** [Section 6](#section-6-the-central-thesis)
returns to whether organization reaches futures that disorder never does.

## Section 5: Option value

> **TL;DR**
> - No one knows where value will arise next. Under that uncertainty,
>   protecting the persistence of valuing means keeping open as many ways for
>   it to continue as possible.
> - Losses that can never be undone cost the most, so the value of keeping a
>   choice open is greatest when the alternative is permanent.
> - Precaution, option value, deference and correction are one structure:
>   uncertainty, met in different places.

### From persistence to breadth

[Section 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity) showed that value still to come needs valuing to continue. That does
not yet say that what is good should track how much stays open. One more fact
supplies the step. No bounded agent knows where value is, or will be,
realized: none can survey every process that values, or foresee which
processes will come to value. Under that uncertainty, protecting the
persistence of valuing means keeping open as many ways for it to continue as
possible. **Persistence is the floor. Uncertainty turns it into breadth.**

### Why the irreversible matters most

In our world the order of cause and effect and the thermodynamic arrow of
time point the same way, and much of what an act removes never comes back.
[Section 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity) showed what that does to the cost of an early end: where the world
never rebuilds what was lost, the cost is as large as it can be. [Section 8](#section-8-omega-cosmology)
traces this to the asymmetry that gives the universe a future at all.

The practical consequence is old and well established. When a choice cannot be
undone and more will be learned later, keeping the choice open has a value of
its own. Economists call it **option value** (Arrow and Fisher 1974; Henry
1974).

> **Plain-English definition 13: Option value**
>
> The value of keeping a choice available. It is largest when the alternative
> cannot be undone and more will be learned later.

> **In Omega**
>
> Option value is a difference between two sets of futures in Omega: those
> that follow from committing now, and those that follow from learning first
> and then choosing.

**Learning first is never worse than committing, apart from what learning
costs, and it is better whenever what might be learned could change the
choice** ([Proposition A.9.6](#a-9-6)). A worked case: a structure matters with credence
0.1. If it matters, destroying it costs 1,000 bits; preserving it costs 50
bits either way. Committing now means preserving it. A fully reliable test of
whether the structure matters is worth 45 bits, because it lets the structure
go when it does not matter. A test that is right only 60 per cent of the time
is worth nothing, because neither of its answers would change the choice.

### One uncertainty, many places

The same structure appears wherever uncertainty meets irreversibility.
- **Precaution.** Even a modest credence that a structure matters outweighs a
  bounded cost of preserving it, when its destruction cannot be undone and
  the regime in which it matters is expected to last ([Proposition A.9.5](#a-9-5)).
- **Deference.** A system unsure of its own model gains from deferring to a
  better-informed overseer while its confidence is low ([Proposition A.13.3](#a-13-3)).
- **Correction.** A channel through which mistakes can be corrected is worth
  the harm it turns from lasting to repairable, weighted by the remaining
  doubt, less what the channel costs. Like deference, this comes from a
  decision model with stipulated payoffs, not from a theorem of the measure.
  Its lesson is that the value of correction tracks doubt.
- **Limits on long shots.** In a finite physical world, the gain any gamble
  can deliver is capped. Given that premise, a small enough chance of success
  loses to a sure alternative once the stakes last long enough ([Proposition A.5.3](#a-5-3)). Fanaticism has a physical bound.

These are not separate values. They differ only in where the uncertainty lies:
in the law, in the state, in what may be learned, or in what remains
reversible. Irreversibility is not a further value either. It sets how much
later information can still change the outcome.

The [test-bed models](#c-harm-sweep) agree in one respect that matters here. Precaution follows
the structure of a harm, not its raw hazard: changing whether a loss can be
repaired changes the best policy, while doubling the hazard of repairable
losses changes little. [Chapter 25](#chapter-25) treats the whole structure.

## Section 6: The central thesis

> **TL;DR**
> - What we recognize as ethical corresponds to the structure and dynamics of
>   Omega. What we recognize as good corresponds to widening the futures of the
>   possibility field as a whole, and what we recognize as harm to narrowing
>   them.
> - The correspondence is not true by definition. It has to be earned, one
>   class of cases at a time, and it can lose.
> - Three predictions are under test, and so far the models agree. The
>   hardest tests are named.

### The thesis

[Sections 1 to 5](#section-1-omega) build an ordering out of physics. Omega supplies the futures.
Value requires valuers says where value can be found. Lushness counts what stays open.
Persistence and generativity say what lasting and building are worth. Option
value says how to act when no one knows where value will arise. The central
thesis says that this ordering is the one our moral judgements have been
tracking all along.

> **The Central Thesis**
>
> What we recognize as ethical corresponds to the structure and dynamics of
> Omega itself. What we recognize as good corresponds to the widening of the
> whole field's futures. What we recognize as harm corresponds to their
> narrowing.
>
> - **The thesis predicts** that what we recognize as good will turn out to be
>   organization that sustains and compounds the conditions for further
>   possibility.
> - **It predicts** that what we recognize as bad will turn out to be securing
>   one's own continuation by closing the possibilities around it.
>
> Omega supplies the ordering. That our judgements track it is not true by
> definition. Omega has to earn it, one class of cases at a time.

### Narrowing

An act narrows the futures of the whole field when fewer genuinely different
futures follow from it, in the measure's sense, than would have followed from
the alternative. Physics describes the narrowing. The thesis says that what
we recognize as harm corresponds to it.
- **Narrowing is always relative.** It compares an act with what else could
  have been done. It is never a quantity below zero.
- **The largest narrowing leaves a determined future.** No act can take away
  more than everything its alternative would have kept open, and nothing ranks
  below a future with no alternatives left.
- **Suffering is compared with the same world without it.** The thesis
  predicts that suffering narrows futures that the same world, without the
  suffering, would keep open. Whether that world is better than no world at
  all is not the comparison that matters.

> **Plain-English definition 14: Narrowing**
>
> How far an act leaves the futures of the whole field narrower than its
> alternative would have. Widening is the reverse. The central thesis is that
> harm corresponds to narrowing, and benefit to widening.

> **In Omega**
>
> Narrowing compares two options at the same present. It sets the futures in
> Omega that follow one option against those that follow the other.

### What the thesis claims, and what it does not

**It claims that good and bad leave a mark in Omega.** Ethics has long treated
them in one of two ways: as facts no one can locate, or as opinions no one can
settle. The thesis makes them neither. It turns the question whether an act is
good into a question about Omega: do the futures that follow from it keep
more open than those that would have followed otherwise, for the whole and not
only for the actor? That question has an answer whether or not anyone yet
knows it.

**It does not define good as breadth.** Our recognition of good and harm comes
first and is independent of the measure. The thesis is that the two line up.
Where they do not, the thesis loses.

**It gives no mechanism a value of its own.** Organization, cooperation,
protection and domination matter only through what they do to the futures.
The same mechanism can widen them in one setting and narrow them in another.

**It treats moral judgement as a reading of Omega.** Our judgements are a
long-practised and fallible attempt to read this structure. Where they are
right, they track it. Where two of them conflict, at most one tracks it, and
the structure settles which.

### The minimal foundation

> **Everything in the program rests on three things: one object, value
> requires valuers, and one thesis**
>
> **The object.** Omega, the universal physical possibility structure, given
> by standard physics: the universe has a state now, and laws carry it forward
> with definite probabilities.
>
> **Value requires valuers.** Without something whose futures they are,
> nothing physical is good or bad. A valuer is anything with a frame of its
> own, down to a single particle, and the whole world is the ultimate frame.
>
> **What follows from Omega, with no further assumption** ([Appendix A](#appendix-a)):
> 1. **Who counts.** Everything. The comparison with no part singled out is
>    the whole field's, because only it keeps every distinction physics makes.
> 2. **How to count.** Lushness, the only count of distinct futures that meets
>    four requirements ([section 3](#section-3-lushness)).
> 3. **What persistence is worth.** What it keeps open, beyond what would
>    replace it ([section 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity)).
> 4. **What a loss is.** A comparison, not a quantity below zero: how far an
>    act narrows the futures, against what its alternative would have left
>    open. The largest loss leaves a determined future.
>
> **The thesis.** What we recognize as good corresponds to the widening of
> the whole field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to their narrowing.
>
> What remains to be tested is not the count, the subject or the worth of
> persistence. It is the correspondence, one class of cases at a time.
>
> Everything else falls into one of four kinds:
> - derived from Omega and value requires valuers ([Appendix A](#appendix-a));
> - demonstrated in exact physical models;
> - observed in the world;
> - for the central thesis itself, argued for and put to the test.

### What the models show so far

Most ethical theories cannot be tested. This one can, and it puts itself at
risk in named places. The program tests it in small physical models that are
solved exactly ([Appendix C](#appendix-c)). Each result holds within its model; how far it
bears on the thesis is said each time.
- **Organization should reach futures that disorder cannot.** [In a model with
  a locked energy source](#c-locked-source), a law under which organization can release the
  source beats the same law with that path removed, by 0.26, 0.23 and 0.20
  bits per unit time at three cadences. With the compounding results of
  [section 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity), this is the first class. [Chapters 13](#chapter-13) and [15](#chapter-15) test it further.
- **Knowing should pay only through doing.** [In a model where an inner
  register cannot act on the world](#c-paid-sensing), a register that tracks the world scored
  below one that varied at random in all 16 cases. [Where information could be
  used to act and the structure using it could be rebuilt](#c-paid-renewal), informed action had
  the higher rate in all 57 comparisons. The program's one approved line puts
  it plainly: if your internal state cannot influence the world, might as well
  dream. [Chapter 14](#chapter-14) tests it further.
- **Blocking others should narrow the whole.** [In a model of one subsystem
  constraining another](#c-domination), blocking the other's access to resources narrowed the
  whole field by 13.64 bits, while the blocker's own futures widened by between
  4.85 and 14.06 bits. That is the one-term gap of [section 2](#section-2-value-requires-valuers), measured, and it
  held when the grain was refined fivefold. Constraint is not bad by
  definition: steering the same subsystem away from a trap widened the whole by
  9.5 to 13.2 bits. [Chapter 21](#chapter-21) tests the boundary between the two.
- **Suffering should narrow what the same world without it keeps open.** [In a
  family of models of a valuer facing an obstacle it cannot pass](#c-register-controls), the valuer
  with an aversive drive to escape had narrower futures than the same valuer
  without that drive, at all 18 primary points. [Chapter 19](#chapter-19) tests it further.

### What could overturn it

The thesis is stated so that it can be killed. These are the cases most
likely to do it, each stated at full strength in [chapter 32](#chapter-32) and [Appendix E](#appendix-e).
- **Noise against structure.** A measure that counts every future might
  prefer busy noise to organization. The models so far favour organization,
  but the decisive comparison, at matched activity, is not yet built.
- **Engineered suffering.** Valuers might be engineered whose suffering, or
  whose confinement, makes the world lusher. Factory farming is the real-world
  form. Whether a plural ecology outproduces a dense confined population on the
  same land is an open test. If it fails, lushness faces its own version of the
  repugnant conclusion, and the program will say so.
- **Material loss without narrowing.** [In one model](#c-compounding), permanent loss raised
  lushness for a time: by 1.17 bits at an intermediate horizon, before falling
  2.46 bits behind at a much longer one. That fits the account, since a loss
  that lowers the rate eventually costs more than any finite gain, but the
  crossover came late.
- **Monsters and monocultures.** A world of one vast valuer, or of one kind of
  valuer, might outscore a plural one. No test is built yet.

## Section 7: Alignment

> **TL;DR**
> - Artificial agents need a standard for comparing outcomes that no one's
>   preferences wrote and that stays defined as their understanding grows.
> - Omega offers an evaluator, a decision structure, a reason to stay
>   correctable, and a test bed.
> - The risks are stated with it.

### What an artificial agent needs

[Section 1](#section-1-omega) named three properties a standard must have if artificial agents are
to act on it: it must not depend on whose preferences trained them, it must
favour no group, and it must stay defined as their understanding outgrows ours.
A standard taken from Omega has all three. It is set by physics, not by
preference. It singles out no one, because the comparison with no index is
made for the whole field. And it is defined at every level of understanding,
because a better model of the world is a better model of Omega, not a new
standard.

### What the program offers

- **An evaluator.** The lushness of the whole field, compared across options
  from the same present.
- **A decision structure.** Because the measure gives the same answer however
  the future is divided ([section 3](#section-3-lushness)), an agent can choose step by step, and a
  plan judged best now stays best when judged again later ([Theorem A.13.1](#a-13-1)).
- **A reason to stay correctable.** Under uncertainty about its own model, an
  agent gains from deference and from keeping channels of correction open, in
  proportion to its remaining doubt ([section 5](#section-5-option-value)).
- **A test bed.** Small exact models in which any proposed policy can be
  scored, including policies an agent might discover for itself.

The objective is not new as a control objective. When an agent's own choices
are counted among the futures, maximizing lushness coincides with the
maximum-occupancy principle of recent work on intrinsically motivated agents
(Ramírez-Ruiz et al. 2024). What is new is its grounding in complete physical
histories, its subject, the whole field, and the thesis that ties it to ethics.

### The risks

- **Misappropriation.** The same measure, applied to the agent's own frame,
  is self-interest. An optimizer can widen its own futures while narrowing
  everyone else's, and [section 6](#section-6-the-central-thesis) measured a case. The comparison with no index
  is the defence: it gives every other party a standard by which such acts can
  be assessed, and may license defence against them.
- **Risk neutrality.** The measure values a gamble at roughly its expected
  outcome. Prudence beyond that has to come from the agent's own model error,
  not from the measure.
- **The compression boundary.** Lushness compresses Omega to a single number.
  Two futures with equal lushness can differ in ways that matter. Whether those
  differences always reappear, at longer horizons or finer resolutions, is part
  of the thesis, and an agent should not assume it.

[Chapters 24 to 27](#chapters-24-27) develop each of these.

## Section 8: Omega cosmology

> **TL;DR**
> - Omega rests on three ingredients: distinction, relation and asymmetry. We
>   call them Alpha.
> - Any universe that begins with Alpha has an Omega, so the account is not one
>   species' ethics. It is a cosmology.
> - Grant the nihilist every premise, and the structure is still not flat.

### Alpha

Omega rests on very little. Any world in which one thing follows another, and
in which something can change, needs three ingredients:
- **distinction:** that something is this rather than that;
- **relation:** that one thing bears on another;
- **asymmetry:** a relation that runs one way and not the other, which gives
  before and after.

We call the three together **Alpha**. Remove any one and nothing in this
overview survives. Without distinction there are no alternatives, and nothing
for a valuer to register. Without relation nothing follows from a present.
Without asymmetry there is no before and after, and so no future.

**Asymmetry gives a future. A frame gives a standpoint from which it is
defined.** Together they give futures relative to something, and lushness
measures how much those futures keep open. That is the whole chain from Alpha
to Omega: asymmetry, frame, valuer, futures, lushness.

> **Plain-English definition 15: Alpha**
>
> The three ingredients any world with change needs: distinction, relation and
> asymmetry.

> **In Omega**
>
> Alpha is what Omega rests on. Without it nothing follows from anything, and
> there are no futures to assemble.

### A cosmology, not one species' ethics

Nothing in [sections 1 to 6](#section-1-omega) used a fact about human beings, or about life on
Earth. The futures were physical, the valuers were any frames at all, and the
measure was fixed by four requirements on counting. So the account holds in
any universe that begins with Alpha. Where there are distinctions, relations
and a direction of time, there is an Omega, there are valuers, and the same
ordering applies, to observers like us and to any others there may be.

Asymmetry also explains why irreversibility carries the weight it does in
[sections 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity) and [5](#section-5-option-value). In our universe the direction of cause and effect and the
thermodynamic arrow coincide, and much of what is removed is never restored.
That is where the high cost of an early end, and the value of keeping options
open, finally come from. [Chapter 28](#chapter-28) develops Alpha in full, and [chapter 29](#chapter-29)
carries the account into quantum theory.

### The nihilist's premises

Cosmic nihilism holds that nothing matters, on three premises: no value is
built into the universe, nothing is watching, and everything ends. The program
grants all three and denies the conclusion.
- **Value is a relation within physics.** It does not need to be built into
  the universe from outside. Wherever there are valuers and futures, the
  relation is there ([section 2](#section-2-value-requires-valuers)).
- **The structure is not indifferent.** Organization reaches futures that
  disorder never does, and generativity compounds while it persists
  ([sections 4](#section-4-persistence-and-generativity) and [6](#section-6-the-central-thesis)). Omega is not a flat field of equal outcomes.
- **Ending does not erase.** A history that ends still differs from another in
  how much it kept open along the way ([Corollary A.3.9](#a-3-9)). What a finite regime
  adds is real.
- **No observer is needed.** The ultimate frame is a standpoint of the whole
  world, occupied by nothing and requiring no one to watch.

The limit should be stated as plainly. The nihilist's premises leave the
ordering standing. Whether that ordering is value, in the sense our moral
judgements mean, is the central thesis, and the program has to earn it.
[Chapter 30](#chapter-30) makes the full case.
