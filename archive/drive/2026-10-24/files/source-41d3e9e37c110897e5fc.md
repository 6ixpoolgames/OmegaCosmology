## Evidence rubric

A result can support a claim only if its model instantiates the object the claim
is about. A model may still support claims about the objects it does instantiate,
such as a theorem about the measure, a local mechanism or a single-agent result.

Theory-bearing evidence for a claim requires all of the following:

1. **Matching.** Conserved quantities and resources are matched across the
   comparison.
2. **Presence.** The relevant coupling or subject is physically present. Claims
   about appropriation, domination, whole-field ethics or coupled futures need
   those relations in the model.
3. **A stable objective.** The primary objective was not changed after the
   results were seen.
4. **Accounting.** Resource accounting is sufficient for the claim.
5. **No built-in answer.** No analytical feature predetermines the ranking being
   sought.

**Rubric rules added in r7.** These are proposals by Codex, Sol and the
assistant, pending the originator's approval. Rows that rely on them (A22's
matching prescription, C17's factorial reporting) say so. The r7 rule on
post-hoc reclassification was removed in r9, per the originator's ruling of
15 October: guessed thresholds are not verdicts on the principle.

- **Matching includes internal degrees of freedom.** A comparator that removes
  a valuer's inner variation together with the feature under test compares
  against a simpler valuer.
  - **Matching prescription for "the same valuer without X":** keep the same
    state space, the same laws of activation and relaxation, and the same
    costs; remove only the coupling that constitutes X.
  - This is a declared methodological choice, not a derived identity criterion
    for valuers.
- **Resolution.** At each grain a comparison is resolved in one direction or
  unresolved, when the entropy bounds overlap. An unresolved grain supports no
  ranking either way.
- **Factorial reporting.** For two-factor mechanism contrasts, report the exact
  decomposition C + A + G (A17, item 5), together with all absolute scores.
  Report winners only together with where they sit relative to the boundary.
  Never add physical bills to entropy terms, and never replace the objective
  with an interaction term.

If any of the five criteria is missing, the result teaches us about the
diagnostic, not about the target claim. Classification follows the structure of
the model and applies equally to favourable and unfavourable results. An adverse
result is set aside only by naming the criterion its model fails.

**Reading results (r9, originator, 15 October).** Report what a run shows about
the principle. Predictions and thresholds filed before a run are working
guesses. A run that misses a guessed threshold while demonstrating the
principle is a bad guess, not a failed experiment. Adverse findings are those
that bear on the principle itself, such as a sign that reverses at an
admissible grain or horizon.

**Structural account.** Every important scalar result is reported together with
answers to nine questions:

1. What changed in Omega?
2. Which channel or dependency caused the change?
3. How robust, repairable or substitutable is that channel?
4. Does using the channel deplete it or maintain it?
5. Do descendants reconstruct or expand it?
6. What remains after it is lost, and what would be available without it?
7. What does finite-history lushness report?
8. What does the stationary rate report?
9. Are those results stable across the declared resolution corridor?

The structural account explains and predicts. It does not overrule lushness. A
disagreement between them is evidence about the compression boundary (A14) and
the central wager.

**Two experimental layers.**

- **Oracle layer.** The model is known exactly. It shows what lushness itself
  ranks.
- **Bounded-agent layer.** The controller has partial observation, uncertainty
  or a mismatched model. It shows what an embedded controller can know and
  implement.

Every row's evidence is tagged by layer. Precaution, correction and deference in
v4 derive from uncertainty, so oracle results neither confirm nor refute them.
This sentence is a proposed detail of Sol's two-layer ruling, not itself a
ruling.

A controller that receives perfect state information and re-plans from each new
present (receding horizon) still has the exact model. It belongs to the oracle
layer, as a check that a result does not depend on the restriction to stationary
controllers.

In bounded-agent experiments, report how much of the value of inspection or
consultation comes from its effect on later choices, and how much from the
randomness of the inspection event itself. Randomness counts, but the two must
not be confused. CORR-1 and S5 both showed probing chosen partly for its own
entropy.

## Fields

Each entry records:

- the **current wording**, quoted from the Drive chapter with its location;
- the **disposition**: retain, revise, qualify, demote or hold;
- the **proposed wording**;
- the **claim type**: physics, measure, empirical, normative or decision-theoretic;
- the **evidence class**: theorem or property of the measure, theory-bearing
  experiment, external correspondence (A12), diagnostic, failed probe, or
  conjecture;
- the **evidence**, the **pressure** (contrary results stay visible), the
  **dependencies** and the **pending test**.

Diagnostics are named in each entry for provenance. They are never cited as
support.

### A2. Preparation, process and mechanism

- **Current wording.** 4.5: "In a closed system that eventually forgets its
  starting point, all starting points share one rate, and the comparison falls to
  the offset (section 4.6)." Prologue status: "Organized preparations beat
  matched references in some regimes and lose in others."
- **Disposition.** Revise, by adding a three-way taxonomy:
  - **preparation offset:** the same law, different starts;
  - **process or policy rate:** the same physics, different controllers;
  - **mechanism contrast:** different laws.
- **Type and class.** Measure (the offset theorem) together with theory-bearing
  experiments.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Preparation offsets.** The organized root led the matched shell in all
    5,184 finite comparisons at capture factors 1–6. Some controllers gave
    negative offsets at factor 12.
  - **An organized preparation that loses.** The growing-population seed ended
    behind its matched reference by 1.56 bits. Its four-slot growth was largely
    turnover, so its organization did not persist. That is what the persistence
    relation predicts (C22), and v4 already says organized preparations win in
    some regimes and lose in others.
  - **Embodiment.** The embodied seed led its reference by 14.45 bits (reversible
    law) and by 25.80 bits (absorbing law).
  - **Mechanism.** The capture advantage raised the rate by 1.38 bits per unit
    time, but that is a contrast between laws.
- **The theorem, not a pressure (r9).** Roots that share one finite irreducible
  generator share its rate, so within such a generator no preparation can show
  a rate advantage. Those preparations differ by offsets; rate advantages there
  belong to mechanism contrasts. Earlier revisions listed this as a pressure,
  which misread the theorem as missing evidence.
  - **Scope (Codex).** The theorem is conditional on the shared finite
    irreducible generator. Growing or nonergodic systems can behave
    differently under one encompassing law: different preparations can reach
    different closed classes, or grow without settling, and then differ in
    rate.
- **Dependencies.** Finite irreducibility. In reducible worlds, the offsets
  depend on absorption weights.

### A4. Protecting channels, not stock

- **Current wording.** 5.3, "Persistence: the metavalue", and 5.4, strict and
  precautionary protection. The current text does not distinguish stock from
  capacity.
- **Disposition.** Revise.
- **Proposed wording.** Distinguish five things:
  - stock preservation;
  - functional availability;
  - recovery;
  - a continued channel of access;
  - generative capacity.

  Protection targets the capacity for further continuation. Maximal protection
  of particular structures can amount to lock-in. Margin is precautionary, graded
  by uncertainty and irreversibility.
- **Type and class.** Normative, with theory-bearing experiments as illustration.
- **Evidence (test bed, whole-world objective):**
  - **Between lock-in and burnout.** The breadth winner sits between the
    protective controls (idle, always service) and the active ones (always
    export, half/half) on every protection readout.
  - **Margin.** It keeps 4.8–9.2% less working apparatus than always servicing,
    across capture factors 1–12. That is what ruling 9 expects.
  - **Corrected in r9.** Several earlier reports scored this behaviour as a
    failure: "protection alignment is conditional" (paid capacity, 12
    October), shallow-well prediction S1, scored as failed because the winner
    retained less usable material than constant servicing, and "less functioning capacity
    than the passive benchmark" (growing substrate). In each, the breadth
    optimum kept slightly less stock or functioning than a protective control
    while the channel stayed open. Under ruling 9 (do not aim for maximum
    protection; keep the channel open) that is the expected behaviour, not a
    failure.
  - **Capability (ruling 6).** In the paid-capacity batch all 78 productive
    configurations beat their nonconducting arm and the same-law baseline. The
    report declined to call small margins a demonstration; under ruling 6 the
    capability is what counts, and 78 of 78 shows it.
  - **Recoverability.** Protection rises with recoverability: with shallow wells,
    the stationary usable fraction is 0.66 at depth 0 and 0.03 at depth −4.
- **Diagnostic, not cited.** The legacy surviving-hardware deep grid (closure
  2.106% against 1.080%). Its ranking was decided by the tail after closure.
- **Pressure.** Closure risk remains a diagnostic pressure point. See C9.
- **Consistent (reserve batch).** Doubling the hazard, and making shocks fully
  correlated, changed none of the 15 winners. In that fixture every harm was
  repairable (working apparatus became worn) or reversible (trapping at depth
  −1), so a reserve bought nothing that repair did not. With the channel never
  at risk, there was nothing for precaution to protect, as the
  channels-not-stock reading predicts. Earlier revisions listed this as a
  pressure.
- **Evidence (harm sweep, oracle layer).** The structure of harm changes policy.
  Irreversible loss of the powered repair hardware led the winner to service
  *less* than under fast repair, at T64 and T256 with h = 0.02. Hardware was
  mostly used rather than preserved: an 87–91% chance of losing it. The channel
  never closed, because basal construction and thermal recovery remained as
  substitutes. This is consistent with protecting the channel rather than stock.
  When one capability is lost irreversibly but substitutes keep the channel open,
  that particular structure is not protected.
- **Dependencies.** A5.

### A15. Compounding generativity in our universe

- **Current wording.** 3.6–3.7, and 3 (status, hypotheses): "Compounding
  generativity is the self-sustaining source of widening structure."
- **Disposition.** Revise, by adding support from the literature with its limits
  stated.
- **Proposed wording.** Over cosmic history, compounding complexity is documented
  empirically:
  - free-energy rate density rising from galaxies to societies (Chaisson 2001);
  - the major evolutionary transitions (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995);
  - mineral diversity roughly tripled by life (Hazen et al. 2008);
  - faster-than-exponential long-run growth of human population and ideas
    (Kremer 1993).

  Models reproduce compounding under stated assumptions:
  - autocatalytic sets (Hordijk and Steel 2004);
  - novelties that trigger novelties (Tria et al. 2014);
  - the adjacent-possible equation (Cortês et al. 2022).

  These are not theorems about physics, and free energy bounds them. At the
  finest grain, our universe's branching is dominated by thermal processes:
  black holes and radiation dominate its entropy (Egan and Lineweaver 2010).
  Under the capability formulation (A3) no large margin is required. Organization
  can still raise rate in two ways:
  - by unlocking gradients that disorder cannot release within the horizon (C3);
  - at grains on which durable, distinguishable outcomes register (B3).
    This second route leans on Rule A, which is not a standing ruling (struck
    15 October). Keep it conditional.
- **Addition (r8, originator's instruction): compounding beats the thermal
  background in the real world.** Cite these as external correspondences
  (A12). Full list in A21.
  - **Planetary.** Earth's atmosphere–ocean system holds about 2,326 J/mol of
    available Gibbs energy. That is at least an order of magnitude more than
    any other atmosphere in the solar system, and it is maintained mainly
    through life: N₂, O₂ and liquid water coexisting (Krissansen-Totton,
    Bergsman and Catling 2016).
  - **Free energy.** Photosynthesis generates chemical free energy at about
    215 TW, against less than 1 TW from abiotic geochemistry, one to two
    orders of magnitude more (Kleidon 2012). Lovelock's disequilibrium criterion for
    life is the historical source.
  - **Sustained adaptation.** Over more than 50,000 generations in a constant
    environment, fitness kept rising along a power law with no plateau (Wiser,
    Ribeck and Lenski 2013).
  - **Structure paying to beat thermal error.**
    - Kinetic proofreading spends free energy to push error rates below the
      equilibrium discrimination limit (Hopfield 1974; Ninio 1975).
    - Redundant organization yields reliable computation from unreliable
      components (von Neumann 1956).
    - Coding gives reliable transmission at any rate below channel capacity,
      despite noise (Shannon 1948).
  - **The limit of beating noise.** Replicated information persists only above
    a fidelity threshold (Eigen 1971). The test bed shows the same shape:
    retained three-link compounding collapses between damage rates 0.005 and
    0.08 (C23, r9).
  - **Information turning fluctuations into work.**
    - An experimental feedback engine converted information to free energy at
      about 28% efficiency (Toyabe et al. 2010).
    - The thermodynamics of information is reviewed by Parrondo, Horowitz and
      Sagawa (2015).
  - **Caution.**
    - England's (2013) dissipation bound on self-replication is disputed:
      Kolchinsky argues it does not constrain replicator growth and decay rates
      as commonly read. Do not lean on it.
    - These sources show organized, compounding processes outproducing and
      outlasting the thermal background in free energy, disequilibrium,
      fidelity and adaptation. They do not measure lushness.
- **Type and class.** Empirical, established elsewhere, and analytic models.
  This is support for compounding complexity, not lushness evidence as such.
- **Dependencies.** A3 and B3.

### A17. Additions to the formal core (chapter 12)

- **Current wording.** 12.5 (Proposition 6, rate and offset for finite chains)
  and 12.7. Chapter 7's derived result on consent and coercion has no
  proposition in chapter 12.
- **Disposition.** Add seven items (items 4–7 added in r7):
  1. **Control invariance.** Two processes with the same sufficient present and
     the same weighted law have equal lushness, whoever implements them. This
     formalizes chapter 7's derived result and supports A9.
  2. **Reducible chains.** For a finite chain with transient states and several
     closed classes, form Π from the absorption weights and the stationary law of
     each class. Then the rate is r = Πg, the offset b solves
     (I − P + Π)b = g − r with Πb = 0, and ℓ_n = n r + b − Pⁿb. This is
     implemented and checked by residuals in the test bed, and it extends
     Proposition 6.
  3. **Continuous-time readout.** A continuous-time process is read at a declared
     cadence δ through P_δ = exp(δQ). The cadence is part of the declared
     resolution (B3). v4 currently works in discrete steps.
  4. **Inner registers (r7).** Let S be an internal register and Y every other
     coordinate, read in the order Y, then S:

     > **ℓ(Y, S) = Σₜ H(Yₜ | past) + Σₜ H(Sₜ | past, Yₜ)**

     *In words:* a register adds exactly the part of its switching that the past
     and the rest of the present do not predict.

     - Compared with the same valuer whose register is held constant, the change
       in the first sum can have either sign.
     - **Theorem (Codex).** For fixed marginal history distributions,
       H(Y, S) = H(Y) + H(S) − I(Y; S). Under those constraints, independence
       maximizes joint entropy.
     - The register controls match occupancy and switch count, not full
       marginals. Their ranking (independent above injury-linked) is evidence
       for the mechanism, not an instance of the theorem.
  5. **Factorial decomposition (r7, Codex).** For two binary mechanism factors,
     information (I or R) and actuation (X or 0):

     > **ℓ_IX − ℓ_R0 = C + A + G**

     *In words:* informed action's advantage over uncoupled variation splits
     exactly into information alone, action alone, and their interaction.

     - The terms: C = ℓ_I0 − ℓ_R0, A = ℓ_RX − ℓ_R0, and
       G = (ℓ_IX − ℓ_I0) − (ℓ_RX − ℓ_R0).
     - Informed action wins exactly when G > −(C + A).
     - All terms are complete continuation differences. Physical bills are
       already inside them.
  6. **Temporal two-term expansion (r7).** At fine cadence τ:

     > **ℓ_T(τ) ≈ N̄(T) log₂(1/τ) + F(T)**

     *In words:* breadth splits into an activity term that diverges as the clock
     gets finer and a finite term set by which events happen and how unevenly.

     For two processes, the ranking flips at the crossover resolution,
     log₂(1/τ×) = (F₂ − F₁)/(N̄₁ − N̄₂) (B3).
  7. **Horizon crossover (r7).** If ℓ(T) = hT + b + o(1) for each arm, the
     finite-history ranking settles after

     > **T_× = −Δb/Δh**

     *In words:* an arm that starts behind but has the higher rate overtakes
     after the deficit divided by the rate gain.

     This is a diagnostic estimate, not an exact crossing. Exact finite-history
     probes near the estimate are retained.

     **Scope (Codex).** The formula is asymptotic, and it applies only to
     contrasts between mechanisms with different rates. Within one finite
     irreducible law, preparations have Δh = 0, and T_× is undefined. Their
     difference is a converged offset.
- **Type and class.** Theorems and definitions.

### B2. Rate-first as a decision rule

- **The crossover-resolution method (r7).**
  - For every primary comparison, report:
    - the expected event count N̄;
    - the finite term F;
    - the jump-chain (outcome-order) entropy;
    - the crossover resolution τ× (A17, item 6).
  - Claim a ranking only if τ× lies outside a physically declared admissible
    band [τ_min, τ_max]:

      variable;
    - τ_max is the coarsest cadence that still resolves every native event
      family.
  - **Why it helps.** It replaces sampling at three cadences with an exact
    location of the flip. It makes the Zeno divergence explicit and bounded. It
    separates what happens from how often things happen.
  - **Worked examples.**

    - The first-batch K1 flip. The temporal calibration (T6) located it exactly

      flip must be verified at the actual finite cadence.
    - **Dominance across resolutions (compounding batch).** When an arm leads in

      - ΔN̄ = +49.8 and ΔF = +99.5 bits at T64;
      - the loop's lead holds at every cadence;
      - this is the v4 target of 4.7, met in one comparison, within the
        expansion's validity.
  - **Supporting observations.**
    - Matched comparisons, such as A0 − A, kept their sign across cadences
      where unmatched ones flipped.
    - In paid renewal, pooled signs agreed across all three cadences at every
      horizon.
  - The temporal-profile calibration on the existing fixed pairs (T6) was
    completed before the renewal batch.
- **State grain.** Hiding a register yields a non-Markov observed process. The
  test bed bounds its entropy by conditioning inequalities, validated against
  exact enumeration. It never substitutes a lumped chain. Overlapping bounds are
  unresolved (rubric, resolution).

**C23 in detail (r9, robust-compounding run).** Count chemistry, well mixed,
with three catalytic roles, paid construction and capture, basal
reconstruction, and native stochastic processes. 40 settings, 144 cases, three
cadences, five horizons.

- **Three-link compounding: demonstrated.** Three successive paid enabling
  links (A→B→C→A) with retained expansion appear in all 12 shape settings,
  including square-root feedback.
  - Example (linear, full assistance, source 2.4): initial-seed three-link
    completion by T256 is 16.2%; completion with retained expansion, 11.1%.
    The tracker is conservative and misses true chains, so these are lower
    bounds.
  - Cutting any single link lowers retained expansion as measured without the
    link labels, so the ablation is not circular.
- **Lushness: the mechanism wins.** At T256 the full mechanism beats the
  unbound preparation, the all-assistance-off arm and the C-to-A cut at every
  cadence in all 40 settings, stressed settings included. Example: +165 bits
  over unbound and +366 over all-off at source 2.4.
- **Persistence: shown at baseline wear.** Functional uptime over 64–256
  reaches 50–55% with no closure at medium and high source, while compounding
  and the lushness lead hold.
- **Heavy stress: bounded.** At source 0.6, raising damage from 0.005 to 0.02
  and 0.08 drops completion-with-retained-expansion from 10% to 0.9% and about
  0.001%, and uptime from 50% to 11% and 0.1%. Three-link completion still
  occurs at damage 0.02 (9.8%), but most of it is not retained. Compounding
  happens while the structure persists; heavy damage stops it persisting. The
  full mechanism restores function better than the C-to-A cut. The lushness
  lead shrinks under stress but never reverses.
  - The collapse between damage 0.005 and 0.08 resembles an error threshold
    (Eigen 1971). Real compounding systems persist through copy number and
    repair. The next test sweeps those (A20).
- **Funding.** Across horizons the loop is mainly externally funded: the
  conservative external lower bound is 75% at T256, with the initial 3-unit
  packet fully deducted, and rises to 87.7% at T1024 and 92.7% at T4096. The
  80%-at-T256 threshold filed beforehand was a poor guess, because the initial
  packet has not amortized by then.
- **Thermal reference: wrong reference for the question.** The equal-energy
  count mixture starts with 4.26 apparatus on average and a complete loop 47%
  of the time; the seed starts with one apparatus. The comparison is therefore
  between more and less starting machinery, not between organization and
  disorder. Count chemistry has no positions, so it lacks the dispersal entropy
  that makes disorder the thermal default. Under ruling 7, the gas comparison
  belongs in the spatial model (C1a). The references also invert across models:
  on the lattice the seed beat the thermal mixture and lost to the unbound
  start; in count chemistry it beat the unbound start and lost to the thermal
  mixture. Reference outcomes are not comparable across adapters.
- **Feedback shape.** Square-root and linear feedback give nearly identical
  results. Check typical apparatus counts with Codex: at counts of 0–1 the two
  forms coincide, and at 2–3 n^0.5 is only 23–36% below n, so a saturating form
  may be the sharper test.
- **Pressure kept visible: permanent loss can add breadth for a while.** A
  disclosed post-run audit found that swapping repairable damage for 5%
  permanent loss raised T256 breadth in 4 of 8 contrasts, across all cadences,
  while lowering uptime. At α = 0.5 and source 0.15: +1.17 bits at T256, still
  positive at T1024, −2.46 at T4096. This fits v4's claim that a harm lowering
  the rate eventually outweighs any finite gain, but the crossover is late. See
  B2 and B8. It is a contrast between laws, not an agent choosing loss.
- **Late tails.** 18 of 40 settings meet the 0.001-bit offset-tail diagnostic
  by T4096; the slow material-loss cases are far from settled. Finite scores
  are exact and do not depend on the tail.
- **Engineering.** 32.9 minutes of capped worker time, at most 353 MiB. Energy
  residuals up to 1.2×10⁻¹¹; coordinate contributions sum to the entropy within
  5.3×10⁻¹⁰. 36 tests pass.
- **Scope.** Count chemistry only, with no translational motion. The run makes
  no ethical ranking. (Codex's report said the apparatus types are not
  valuers; under the originator's ruling of 15 October everything, gas
  included, is a valuer in the theory.) The run does not address open-ended
  expansion, network superiority or the spatial comparison (C1a).

- **r7, compounding boundary.** All four predictions held.
- **Retention.** The full loop retains apparatus near capacity: 3.94/4, 7.95/8, 15.89/16, 30.79/32. Each single-assistance ablation retains roughly a quarter of that or less, across all 24 laws.

The embedded jump-chain entropy rate is 1.05 bits per jump for the loop against 0.38 for basal, so each event is less predictable, not merely more frequent. At fixed jump counts the loop loses at 8 jumps (−1.76 bits) and wins at 32 and 128.

- **Damage.** Isolated, with thermal reconstruction fixed. It lowers retention as predicted.
- **Criterion corrected.** Stationary funding fractions sum to 1, so running-cost coverage above 1 cannot be a requirement. The marker is which source funds the loop.
