# Omega Cosmology v4.1: rough outline — r1

**Date:** 16 October 2026.
**Status:** a working outline for discussion. v4.1 is written from scratch
(ruling 23). The v4 chapters, ledger r9 and the program review are sources of
content, not texts to edit.
**Length:** book length. A rough guide is 180,000–230,000 words across eight
parts (the sum of the table at the end), plus appendices. The extra length goes to the detail and nuance the
experiments exposed.

## Governing discipline

These apply to every chapter.

1. **The headline claim** (Sol, adopted): the formal architecture remains
   intact. Several of its physical mechanism claims now have substantially
   stronger support. The central normative wager remains open.
2. **Every substantive claim carries a status,** recorded in the working
   files and visible in the prose wherever it matters:

   | Label | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | D | definition |
   | T | theorem, with assumptions and proof |
   | M | model result: shown in a test-bed adapter, within its scope |
   | E | empirical correspondence from the published literature |
   | N | normative premise, named |
   | C | conjecture or wager, able to lose |

   No sentence moves between these categories silently.
3. **Do not weaken Omega because a toy model surprises us, and do not
   strengthen it because a toy model agrees with us** (Sol). Models establish
   physical relationships and operating ranges.
4. **Firewalls:**
   - between each model result and the universal wager it bears on;
   - between the subject distinction and the move to ethics.
5. **Voice:** one voice, channelling Parfit:
   - arguments explicit, and numbered where they carry weight;
   - premises named;
   - objections stated at full strength before they are answered;
   - concrete physical claims;
   - austere formal sections;
   - worked examples instead of analogies;
   - short memorable lines only when they clarify.

   Standing rules: "program", "manuscript", author-year citations, an
   "In words:" line after every boxed equation, and only the approved
   aphorism.

**Decisions still pending, marked where they bite:**
- [P1] three tiers for compounding (ruling 7);
- [P2] a confirmatory protocol for central-wager tests (ruling 12);
- [P3] impartiality as a named premise (rulings 2 and 3);
- [P4] the "valuer" term (ruling 10).

## Part I — The object: physical possibility (3 chapters)

### 1. What follows from a present
- Omega, the futuresfield: complete physical developments from a present, in
  classical and quantum form (D).
- Reconvergence and re-rooting; ending against not knowing.
- What the structure is not: not a probability over beliefs, not a
  utility.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 1.

### 2. Frames, access and information
- Present frames and sufficiency (D).
- Access as what can actually be done; realization witnesses.
- Information is not access. This leads into Part III: information pays
  only through action.
- Weights from a frame; access over time; the ultimate frame.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 2.

### 3. Composition and intertwined futures
- Sequential and parallel composition; joint feasibility (D, T).
- Coupling, and why futures do not factor. This is the physical setting for
  the subject question of chapter 7.
- Control invariance: the same sufficient present and law give equal breadth,
  whoever implements them (T; A17, item 1).
- *Sources:* v4 chapters 1.3 and 7.1; A17.

## Part II — The measure: lushness (5 chapters)

### 4. Lushness
- Definition: finite-history entropy of the physical process; the chain rule;
  uniqueness within the class (D, T).
- Breadth versus its explanations: generativity, persistence and control
  explain breadth but are not terms in it (A1).
- Everything counts:
  - randomness, gas included (ruling 5);
  - stochastic costs need no separate term (A10);
  - repeated labels are not outcomes (B12).
- *Sources:* v4 chapters 4.1–4.4 and 12.2–12.3; A1, A10, B12.

### 5. Horizon, rate and offset
- Lushness is cumulative. Rate and offset as summaries; the reducible-chain
  identity (T; A17, item 2).
- **The offset theorem and its scope** (T): within a shared finite
  irreducible generator, roots share a rate. Growing or nonergodic systems can
  differ.
- **Preparation, process and mechanism** (D; A2): which comparisons the
  theorem decides in advance.
- **Crossover horizon** T× = −Δb/Δr (T, asymptotic; A17, item 7), with a
  worked example: the bracketed steering crossover.
- **Persistence and the generative window** (T, after audit; B8). Why a finite
  irreducible world shares its final rate, and why "rate" in practice means the
  rate during the window.
- Ruin with a residual process keeps its rate (A11). Short-termism and its
  mirror (A13).
- *Sources:* v4 chapters 4.5–4.6 and 12.5; A2, A11, A13, A17, B8; rate-first
  analysis r3.

### 6. Resolution and grain
- Cadence as part of the declared resolution (A17, item 3).
- The temporal expansion N̄ log(1/τ) + F (A17, item 6): why rankings flip,
  and the crossover resolution.
- **The resolution corridor** (B3): admissible grains, sign stability, and the
  difference between small differences and flips.
- Dominance across resolutions: the compounding loop as a worked case.
- State grain: hidden registers, conditioning bounds, unresolved overlaps. Bank
  resolution as a worked check (the C5 conflict is grain-stable).
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 4.7; B3; temporal calibration; fix note r2.

### 7. Subjects of the measure
- Marginal histories of a subsystem; one-observation and deeper bounds (D, T).
- The self and the whole field: one objective, different subjects (rulings
  1–3).
- Intertwining measured: mutual information between histories.
- Why private rankings are often unresolved (the measurement bottleneck), and
  what tighter queries would need.
- **A firewall:** this chapter establishes that subject-relative rankings
  differ. It does not establish which subject ethics should take (chapter 16).
- *Sources:* C5; B1; the policy-search reports.

### 8. What the measure compresses
- **The compression boundary** (A14): equal lushness, different structure.
  Whether the differences reappear is part of the wager.
- **Mixtures and risk** (T, after audit): the lushness of a physical gamble is
  the probability-weighted lushness of its outcomes, plus at most H(p).
- Valence and the measure: detection only through structural impact (ruling
  8). Stated as a limit of the measure's current reach, not an escape from
  refutation.
- Neighbouring objectives: maximum occupancy, empowerment and causal entropic
  forces (A19). The program's controllers as maximum-occupancy agents.
- *Sources:* A14, A19, A23; rate-first analysis r3.

## Part III — Mechanisms: how organization changes lushness (7 chapters)

This is the empirical heart of the book. Every result is labelled M or E,
with its adapter and scope.

### 9. The test bed, and how to read it
- What exact small models can and cannot show; the oracle and bounded-agent
  layers (ruling 14).
- **The evidence rubric:** matching, presence, a stable objective,
  accounting, no built-in answer. Diagnostics stay in provenance (ruling 4).
- Matching that includes internal degrees of freedom; resolution; factorial
  reporting (r7 rules, approval pending). Matching explains a result; it does
  not constrain an optimizer.
- **Exploration and confirmation** [P2]: guessed thresholds are not verdicts
  (ruling 12). Confirmatory tests freeze the model, comparator, grain and
  disconfirming observation before ranking.
- Reproducibility: frozen sources, receipts, independent recomputation.
- The single-model ideal (ruling 13): how far the current adapters overlap,
  and which properties have not yet been shown in the same model.
- Lessons from audits: the steering artifact; names are not flows.
- *Sources:* ledger rubric; code audit r1; fix note r2; experiment-workflow
  documents.

### 10. Access that disorder cannot reach
- The locked gradient: organization releases what disorder cannot within the
  horizon (M; C3).
- **The principle of counterfactual access:** organization adds rate in
  proportion to the access it provides beyond routes that need no
  organization.
- A worked lesson: the "uncatalysed" control that was a more permissive law.
- *Sources:* C3; A3; resource feedback.

### 11. Information, action and the dream line
- Inner registers and the chain rule (T): a register adds only its
  unpredicted switching.
- Paid neutral variation (M; A10). Information without leverage loses (M;
  A24, A26).
- **The factorial decomposition and renewal** (T, M): informed action wins
  through the interaction of information and action (57 of 57).
- The repayment horizon, and why mortal valuers did not live long enough.
- The approved aphorism: "If your internal state cannot influence the world,
  might as well dream."
- *Sources:* A10, A17 items 4–5, A24, A26, C17; renewal; paid sensing;
  register controls.

### 12. Persistence: structure pays when it lasts
- Δℓ ≈ −C + g·τ_up, with expected functional uptime (C22). How to avoid making
  it a tautology.
- Uptime and payoff in the robust-compounding run. Open questions about
  partial structure.
- Opportunity windows (open).
- *Sources:* A26, C22, C23.

### 13. Compounding
- Three tiers [P1]:
  - cumulative enabling in nature (E: Chaisson; Hazen; Kremer; and others);
  - a sustained rate gain from organized enabling in test-bed adapters (M);
  - generality to cosmological physics (C).
- The enabling loop: retention, funding, rate growth with capacity; matched
  accepted work (M; C4, C1b).
- **Three-link compounding** with retained expansion; stress and the error
  threshold (M; C23; E: Eigen 1971).
- Compounding beating the thermal background in the real world (E: Kleidon;
  Krissansen-Totton et al.; Lenski; Hopfield; Toyabe et al.).
- *Sources:* A15, A16, C4, C1b, C23; A21 correspondences.

### 14. Organization against disorder
- Gas baselines, and what each adapter can test. Count chemistry lacks
  dispersal entropy (B6).
- Same-law preparations on a lattice: a characterized regime, with the
  mobility caveat (M; C1a).
- The thermal background at fine grain, and the capability formulation:
  capability, not proportion (ruling 6; E: Egan and Lineweaver 2010).
- *Sources:* B6, C1a, A3; ruling 7.

### 15. Activity and productivity
- **The busyness objection, stated at full strength:**
  - paid flicker;
  - exchange turnover;
  - shams;
  - funded transfer;
  - selector cycling.
- The long-run pattern: activity pays now, capacity pays later (renewal,
  compounding, constraint, permanent loss).
- What would decide it: the activity-matched comparator (C2) and compression
  pair 4. Present the result if it has landed; state the objection as open if
  not.
- *Sources:* C2; A10; the matched-flow and C5 studies; the integrated control
  map.

## Part IV — Value (5 chapters)

### 16. From measure to value
- Value requires valuers; commitment carries to conditions; persistence as the
  metavalue; the vortex (from v4 chapter 5).

  agent), or the "every process contributes; any subsystem can be a subject"

- **The move from the self to the whole field** [P3]:

  - what intertwining does not do: derive the premise.
- Misappropriation by power-seeking controllers, and the impartial standard
  the whole-field reading gives others (ruling 3).
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 5.1–5.3; B1, B9; rulings 1–3 and 10.

### 17. Protection
- **Channels, not stock** (M; A4; ruling 9). Keep the channel open rather
  than maximize protection. Criticality is a statement about being, not about
  ethics.
- Dependence produces maintenance, within scope (M; A5).
- The structure of harm sets policy; raw hazard does not (M; C14).
- **Precaution in two layers** (ruling 22):
  - option value, which follows from the measure under uncertainty;
  - a margin from the actor's model error, which correction shrinks.
- Strict and precautionary protection, restated.
- *Sources:* A4, A5, C14, C15; v4 chapter 5.4; rate-first analysis r3.

### 18. Harm, recovery and irreversibility
- Harm as a physical signature; the verdict belongs to lushness.
- Recoverable against irreversible harm. A rate-lowering harm eventually
  outweighs any finite offset, but the crossover can be late (M; B2; C23
  permanent loss).
- What recovery is; redemption; turnover across scales.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 6.1–6.7; B2; C23.

### 19. Suffering
- **Structural disvalue** (A23): what the measure can register, and the
  resolution limit as a statement of reach, not an escape hatch.
- **The firewall:**
  - the model result (M): in the tested family, matched, obstructed suffering
    lowers lushness relative to the matched comparator;
  - the universal statement (C): kept as the wager.

  A reversal in an adequate model would count against the wager.
- Separating register variation from futile struggle; correcting E9 (A25).
- Signals: tracking that cannot act; hijacked signals and signal design
  (open).
- *Sources:* A22–A25, C16, C20, C21; register controls; v4 chapter 6.8.

### 20. The central wager
- The statement, its universal form, and why the program expects it to hold.
- **What would decide it:**
  - the compression suite;
  - equal-breadth pairs;
  - **the factory-farming objection** as the named adversarial target (A28,
    still open);
  - plural ecology against monoculture (C6).
- What failure would mean, including lushness's version of the repugnant
  conclusion.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 5.5–5.6; A14, A22, A28, C6.

   |---|---|

   | E | empirical correspondence from the published literature |
   | N | normative premise, named |
   | C | conjecture, able to lose |

3. **Do not weaken Omega because a toy model surprises us, and do not
   strengthen it because a toy model agrees with us** (Sol).
4. **Firewalls:**

   - between the subject distinction and the move to ethics.

   - each result is argued once, in the chapter where it does work, and other
     chapters refer back to it;

   - proofs and evidence rows go to the appendices.

   - arguments explicit, and numbered where they carry weight;
   - premises named;
   - objections stated at full strength before they are answered;
   - concrete physical claims;
   - austere formal sections;
   - worked examples instead of analogies.

   Standing rules: "program", "manuscript", author-year citations, an
   "In words:" line after every boxed equation, and only the approved

## Part I — The problem

The reader learns why comparing outcomes is stuck, and what a way out would
have to provide.

### 1. Who counts?
- **Question.** Every ethics compares outcomes. Why has no standard for doing
  so ever been settled?
- **Answer.** Every standard settles who and what counts before the inquiry
  begins, so every dispute about that starting point is a dispute about the
  whole standard. More argument cannot end it.
- **Builds.** A survey of the standards (welfare, preference, rights, virtue,
  contract, capabilities, biocentric ethics) and the starting points each
  depends on; the is–ought problem stated fairly.
- **Then.** Why artificial agents make the problem urgent: they need a standard
  that does not depend on whose preferences trained them, favours no group,
  and stays defined as their understanding grows.
- **Hands on.** Three requirements for a way out:
  - begin with no decision about who counts;
  - stay well defined;
  - be testable.
- *Primers:* moral philosophy; AI objectives.

### 2. Possibility as a standard
- **Question.** Others have tried to ground value in freedom, options or
  possibility. Why have they stalled?
- **Answer.** They stall at four points:
  - **grain:** what counts as a different option;
  - **weighting:** how likely options count;
  - **subject:** whose options;
  - **busyness:** whether noise counts as possibility.

  Any proposal must resolve all four.
- **Builds.**
  - Freedom of choice: Pattanaik and Xu.
  - Capabilities.
  - Option value: Arrow, Fisher and Henry.
  - Empowerment, causal entropic forces and maximum occupancy.
  - Open-endedness.
- **Hands on.** The four points become the agenda of Part II.
- *Status:* E (literature).

## Part II — The object and its measure

Each chapter opens with the ethical problem its physical tool resolves. The
reader then meets the tool.

### 3. What follows from a present
- **Question.** If not welfare or preference, what is there to compare?
- **Answer.** Everything that can follow from a present, with its
  probabilities: Omega. States cannot rank options. Under the fundamental laws
  the world's total information does not change with what we do. The
  differences lie in what can follow, and in what each part of the world can
  reach.
- **Builds.**
  - Plain-English definitions: future, Omega, present, frame, access.
  - **Orientation:** asymmetry gives a future; a frame gives the standpoint
    from which it is defined.
  - What Omega is not: a probability over beliefs, or a utility.
- *Primer:* probability and histories.
- *Formal:* A.1.

### 4. Counting futures
- **Question.** How much possibility does a present hold? The grain problem and
  the weighting problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** Physics decides what counts as the same, and probability decides
  how much there is. Four requirements on any honest count leave one measure,
  the perplexity of the futures: lushness.
- **Builds.**
  - resolution;
  - the continuation structure;
  - lushness;
  - why similarity weighting is excluded;
  - counting everything, randomness included;
  - the compression boundary: what the measure cannot see.
- *Primers:* entropy and perplexity; lushness is not the entropy of heat.
- *Formal:* A.1.1, A.2.

### 5. Futures through time
- **Question.** Should a head start you can see now count for more than a lead
  that grows later?
- **Answer.** Compare the whole profile of the difference. Its slope is the
  rate, its level the offset, and its zero the crossover horizon. Over
  everything that follows, rate decides first and offset second.
- **Builds.**
  - the profile;
  - rate and offset;
  - the crossover horizon;
  - preparation, process and mechanism contrasts;
  - the offset theorem.
- *Primer:* Markov processes.
- *Formal:* A.3, A.3.9.

### 6. How long things last
- **Question.** Should what exists be preserved? Is a fast gain worth taking if
  it may not last?
- **Answer.** Persistence is worth exactly the rate it sustains beyond whatever
  would replace it.
  - A gain must repay its cost over its expected life (the robustness floor).
  - An early end costs least when the world can rebuild what was lost, and most
    when it never can.
  - Long shots are bounded by a finite world.
- **Builds.**
  - random horizons;
  - what persistence is worth;
  - the cost of an early end and regeneration;
  - the generative window;
  - gambles and finite stakes.
- *Primer:* expected value.
- *Formal:* A.4, A.5.

### 7. Resolution: what counts as different
- **Question.** Rankings can flip with how finely or how often we look. Can any
  verdict be trusted?
- **Answer.** Declare the resolution. Let the physics set the admissible range.
  Accept only verdicts that keep their sign across it. A sign that flips means
  the comparison is unresolved, not tied.
- **Builds.**
  - admissible resolutions;
  - the temporal expansion;
  - the crossover resolution;
  - sign stability.
- *Formal:* A.6.

### 8. Parts and wholes
- **Question.** Whose futures? The subject problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** The measure applies to any part of the world or to the whole. A
  part never has more lushness than the whole. Self and whole differ by
  exactly one term: what an act does to everything else, given what it does to
  the part. Parts the act cannot affect drop out, and there is no ledger of
  subjects to keep.
- **Builds.**
  - projections and subjects;
  - bounds on a part's lushness;
  - intertwining;
  - the self–whole gap;
  - subjects can disagree;
  - unaffected parts cancel.
- *Primer:* mutual information.
- *Formal:* A.7.1–A.7.7, A.7.10.

## Part III — From physics to value

The turn from what there is to what matters.

### 9. Value requires valuers
- **Question.** Physics describes what can follow. Where does value enter?
- **Answer.** Value requires valuers: without something that values, nothing
  physical is good or bad. This is the link from the physical to the moral,
  not a moral rule.
  - A valuer is anything with a frame of its own, from which its futures can
    be projected.
  - Every physical system counts, gas included. The whole world is the
    ultimate frame.
- **Builds.**
  - valuer; the ultimate frame;
  - what makes a frame physically instantiated (the candidate, and the open
    problem);
  - persistence as the metavalue;
  - disvalue as narrowing;
  - the principle of practical reason;
  - why a hedonic definition would bring back negative totals.
- *Formal:* A.7.1; A.15.

### 10. The comparison with no index
- **Question.** Self-interest and ethics: two objectives, or one?
- **Answer.** One measure, two comparisons. The indexed one, for the self, is
  what prudence tracks. The one with no index, for the whole, is what the
  thesis says ethical judgement tracks.
- **Builds.**
  - agent-neutral and agent-relative reasons;
  - why expect ethics to track the whole (intertwining, no privileged
    boundary, a standard others can use);
  - misappropriation by power-seekers, and the defence the whole-field
    standard licenses.
- *Formal:* A.7.8–A.7.9.

### 11. The central thesis
- **Question.** What exactly is claimed?
- **Answer.** What we recognize as good corresponds to widening of the whole
  field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to their narrowing.
  - Why expect it: persistence and uncertainty together turn the link into
    breadth.
  - What it is not: a definition of good, or a rule to maximize entropy.
  - Mechanism words (organization, cooperation, domination) carry no sign.
- **Builds.**
  - narrowing, widening and foreclosure;
  - the largest narrowing;
  - the proof procedure: one class of cases at a time, each with positive and
    negative cases, matched controls, compression cases and resolution tests,
    and no hidden value terms;
  - what failure would look like.
- *Formal:* A.9.2–A.9.4; A.15.

## Part IV — Earning the thesis I: organization

The first test classes. Every result carries its model and its scope.

### 12. How the thesis is tested
- **Question.** What can small exact models show, and what can they not show?
- **Answer.** There are two layers:
  - the oracle layer, which discovers what lushness ranks;
  - the bounded-agent layer, which discovers what an embedded agent can know.

  Every experiment is a declared contrast: context, options, what is held
  fixed, resolution and horizon. It reports the profile, the narrowing and the
  foreclosure. Diagnostics are not evidence about the theory.
- **Builds.**
  - the evidence rubric;
  - matched comparisons;
  - the gas baseline;
  - reproducibility;
  - the single-model ideal.

### 13. Organization against disorder
- **Prediction 1.** Organized systems reach, within the time available,
  futures that disorder never reaches.
- **Builds.**
  - counterfactual access;
  - locked gradients;
  - gas and same-law comparisons;
  - capability, not proportion;
  - the uncatalysed control that was a more permissive law.
- *Status:* M, E.

### 14. Information pays through action
- **Prediction 2.** Knowing more widens the future only when the knowledge is
  used to act.
- **Builds.**
  - a register adds only its unpredicted switching;
  - "If your internal state cannot influence the world, might as well dream";
  - informed action;
  - the repayment horizon.
- *Status:* T, M.

### 15. Persistence and compounding
- **Question.** Does structure that lasts and builds on itself beat the thermal
  background?
- **Answer.** Our own existence shows that it can. The models show how:
  retention, funding, and rate growing with capacity. The literature shows it
  across nature.
- **Builds.** Uptime and payoff; the enabling loop; three-link compounding;
  error thresholds.
- *Status:* M, E, with three tiers [P1].
- *Primer:* dissipative and self-maintaining systems.

### 16. Activity is not breadth
- **Question.** If the measure counts everything, must it prefer noise and
  busyness to structure? The strongest objection, at full strength.
- **Answer.** Activity pays now and capacity pays later. The activity-matched
  comparator and the compression pairs decide it. Results are given if they
  have landed; otherwise the objection is stated as open.
- *Status:* M, open.

## Part V — Earning the thesis II: loss

### 17. Harm as narrowing
- **Question.** What does harm look like in the structure?
- **Answer.** Narrowing relative to what else could have been done, never a
  level below zero.
  - A harm that lowers the rate eventually outweighs one with a finite cost.
  - Irreversibility makes an early end most costly.
  - Material injury and narrowing are different things, and the
    funded-extraction case tests the gap between them.
- **Builds.**
  - offset and rate harms;
  - recovery;
  - foreclosure beside narrowing;
  - turnover across scales.
- *Formal:* A.9, A.4.9.

### 18. Protection: channels, not stock
- **Answer.** Keep channels open; do not maximize preservation. Dependence
  produces maintenance. The structure of a harm sets policy, not its raw
  hazard.
- **Builds.** Criticality as a statement about being, not ethics;
  precautionary protection; protection without a valuing premise.
- *Status:* M, T.

### 19. Suffering
- **The test class.** A world of obstructed suffering is less lush than the same
  world, with the same obstruction and the same valuer, without the suffering.
- **Builds.**
  - the measure detects valence only through its effect on the future;
  - the factory-farming objection as the named adversarial target;
  - register controls.
- *Status:* M, C.

## Part VI — Earning the thesis III: many valuers

### 20. Cooperation and joint access
- **Answer.** Cooperation is joint access. Concentration of control costs
  breadth only through what it does to the continuation.
- **Builds.** Coupling at fixed marginals; division and concentration; control
  invariance; the commons.
- *Formal:* A.10, A.2.5.

### 21. Constraint and domination
- **Prediction 3.** Constraint that blocks others' access narrows the whole,
  even when it widens the futures of whoever imposes it.
- **Builds.**
  - domination as a setup, not a verdict;
  - where constraint turns from good to bad, located by a sweep;
  - the self–whole conflict measured;
  - the boundary in time.
- *Status:* M.

### 22. Justice, forgiveness and virtue
- **Answer.** Each is a dynamic of the continuation structure: coordination,
  repair and durable policy. Protection among the governed is the core of a
  rule of law.

### 23. Persons, populations and minds
- **Builds.**

  - how sharply a frame is individuated;

- *Status:* M, C.

## Part VII — Acting without knowing

### 24. Choosing inside the structure
- **Answer.** A bounded agent decides step by step. The decision recursion is
  consistent because the measure composes. It acts on finite horizons, with
  bounds on what it has not seen.
- *Formal:* A.13, A.12.

### 25. One uncertainty, many places
- **Answer.** Precaution, option value, deference, keeping mistakes correctable,
  and a margin for model error are one structure:
  - uncertainty;
  - information that may arrive;
  - what stays actionable;
  - how reversible a step is.

  Irreversibility is not a further value.
- **Builds.** The robustness floor in practice; anti-fanaticism; correction
  channels priced by regeneration.
- *Primer:* value of information.
- *Formal:* A.9.5–A.9.6, A.13.3.

### 26. Controllers that reshape the field
- **Builds.** What an embedded controller is physically; state-dependent
  optima; maximum occupancy.
- *Status:* M.

### 27. Alignment
- **Answer.** What the framework offers artificial agents:
  - an evaluator;
  - a decision structure;
  - conditions for correction;
  - a test bed.

  Its risks: misappropriation by self-directed optimizers, risk neutrality,
  and the compression boundary.
- *Primer:* corrigibility.
