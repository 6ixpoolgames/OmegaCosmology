MULTI-AGENT COMPATIBILITY AND CONFLICT
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

Once multiple valuers or candidate valuers share a physical environment, their continuations can interact.

The project studies this interaction through compatibility, robust securability, recovery, joint constraints, policy diversity, capture, and power asymmetry.

The central structural question is:

    Which sets of valuer-bearing continuations can be jointly realized,
    and under what policies and contingencies can they remain jointly viable?

Compatibility is not assumed to imply moral obligation. It is the physical and decision-theoretic structure on which later normative reasoning operates.


1. JOINT CONTINUATION

Let F be a family of continuation requirements associated with several valuers.

A family may include conditions such as:

- survival;
- access to critical resources;
- preservation of identity;
- retained correction;
- recoverability;
- maintenance of agency;
- avoidance of specified harms.

The first question is simple:

    Is there at least one physically realizable continuation satisfying all requirements?

If yes, the family is jointly realizable.


2. MAY COMPATIBILITY

The project calls simple joint realizability May compatibility.

Let Real(F) denote the set of realizations satisfying family F.

Then:

    May(F)
        iff
    Real(F) != empty.

May asks:

    Can these continuation requirements coexist in at least one realization?

It is an existential notion.

One witness is enough.


3. ROBUST COMPATIBILITY

A stronger notion asks whether one policy can secure the family across a declared set of contingencies E.

Let pi be a policy and outcome(pi,e) its result in environment e.

Then:

    Robust_E(F)
        iff
    exists pi
        such that
    for all e in E,
        outcome(pi,e) in Real(F).

Robust asks:

    Is there a single policy that keeps the family jointly realized
    across every contingency under consideration?

This is much stronger than May.


4. ROBUST IMPLIES MAY

If one policy works across every environment in E, then at least one joint realization exists.

Therefore:

    Robust_E(F) -> May(F).

The converse does not hold.

A family can be jointly realizable but fragile to uncertainty, incomplete information, or policy conflict.


5. PAIRWISE COMPATIBILITY IS NOT ENOUGH

Suppose A, B, and C are three requirements.

It can happen that:

    May({A,B})
    May({A,C})
    May({B,C})

all hold,

while

    not May({A,B,C}).

This is the hollow-triangle phenomenon.

Every pair can coexist while the triple cannot.

Thus compatibility is non-flag:

    pairwise compatibility does not determine higher-order compatibility.

Any serious multi-valuer analysis must therefore retain genuinely joint structure.


6. PAIRWISE ROBUST COMPATIBILITY CAN ALSO FAIL JOINTLY

The same problem appears at the robust level.

Every pair may admit a policy that secures it across uncertainty, while no policy secures all three simultaneously.

Therefore:

    pairwise Robust
        does not imply
    joint Robust.

This is important because local compromise cannot always be assembled into one global solution.


7. JOINT REALIZABILITY DOES NOT IMPLY ROBUST SECURABILITY

A set of valuers may have a continuation in which all survive.

Yet uncertainty can make that continuation impossible to guarantee.

Example:

- in a wet environment, policy pi_1 works;
- in a dry environment, policy pi_2 works;
- the decision-maker cannot distinguish wet from dry before acting;
- pi_1 and pi_2 require opposite actions.

Then:

    May(F)

holds, but

    not Robust_E(F).

This separates physical coexistence from robust governance.


8. POLICY ATLASES

Sometimes every context admits a successful policy, but no single policy works in all contexts.

Then the system requires a set of local policies.

Let

    S_pi =
        { e : pi secures F in environment e }.

A policy atlas is a collection of policies whose successful regions cover E.

The covering number is

    kappa_F(E)
        =
    inf {
        |P| :
        E subset union_{pi in P} S_pi
    }.

Interpretation:

    kappa = 1
        one policy robustly secures the family.

    1 < kappa < infinity
        complementary policies are required.

    kappa = infinity
        the declared policy class cannot cover all contingencies.

This is a structural measure of policy diversity.


9. POLICY DIVERSITY IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY POLITICAL PLURALITY

A covering number greater than one means multiple distinct policies are functionally required under the declared interface.

It does not by itself prove:

- multiple authorities;
- multiple moral subjects;
- democracy;
- decentralized governance;
- independent consciousnesses.

One sufficiently expressive controller might implement all local policies conditionally.

The important structural fact is complementary policy behavior.


10. ATTENUATION

A controller can sometimes make robust control easier by simplifying the environment.

For example, it might:

- suppress variability;
- eliminate competitors;
- standardize behavior;
- remove uncertain states.

This can improve robustness under a narrow register while destroying valuable continuation outside that register.

This is the attenuation problem.

A compatibility calculation is only as good as the continuation requirements and affected valuers it represents.


11. INTEGRATION AND CAPTURE

Integration can be generative.

Several lower-level processes may coordinate into a higher-level system with new capabilities.

Capture is different.

Capture occurs when previously independent sources of continuation become entirely mediated by one controlling organization.

Signs include:

- loss of independent policy channels;
- inability to re-form independently;
- all external effects routed through one controller;
- disappearance of separate correction channels.

Capture is a structural relation, not automatically a moral verdict.


12. TERMINAL SOLITUDE

A limiting case is terminal solitude.

Informally:

    only one independently situated source of valuation remains,
    and no independent source can regenerate within the scope.

One formal sketch is:

    Independent_h(p) = empty

and

    Regen_h(p) = empty.

A system can exhibit terminal solitude even if many nominal individuals or internal simulations remain.

The issue is independent continuation and regenerability, not headcount.


13. NON-CAPTURE

A multi-valuer continuation is non-captured when distinct valuers retain enough independent organization to remain genuine loci of continuation-sensitive stakes rather than mere internal subroutines of one controller.

Possible indicators include:

- independent correction;
- independent reconstruction;
- separate policy formation;
- causal access to shared structure;
- ability to resist or exit;
- possibility of regeneration.

The exact criterion remains an active research problem.


14. RECOVERY

Conflict should be analyzed not only by immediate loss but by recovery.

For valuer i, define a registered loss state and ask whether allowed repair can restore the relevant fact within horizon H.

Two actions with similar immediate damage can differ greatly if:

- one preserves repair routes;
- one destroys them;
- one leaves information needed for restoration;
- one makes loss irreversible.

Recovery therefore enters compatibility as a joint property.


15. JOINT RECOVERY

Individual recovery does not guarantee joint recovery.

Several valuers can each possess a repair path in isolation while their repair requirements conflict when combined.

Thus:

    individual recoverability
        does not imply
    joint recoverability.

This is another reason multi-agent structure cannot be reconstructed from marginal profiles alone.


16. SACRIFICE

Some systems admit actions that destroy one local continuation while preserving a larger integrated organization.

Examples include:

- immune response;
- surgical removal;
- resource triage;
- ecological intervention.

This prevents a simple rule that every local contraction is automatically wrong.

The relevant analysis asks:

- whose continuation is affected;
- whether the local process is a valuer;
- whether loss is recoverable;
- what larger continuation depends on the intervention;
- whether alternatives exist;
- how standing is assigned.

Sacrifice is therefore a joint structural and normative problem.


17. POWER ASYMMETRY

Compatibility results can become irrelevant if one controller has overwhelming power.

A dominant agent may:

- externalize costs;
- destroy opposition;
- manipulate records;
- alter the environment;
- prevent recovery;
- redefine the decision interface.

Power must therefore be represented explicitly.

A family may be nominally compatible while one member lacks any practical ability to secure its continuation.


18. CONTAINMENT

When one agent threatens the continuation of others, defensive containment becomes a structural possibility.

Containment can include:

- restricting actions;
- limiting access;
- isolating control channels;
- reducing capability;
- changing incentives.

The structural question is whether containment preserves threatened continuation.

The normative question is whether the affected parties have standing and whether the intervention is authorized and proportionate.

These are distinct.


19. AUTHORITY

Successful control does not create moral authority.

An optimizer may find a policy that preserves a declared compatibility set.

That does not answer:

- who chose the set;
- whose facts count;
- who can impose the policy;
- how dissent is represented;
- whether compensation is adequate;
- whether the controller is itself captured or biased.

This is the authority problem.

It is one of the major open bridges between formal decision machinery and ethics.


20. STANDING

Multi-agent analysis depends critically on which systems count as valuers and which aspects of their continuation have standing.

Standing cannot be inferred from:

- causal power;
- population size;
- complexity;
- policy success.

A weak valuer can have strong standing.

A powerful non-valuer can have none intrinsically while remaining extremely important instrumentally.


21. AGGREGATION

When valuers conflict, the project does not assume one universal scalar utility.

Several structures may remain:

- partial orders;
- incomparable claims;
- feasibility constraints;
- Pareto frontiers;
- lexicographic protections;
- rights-like exclusions;
- negotiated tradeoffs.

Aggregation is therefore a substantive normative problem rather than a mathematical cleanup step.


22. COMPENSATION

One possible response to loss is compensation.

Compensation requires more than adding unrelated benefit elsewhere.

A valid compensation claim may need to preserve or restore the relevant continuation fact for the affected valuer or supply a justified substitute.

This raises hard questions about:

- fungibility;
- identity;
- substitutability;
- irreversible loss;
- cross-valuer trade.

The framework is intentionally skeptical of compensation arguments that rely on an overly coarse description.


23. CORRECTION CHANNELS

A robust joint system should preserve the ability to discover and correct mistakes.

This includes:

- measurement;
- dissent;
- model revision;
- institutional review;
- scientific testing;
- appeal;
- repair.

Destroying correction channels can make a policy appear stable while making future error unrecoverable.

This links compatibility to humility and open-ended adaptation.


24. COMPATIBILITY AND LUSHNESS

A large consequence space can still be dominated by one controller.

Lushness therefore becomes especially interesting in valuer-bearing continuation when it includes:

- nonredundant differentiation;
- compatibility;
- generativity;
- recovery;
- non-capture;
- room for new valuers and new distinctions.

This is not identical to maximizing the number of independent agents.

Integration can be generative.

The question is what kinds of organization sustain rich value-bearing continuation.


25. CURRENT FINITE RESULTS

The project has several robust structural results in finite models:

- adding candidate requirements can only shrink realization sets;
- enlarging the uncertainty environment can only shrink robust securing-policy sets;
- Robust implies May;
- pairwise May does not imply joint May;
- pairwise Robust does not imply joint Robust;
- joint realizability does not imply joint robust securability;
- every context can admit a successful policy while no single policy succeeds in every context;
- local recovery and joint recovery can diverge.

These results establish genuine constraints on any future multi-valuer formalism.


26. CURRENT RESEARCH TARGET

The multi-agent layer aims to characterize:

    valuer set
        -> joint continuation requirements
        -> compatibility complex
        -> robust policy structure
        -> recovery structure
        -> power and capture relations
        -> feasible joint region.

The next document adds the normative bridge that turns this descriptive structure into Gradient Ethics.
