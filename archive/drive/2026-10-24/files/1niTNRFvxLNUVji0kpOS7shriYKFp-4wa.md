## 1. What the framework rests on

The framework adds no axioms of its own. It argues from what value is, two
standard norms of reasoning, and physics, toward one thesis.

| Kind | Content | Status |

| **Norm 1: instrumental consistency** | Whoever is committed to an end is committed to the conditions it depends on. It connects valuing to its enabling conditions; it does not by itself fix their scope or magnitude (step 14) | N |
| **Norm 2: no distinction without justification** | Physics, not the theorist, decides what counts as different and what counts as one. Used in the measure (only physically registrable distinctions count), in admission (no criterion beyond consequence), and in the subject (no exclusive moral boundary without a reason beyond the boundary's existence) | N |

Everything else in the chain is a definition, an argument from these, a
theorem, or a model result.

## 2. Physical premises (P)

Claims about the world, open to empirical challenge.

1. Physical development is oriented: occurrences are ordered before and after,
   partially where events are spacelike separated.
2. A native law fixes what can follow from a present, with objective
   probabilities where the physics supplies them.
3. Admissible presents can be specified (section 4, "Frame").
4. Consequences propagate only within causal reach.

Alpha offers an explanation of why such a structure exists (the Alpha
chapter). The argument below does not depend on it.

## 3. The foundational principle

**Value requires valuers.** (F)

- **It fixes where value can be:** relative to valuers. It does not say what is
  good.
- **Its content is consequence.** Consequence is meaningful only because it
  pertains to something that values; valuers are what consequence pertains to.
  A valuer is a physical participant in consequential continuation
  (section 4).
- **Consequence is not branching.** Consequence is the dependence of what
  follows on what is present. Branching is the existence of several
  continuations of one specified present under the law. Admission needs only
  consequence. Comparative value, a better or worse, needs alternatives, so it
  needs branching (steps 5, 6).
- **Why it is not an axiom.** It is argued: largely from what "value" means
  (value is always value to or for something), and, once admission is
  universal, it comes close to "value in a physical world has a physical
  bearer".
- **Compatible with its main rival.** A theory of intrinsic value still places
  value in a bearer that exists, and every physical bearer is a valuer.
- **The joint a critic will press:** that consequence is part of the principle
  and not a second commitment. The reply is the cancellation argument
  (step 4): whatever value realized states carry is common to every option
  from a present, so no choice can turn on it; only what follows can.

## 5. Object, magnitude, thesis

Three things, kept apart in every chapter:

1. **The object:** the physically structured continuation from a present
   (Omega, the futures field).
2. **The magnitude:** its effective multiplicity under declared conditioning
   and resolution (lushness).
3. **The thesis:** changes in that magnitude are changes in accessible value.

"Only lushness matters to the ethical ordering" is the thesis. It does not make
other structure irrelevant to explaining, predicting or testing that ordering.

## 7. Rules for an admissible comparison

**A verdict between options** is admissible only if:

1. **One present.** The options are physically available continuations of the
   same present. Different presents are not alternatives of one another.
2. **Continuation laws, not single histories.** What is compared is the law of
   continuation under each option, never two selected histories (each has
   perplexity 1 once fixed).
3. **One resolution.** Both are read at the same grain, cadence, horizon and
   conditioning (matched resolution).
4. **Implemented, not conditioned.** Options are acts carried out, never
   selections of an outcome that removes the alternatives.
5. **Disclosed horizon.** A finite horizon is a legitimate partial assessment.
   It is stated, and it is not presented as a verdict on consequences it
   omits.
6. **Invariance.** Equivalent representations of the same comparison,
   including equivalent admissible sections, give the same ranking. A
   genuinely later present changes the conditioning and need not keep the
   ranking. (Required; not yet proved: O.)

**Other comparisons are legitimate science, labelled for what they show.**
Contrasts between laws (mechanism contrasts) and between matched preparations
test physical claims. They are not alternative actions from one present, and
their evidential role is stated with them.

Without these rules, a verdict could be manufactured by changing what is
compared.

## 8. The experiments

**Four questions, in the argument's order:**
1. **Access:** can organization open consequential development unavailable, or
   much less accessible, to its matched alternatives?
2. **Persistence and recursion:** can those effects sustain or rebuild their
   enabling conditions?
3. **Scope:** how do the self-directed and whole-field rankings separate when
   consequences are intertwined?
4. **Sufficiency:** can equally or more lush continuations expose a distinction
   the ethical account needs?

**Every report states:** the physical mechanism instantiated; what lushness
ranked; which parameter relationship was shown; the kind of comparison (verdict
between options, mechanism contrast, or matched preparations); and what was
challenged — the theory, a design guess, or the model's adequacy. A missed
guess is a bad guess, not a failed principle; a well-built adverse result is
evidence to explain.

## 9. Difficult consequences, stated where first needed

Short, direct statements, placed once and then referenced:

- **Determinism:** one complete present under one deterministic law supplies
  one continuation, not competing futures of that present.
- **Realized value:** already realized, with consequences that can remain
  active.
- **Negative comparisons:** contraction supplies a comparative sign; the
  argument does not first need to rule on negative experience.
- **Noise:** physical variation counts. Organized structure must earn its
  advantage through its consequences.
- **Compression:** equal magnitude can conceal different structures. Whether
  those differences need different ethical rankings is a central test.

## 10. Kept separate from the foundation

- **Alpha:** an explanation of the physical premises. The endpoint entropy

- **Identity:** a question about trajectories (persons and minds).
- **Agency and the second transition:** controllers.
- **Valence and consciousness:** lushness distinguishes them only through
  differences they make to the assessed continuation law (suffering). This is
  not a general claim about how consciousness can be detected.
