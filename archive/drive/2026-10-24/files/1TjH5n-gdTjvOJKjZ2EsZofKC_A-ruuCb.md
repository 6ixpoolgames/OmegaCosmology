**Reader.** A strong undergraduate from a strong school, or an expert in one of

philosophy, biology or AI. The reader has no prior knowledge of the program,
the measure or any term in it. Every term is defined at first use. Each field's
background is explained to the level the argument needs, in a box the
specialist can skip.

**The object.** The manuscript studies one physical object, Omega: the
completed structure of everything that can follow from every present. Every
concept is derived from it, and each is placed in it, in an "In Omega" box, as
it arrives. The ethics, and its relevance to alignment, fall out of the study
of that object. They are not its starting point.

**Cosmic nihilism** is answered head-on: even granting the nihilist's premises,
the structure is not flat. The main treatment is in chapter 30, with brief
mentions where the point is topical (chapters 1, 6, 9 and 15, and Overview
section 8).

## The spine

The whole manuscript in eleven steps. Each step is one stretch of the book.

1. **The problem.** Every standard for comparing outcomes begins by deciding
   who counts, and that decision is never settled. Artificial agents make the
   problem urgent. *(Part I)*
2. **The proposal.** Begin with physics instead: what can follow from a
   present, and how much of it there is. *(Chapters 3–4)*
3. **The measure is not a choice.** Physics fixes what counts as different, and
   four requirements fix how to count. The count is perplexity, and we call it
   lushness. *(Chapters 4 and 7)*
4. **Time.** Compare whole futures, not snapshots. Rate, head start,
   persistence and irreversibility all come from one profile.
   *(Chapters 5–6)*
5. **Parts and wholes.** The same measure applies to a part of the world or to
   the whole. The two differ by exactly one term. *(Chapter 8)*
6. **Value requires valuers.** A valuer is anything with a frame of
   its own, and the whole world is the ultimate frame. *(Chapter 9)*
7. **The thesis.** What we recognize as good corresponds to widening of the
   whole field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to their narrowing.
   This is a claim to be earned, not a definition. *(Chapters 10–11)*
8. **Earning it, one class of cases at a time:** organization, information,
   persistence, activity, harm, protection, suffering, cooperation,
   domination, justice, persons. *(Parts IV–VI)*
9. **Acting on it without knowing everything.** Precaution, option value,
   deference and correction are one uncertainty applied in different places.
   Alignment follows. *(Part VII)*
10. **Where it sits.** Alpha, quantum frames, and what the account means.
    *(Part VIII)*
11. **What could overturn it.** The evidence by status, the hardest tests, and
    the program ahead. *(Part IX)*

## Part I — The problem

The reader learns why comparing outcomes is stuck, and what a way out would
have to provide.

### 1. Who counts?
- **Question.** Every ethics compares outcomes. Why has no standard for doing
  so ever been settled?
- **Answer.** Every standard settles who and what counts before the inquiry
  begins, so every dispute about that starting point is a dispute about the
  whole standard. More argument cannot end it.
- **Builds.** A survey of the standards (welfare, preference, rights, virtue,
  contract, capabilities, biocentric ethics) and the starting points each
  depends on; the is–ought problem stated fairly.
- **Then.** Why artificial agents make the problem urgent: they need a standard
  that does not depend on whose preferences trained them, favours no group,
  and stays defined as their understanding grows.
- **Hands on.** Three requirements for a way out:
  - begin with no decision about who counts;
  - stay well defined;
  - be testable.
- *Primers:* moral philosophy; AI objectives.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* one response to the stalemate is to deny there is anything to settle. Chapter 30 answers it.

### 2. Possibility as a standard
- **Question.** Others have tried to ground value in freedom, options or
  possibility. Why have they stalled?
- **Answer.** They stall at four points:
  - **grain:** what counts as a different option;
  - **weighting:** how likely options count;
  - **subject:** whose options;
  - **busyness:** whether noise counts as possibility.

  Any proposal must resolve all four.
- **Builds.**
  - Freedom of choice: Pattanaik and Xu.
  - Capabilities.
  - Option value: Arrow, Fisher and Henry.
  - Empowerment, causal entropic forces and maximum occupancy.
  - Open-endedness.
- **Hands on.** The four points become the agenda of Part II.
- *Status:* E (literature).

## Part II — The object and its measure

Each chapter opens with the ethical problem its physical tool resolves. The
reader then meets the tool.

### 3. What follows from a present
- **Question.** If not welfare or preference, what is there to compare?
- **Answer.** Everything that can follow from a present, with its
  probabilities: Omega. States cannot rank options. Under the fundamental laws
  the world's total information does not change with what we do. The
  differences lie in what can follow, and in what each part of the world can
  reach.
- **Builds.**
  - Plain-English definitions: future, Omega, present, frame, access.
  - **Orientation:** asymmetry gives a future; a frame gives the standpoint
    from which it is defined.
  - What Omega is not: a probability over beliefs, or a utility.
- *Primer:* probability and histories.
- *Formal:* A.1.

### 4. Counting futures
- **Question.** How much possibility does a present hold? The grain problem and
  the weighting problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** Physics decides what counts as the same, and probability decides
  how much there is. Four requirements on any honest count leave one measure,
  the perplexity of the futures: lushness.
- **Builds.**
  - resolution;
  - the continuation structure;
  - lushness;
  - why similarity weighting is excluded;
  - counting everything, randomness included;
  - the compression boundary: what the measure cannot see.
- *Primers:* entropy and perplexity; lushness is not the entropy of heat.
- *Formal:* A.1.1, A.2.

### 5. Futures through time
- **Question.** Should a head start you can see now count for more than a lead
  that grows later?
- **Answer.** Compare the whole profile of the difference. Its slope is the
  rate, its level the offset, and its zero the crossover horizon. Over
  everything that follows, rate decides first and offset second.
- **Builds.**
  - the profile;
  - rate and offset;
  - the crossover horizon;
  - preparation, process and mechanism contrasts;
  - the offset theorem.
- *Primer:* Markov processes.
- *Formal:* A.3, A.3.9.

### 6. How long things last
- **Question.** Should what exists be preserved? Is a fast gain worth taking if
  it may not last?
- **Answer.** Persistence is worth exactly the rate it sustains beyond whatever
  would replace it.
  - A gain must repay its cost over its expected life (the robustness floor).
  - An early end costs least when the world can rebuild what was lost, and most
    when it never can.
  - Long shots are bounded by a finite world.
- **Builds.**
  - random horizons;
  - what persistence is worth;
  - the cost of an early end and regeneration;
  - the generative window;
  - gambles and finite stakes.
- *Primer:* expected value.
- *Formal:* A.4, A.5.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* transience does not zero worth. What a finite regime adds is real.

### 7. Resolution: what counts as different
- **Question.** Rankings can flip with how finely or how often we look. Can any
  verdict be trusted?
- **Answer.** Declare the resolution. Let the physics set the admissible range.
  Accept only verdicts that keep their sign across it. A sign that flips means
  the comparison is unresolved, not tied.
- **Builds.**
  - admissible resolutions;
  - the temporal expansion;
  - the crossover resolution;
  - sign stability.
- *Formal:* A.6.

### 8. Parts and wholes
- **Question.** Whose futures? The subject problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** The measure applies to any part of the world or to the whole. A
  part never has more lushness than the whole. Self and whole differ by
  exactly one term: what an act does to everything else, given what it does to
  the part. Parts the act cannot affect drop out, and there is no ledger of
  subjects to keep.
- **Builds.**
  - projections and subjects;
  - bounds on a part's lushness;
  - intertwining;
  - the self–whole gap;
  - subjects can disagree;
  - unaffected parts cancel.
- *Primer:* mutual information.
- *Formal:* A.7.1–A.7.7, A.7.10.

## Part III — From physics to value

The turn from what there is to what matters.

### 9. Value requires valuers
- **Question.** Physics describes what can follow. Where does value enter?
- **Answer.** Value requires valuers: without something that values, nothing
  physical is good or bad. This is the step from the physical to the moral,
  not a moral rule.
  - A valuer is anything with a frame of its own, from which its futures can
    be projected.
  - Every physical system counts, gas included. The whole world is the
    ultimate frame.
- **Builds.**
  - valuer; the ultimate frame;
  - what makes a frame physically instantiated (the candidate, and the open
    problem);
  - persistence as the metavalue;
  - disvalue as narrowing;
  - the principle of practical reason;
  - why a hedonic definition would bring back negative totals.
- *Formal:* A.7.1; A.15.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* value is a relation within physics, not something projected onto it.

### 10. The comparison with no index
- **Question.** Self-interest and ethics: two objectives, or one?
- **Answer.** One measure, two comparisons. The indexed one, for the self, is
  what prudence tracks. The one with no index, for the whole, is what the
  thesis says ethical judgement tracks.
- **Builds.**
  - agent-neutral and agent-relative reasons;
  - why expect ethics to track the whole (intertwining, no privileged
    boundary, a standard others can use);
  - misappropriation by power-seekers, and the defence the whole-field
    standard licenses.
- *Formal:* A.7.8–A.7.9.

### 11. The central thesis
- **Question.** What exactly is claimed?
- **Answer.** What we recognize as good corresponds to widening of the whole
  field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to their narrowing.
  - Why expect it: persistence and uncertainty together turn value requires valuers into
    breadth.
  - What it is not: a definition of good, or a rule to maximize entropy.
  - Mechanism words (organization, cooperation, domination) carry no sign.
- **Builds.**
  - narrowing, widening and foreclosure;
  - the largest narrowing;
  - the proof procedure: one class of cases at a time, each with positive and
    negative cases, matched controls, compression cases and resolution tests,
    and no hidden value terms;
  - what failure would look like.
- *Formal:* A.9.2–A.9.4; A.15.

## Part IV — Earning the thesis I: organization

The first test classes. Every result carries its model and its scope.

### 12. How the thesis is tested
- **Question.** What can small exact models show, and what can they not show?
- **Answer.** There are two layers:
  - the oracle layer, which discovers what lushness ranks;
  - the bounded-agent layer, which discovers what an embedded agent can know.

  Every experiment is a declared contrast: context, options, what is held
  fixed, resolution and horizon. It reports the profile, the narrowing and the
  foreclosure. Diagnostics are not evidence about the theory.
- **Builds.**
  - the evidence rubric;
  - matched comparisons;
  - the gas baseline;
  - reproducibility;
  - the single-model ideal.

### 13. Organization against disorder
- **Prediction 1.** Organized systems reach, within the time available,
  futures that disorder never reaches.
- **Builds.**
  - counterfactual access;
  - locked gradients;
  - gas and same-law comparisons;
  - capability, not proportion;
  - the uncatalysed control that was a more permissive law.
- *Status:* M, E.

### 14. Information pays through action
- **Prediction 2.** Knowing more widens the future only when the knowledge is
  used to act.
- **Builds.**
  - a register adds only its unpredicted switching;
  - "If your internal state cannot influence the world, might as well dream";
  - informed action;
  - the repayment horizon.
- *Status:* T, M.

### 15. Persistence and compounding
- **Question.** Does structure that lasts and builds on itself beat the thermal
  background?
- **Answer.** Our own existence shows that it can. The models show how:
  retention, funding, and rate growing with capacity. The literature shows it
  across nature.
- **Builds.** Uptime and payoff; the enabling loop; three-link compounding;
  error thresholds.
- *Status:* M, E, with three tiers [P1].
- *Primer:* dissipative and self-maintaining systems.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* compounding beats the thermal background. The structure is not indifferent between organization and disorder.

### 16. Activity is not breadth
- **Question.** If the measure counts everything, must it prefer noise and
  busyness to structure? The strongest objection, at full strength.
- **Answer.** Activity pays now and capacity pays later. The activity-matched
  comparator and the compression pairs decide it. Results are given if they
  have landed; otherwise the objection is stated as open.
- *Status:* M, open.

## Part V — Earning the thesis II: loss

### 17. Harm as narrowing
- **Question.** What does harm look like in the structure?
- **Answer.** Narrowing relative to what else could have been done, never a
  level below zero.
  - A harm that lowers the rate eventually outweighs one with a finite cost.
  - Irreversibility makes an early end most costly.
  - Material injury and narrowing are different things, and the
    funded-extraction case tests the gap between them.
- **Builds.**
  - offset and rate harms;
  - recovery;
  - foreclosure beside narrowing;
  - turnover across scales.
- *Formal:* A.9, A.4.9.

### 18. Protection: channels, not stock
- **Answer.** Keep channels open; do not maximize preservation. Dependence
  produces maintenance. The structure of a harm sets policy, not its raw
  hazard.
- **Builds.** Criticality as a statement about being, not ethics;
  precautionary protection; protection without a valuing premise.
- *Status:* M, T.

### 19. Suffering
- **The test class.** A world of obstructed suffering is less lush than the same
  world, with the same obstruction and the same valuer, without the suffering.
- **Builds.**
  - the measure detects valence only through its effect on the future;
  - the factory-farming objection as the named adversarial target;
  - register controls.
- *Status:* M, C.

## Part VI — Earning the thesis III: many valuers

### 20. Cooperation and joint access
- **Answer.** Cooperation is joint access. Concentration of control costs
  breadth only through what it does to the continuation.
- **Builds.** Coupling at fixed marginals; division and concentration; control
  invariance; the commons.
- *Formal:* A.10, A.2.5.

### 21. Constraint and domination
- **Prediction 3.** Constraint that blocks others' access narrows the whole,
  even when it widens the futures of whoever imposes it.
- **Builds.**
  - domination as a setup, not a verdict;
  - where constraint turns from good to bad, located by a sweep;
  - the self–whole conflict measured;
  - the boundary in time.
- *Status:* M.

### 22. Justice, forgiveness and virtue
- **Answer.** Each is a dynamic of the continuation structure: coordination,
  repair and durable policy. Protection among the governed is the core of a

### 23. Persons, populations and minds
- **Builds.**

  - plural ecology against monocultures and monsters;
  - lushness's version of the repugnant conclusion;
  - inner richness adds breadth, which says nothing about experience;
  - consciousness left open.
- *Status:* M, C.

## Part VII — Acting without knowing

### 24. Choosing inside the structure
- **Answer.** A bounded agent decides step by step. The decision recursion is
  consistent because the measure composes. It acts on finite horizons, with
  bounds on what it has not seen.
- *Formal:* A.13, A.12.

### 25. One uncertainty, many places
- **Answer.** Precaution, option value, deference, keeping mistakes correctable,
  and a margin for model error are one structure:
  - uncertainty;
  - information that may arrive;
  - what stays actionable;
  - how reversible a step is.

  Irreversibility is not a further value.
- **Builds.** The robustness floor in practice; anti-fanaticism; correction
  channels priced by regeneration.
- *Primer:* value of information.
- *Formal:* A.9.5–A.9.6, A.13.3.

### 26. Controllers that reshape the field
- **Builds.** What an embedded controller is physically; state-dependent
  optima; maximum occupancy.
- *Status:* M.

### 27. Alignment
- **Answer.** What the framework offers artificial agents:
  - an evaluator;
  - a decision structure;
  - conditions for correction;
  - a test bed.

  Its risks: misappropriation by self-directed optimizers, risk neutrality,
  and the compression boundary.
- *Primer:* corrigibility.

## Part VIII — The whole

The account's setting. The argument of Parts I–VII does not depend on it, and
Part VIII says so in its first paragraph. Alpha's role in the case (orientation,
and irreversibility) is already made in chapters 3, 6 and 17.

### 28. Alpha, Logos and Omega
- **Builds.**
  - distinction, relation and asymmetry;
  - the Logos;
  - the chain from asymmetry to frame, valuer, continuation and lushness;
  - irreversibility and directional asymmetry;
  - orientation darwinism.

### 29. Quantum frames
- **Answer.** A physical frame's recorded histories have an ordinary law.
  - Records made at spacelike separation do not depend on the slicing.
  - Comparisons are adjudicated from a frame above the branching.
  - Where no common refinement exists, the comparison is unresolved.
- **Builds.** Instruments and records; common refinement; extended Wigner's
  friend.
- *Primer:* decoherence and records.
- *Formal:* A.14.

### 30. Completion, choice and meaning
- **Cosmic nihilism, head-on.** Grant every premise: no value built in,
  nothing watching, everything ends.
  - Value is a relation within physics (chapter 9).
  - The structure is not indifferent: organization reaches futures that
    disorder never reaches (chapters 13 and 15).
  - Ending does not zero anything. A finite history still differs in total
    lushness (chapters 5–6).
  - The ultimate frame is a standpoint needing no observer.
  - The honest limit: the premises leave the ordering standing; whether it is
    value is the thesis.
- **Builds.**
  - what completion means;
  - choice in a completed whole;
  - the ultimate frame and the limit of description;
  - what the account means, and what would make it false.
