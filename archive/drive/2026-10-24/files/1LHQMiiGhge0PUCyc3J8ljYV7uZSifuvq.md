## Evidence rubric

A result can support a claim only if its model instantiates the object the claim
is about. A model may still support claims about the objects it does instantiate,
such as a theorem about the measure, a local mechanism or a single-agent result.

Theory-bearing evidence for a claim requires all of the following:

1. **Matching.** Conserved quantities and resources are matched across the
   comparison.
2. **Presence.** The relevant coupling or subject is physically present. Claims
   about appropriation, domination, whole-field ethics or coupled futures need
   those relations in the model.
3. **A stable objective.** The primary objective was not changed after the
   results were seen.
4. **Accounting.** Resource accounting is sufficient for the claim.
5. **No built-in answer.** No analytical feature predetermines the ranking being
   sought.

**Rubric rules added in r7:**

- **Matching includes internal degrees of freedom.** A comparator that removes
  a valuer's inner variation together with the feature under test compares
  against a simpler valuer.
  - **Matching prescription for "the same valuer without X":** keep the same
    state space, the same laws of activation and relaxation, and the same
    costs; remove only the coupling that constitutes X.
  - This is a declared methodological choice, not a derived identity criterion
    for valuers.
- **Post-hoc reclassification** of a result is allowed only with:
  - the original verdict preserved;
  - the reason stated;
  - the timing disclosed;
  - fresh-reviewer adjudication.

  A hypothesis formulated after results is labelled as new. It is never
  presented as a rescue of the old one.
- **Three-valued verdicts.** Every grain is scored as a resolved hit, a resolved
  non-hit, or unresolved. An unresolved grain blocks a survival claim as well as
  a kill.
- **Factorial reporting.** For two-factor mechanism contrasts, report the exact
  decomposition C + A + G (A17, item 5), together with all absolute scores.
  Report winners only together with where they sit relative to the boundary.
  Never add physical bills to entropy terms, and never replace the objective
  with an interaction term.

If any of the five criteria is missing, the result teaches us about the
diagnostic, not about the target claim. Classification follows the structure of the model. Make it
before interpreting a result wherever practicable, and apply it equally to
favourable and unfavourable results. Demoting a failure after the fact requires
naming which criterion it fails.

**Structural account.** Every important scalar result is reported together with
answers to nine questions:

1. What changed in Omega?
2. Which channel or dependency caused the change?
3. How robust, repairable or substitutable is that channel?
4. Does using the channel deplete it or maintain it?
5. Do descendants reconstruct or expand it?
6. What remains after it is lost, and what would be available without it?
7. What does finite-history lushness report?
8. What does the stationary rate report?
9. Are those results stable across the declared resolution corridor?

The structural account explains and predicts. It does not overrule lushness. A
disagreement between them is evidence about the compression boundary (A14) and
the central wager.

**Two experimental layers.**

- **Oracle layer.** The model is known exactly. It shows what lushness itself
  ranks.
- **Bounded-agent layer.** The controller has partial observation, uncertainty
  or a mismatched model. It shows what an embedded controller can know and
  implement.

Every row's evidence is tagged by layer. Precaution, correction and deference in
v4 derive from uncertainty, so oracle results neither confirm nor refute them.

A controller that receives perfect state information and re-plans from each new
present (receding horizon) still has the exact model. It belongs to the oracle
layer, as a check that a result does not depend on the restriction to stationary
controllers.

In bounded-agent experiments, report how much of the value of inspection or
consultation comes from its effect on later choices, and how much from the
randomness of the inspection event itself. Randomness counts, but the two must
not be confused. CORR-1 and S5 both showed probing chosen partly for its own
entropy.

### A4. Protecting channels, not stock

- **Current wording.** 5.3, "Persistence: the metavalue", and 5.4, strict and
  precautionary protection. The current text does not distinguish stock from
  capacity.
- **Disposition.** Revise.
- **Proposed wording.** Distinguish five things:
  - stock preservation;
  - functional availability;
  - recovery;
  - a continued channel of access;
  - generative capacity.

  Protection targets the capacity for further continuation. Maximal protection
  of particular structures can amount to lock-in. Margin is precautionary, graded
  by uncertainty and irreversibility.
- **Type and class.** Normative, with theory-bearing experiments as illustration.
- **Evidence (test bed, whole-world objective):**
  - **Between lock-in and burnout.** The breadth winner sits between the
    protective controls (idle, always service) and the active ones (always
    export, half/half) on every protection readout.
  - **Margin.** It keeps 4.8–9.2% less working apparatus than always servicing,
    across capture factors 1–12. Under this ruling that is a margin readout, not
    a hit.
  - **Recoverability.** Protection rises with recoverability: with shallow wells,
    the stationary usable fraction is 0.66 at depth 0 and 0.03 at depth −4.
- **Diagnostic, not cited.** The legacy surviving-hardware deep grid (closure
  2.106% against 1.080%). Its ranking was decided by the tail after closure.
- **Pressure.** Closure risk remains a diagnostic pressure point. See C9.
- **Pressure (reserve batch).** Doubling the hazard, and making shocks fully
  correlated, changed none of the 15 registered winners. In that fixture every
  harm was repairable (working apparatus became worn) or reversible (trapping at
  depth −1). A reserve therefore bought nothing that repair did not. The result
  is read narrowly: it does not test whether precaution responds to the shape of
  harm (C14).
- **Evidence (harm sweep, oracle layer).** The structure of harm changes policy.
  Irreversible loss of the powered repair hardware led the winner to service
  *less* than under fast repair, at T64 and T256 with h = 0.02. Hardware was
  mostly used rather than preserved: an 87–91% chance of losing it. The channel

  When one capability is lost irreversibly but substitutes keep the channel open,
  that particular structure is not protected.

### A17. Additions to the formal core (chapter 12)

- **Current wording.** 12.5 (Proposition 6, rate and offset for finite chains)
  and 12.7. Chapter 7's derived result on consent and coercion has no
  proposition in chapter 12.
- **Disposition.** Add three items:
  1. **Control invariance.** Two processes with the same sufficient present and
     the same weighted law have equal lushness, whoever implements them. This
     formalizes chapter 7's derived result and supports A9.
  2. **Reducible chains.** For a finite chain with transient states and several
     closed classes, form Π from the absorption weights and the stationary law of
     each class. Then the rate is r = Πg, the offset b solves
     (I − P + Π)b = g − r with Πb = 0, and ℓ_n = n r + b − Pⁿb. This is
     implemented and checked by residuals in the test bed, and it extends
     Proposition 6.
  3. **Continuous-time readout.** A continuous-time process is read at a declared
     cadence δ through P_δ = exp(δQ). The cadence is part of the declared
     resolution (B3). v4 currently works in discrete steps.
  4. **Inner registers (r7).** Let S be an internal register and Y every other
     coordinate, read in the order Y, then S:

     > **ℓ(Y, S) = Σₜ H(Yₜ | past) + Σₜ H(Sₜ | past, Yₜ)**

     *In words:* a register adds exactly the part of its switching that the past
     and the rest of the present do not predict.

     - Compared with the same valuer whose register is held constant, the change
       in the first sum can have either sign.
     - **Theorem (Codex).** For fixed marginal history distributions,
       H(Y, S) = H(Y) + H(S) − I(Y; S). Under those constraints, independence
       maximizes joint entropy.
     - The register controls match occupancy and switch count, not full
       marginals. Their ranking (independent above injury-linked) is evidence
       for the mechanism, not an instance of the theorem.
  5. **Factorial decomposition (r7, Codex).** For two binary mechanism factors,
     information (I or R) and actuation (X or 0):

     > **ℓ_IX − ℓ_R0 = C + A + G**

     *In words:* informed action's advantage over uncoupled variation splits
     exactly into information alone, action alone, and their interaction.

     - The terms: C = ℓ_I0 − ℓ_R0, A = ℓ_RX − ℓ_R0, and
       G = (ℓ_IX − ℓ_I0) − (ℓ_RX − ℓ_R0).
     - Informed action wins exactly when G > −(C + A).
     - All terms are complete continuation differences. Physical bills are
       already inside them.
  6. **Temporal two-term expansion (r7).** At fine cadence τ:

     > **ℓ_T(τ) ≈ N̄(T) log₂(1/τ) + F(T)**

     *In words:* breadth splits into an activity term that diverges as the clock
     gets finer and a finite term set by which events happen and how unevenly.

     For two processes, the ranking flips at the crossover resolution,
     log₂(1/τ×) = (F₂ − F₁)/(N̄₁ − N̄₂) (B3).
  7. **Horizon crossover (r7).** If ℓ(T) = hT + b + o(1) for each arm, the
     finite-history ranking settles after

     > **T_× = −Δb/Δh**

     *In words:* an arm that starts behind but has the higher rate overtakes
     after the deficit divided by the rate gain.

     This is a diagnostic estimate, not an exact crossing. Exact finite-history
     probes near the estimate are retained.
- **Type and class.** Theorems and definitions.

      > **Δℓ(T) ≈ −C + g·τ_up(T)**

      as long as it is actually working.

### B9. Impartiality: where it is derived and where it is a premise

- **Current wording.** 5.4: "Impartiality is not an extra axiom. It is simply the

  dependence extend its concern only to what it depends on, and to whatever it

  Intertwining narrows the gap but does not close it. Chapter 5 should state both
  cases and say which one the ethics addresses.

- **Dependencies.** B1.

### B12. Repeated labels are not outcomes

- **Current wording.** Implicit in 4.4's counting of possibilities.
- **Disposition.** Add, as a modelling rule.
- **Proposed wording.** Branching counts physically distinct outcomes, not

  entropy. A behavioural repertoire enters the measure only through outcomes
  that differ physically, such as in cost, destination, duration or a durable
  trace.
- **Type and class.** Measure; methodological.
- **Evidence.** The W1 implementation removed the escape-repertoire parameter k
  for this reason.

**C4 in detail.**

- **r7, compounding boundary.** All four registered predictions passed.
- **Retention.** The full loop retains apparatus near capacity: 3.94/4, 7.95/8, 15.89/16, 30.79/32. Each single-assistance ablation retains roughly a quarter of that or less, across all 24 laws.
- **Funding.** At the central point, 96.6% of funding comes from capture, and thermal births are negligible. Capture funding falls to 68% at capacity 32.
- **Rate gain.** The loop-minus-basal rate gain grows with capacity: +2.37, +3.44, +4.73 and +6.65 bits per unit time at cadence 0.25. The trend is therefore not a matter of state-space room, which both arms share.
- **Activity.** ΔN̄ > 0 and ΔF > 0, with ΔF about twice the ΔN̄ coefficient. The embedded jump-chain entropy rate is 1.05 bits per jump for the loop against 0.38 for basal, so each event is less predictable, not merely more frequent. At fixed jump counts the loop loses at 8 jumps (−1.76 bits) and wins at 32 and 128.
- **Grain.** No cadence-dependent signs in the grid.
- **Damage.** Isolated, with thermal reconstruction fixed. It lowers retention as predicted.
- **Criterion corrected.** Stationary funding fractions sum to 1, so running-cost coverage above 1 cannot be a requirement. The marker is which source funds the loop.
- **Pending.** A throughput-matched non-enabling control (C1b). The plausibility of the feedback's functional form and coefficients, for fresh reviewers. Unbounded material access. Composition with valuers.
- **Earlier status (r6, and r7 paid renewal).** Loop built; not self-sustaining in this regime. Catalytic feedback refunds only 31% of access costs. The rate increment over no feedback is +0.002 bits per unit time, positive at all three cadences but tiny. The "uncatalysed" control scores higher, but it was a more permissive law (feedback without apparatus at the catalysed rate) and is a diagnostic. Without inflow, every arm relaxes to the same thermal state. **Next:** rebuild with a single law, basal rate plus catalysed acceleration (k₀ + k_cat·a), preserving the equilibrium ratio. Then sweep the energetics consistently (yield, access energy, construction cost, k₀/k_cat) for a fertile region with a refund ratio above 1. Add a controller after that. **r7 (paid renewal):** construction assisted by existing apparatus, at capacity 2, raises the informed arm's rate by 0.032–0.071 bits per unit time. It does so through build-and-reverse-build turnover, not retained expansion: the system is in the expanded state 2–4% of the time, and the added entropy falls mainly on reservoir changes. The gross hardware return is 3.0–8.7, but coverage of all running costs is only 30–68%, with bath-supported assembly supplying the rest. Higher wear was confounded with faster thermal assembly. **Next:** separate resource-funded renewal from thermal reconstruction, with a pure damage axis. Locate where running-cost coverage crosses 1. Open capacity beyond 2, and test whether the rate grows with capacity through expansion that persists. Report the explosive region, if it exists, against physically plausible values.
