PHYSICAL LOCAL STRUCTURE AND GEOMETRY
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

The project currently treats the intrinsic local structure of physical possibility as the key bridge between universal quantum structure and situated Perspective. "Topology" is used as a compact placeholder for this idea, but the intended object may be richer than ordinary point-set topology. The target is the physically generated organization that determines neighborhood, continuity, accessibility, composition, interference, separation, and the propagation of distinctions.

The central research aim is to identify this structure from physics itself and then require perspectival and consequential constructions to respect it.


1. THE ROLE OF LOCAL STRUCTURE

A universal quantum state or process contains enormously more structure than a bounded system can use directly. To understand a situated physical system, it is not enough to know which formal states exist. One must also know how those states or processes are physically related.

Relevant relations include:

- continuity and limiting behavior;
- causal accessibility;
- compositional adjacency;
- dynamical reachability;
- interference and coherence;
- environmental coupling;
- local observability;
- amplification and record formation;
- distinguishability under physically realizable interaction;
- symmetry and equivalence.

These relations define the shape of possibility. A physical difference does not acquire its significance merely because two points have different labels. It matters how the difference sits within the physical structure: whether it can propagate, whether it changes later continuation, whether it can be erased, whether it can interfere, whether it opens or closes a route through the process, and whether another description represents the same physical situation.

The current program therefore seeks a physically intrinsic local geometry before attempting to measure effective possibility or Lushness.


2. TOPOLOGY AS A SCHEMATIC ABSTRACTION

A simple mathematical abstraction is a pair

    (Omega, tau)

where Omega is a space of physical possibilities and tau is a topology encoding neighborhoods and continuity.

A Perspective P occupying part of Omega could inherit a subspace or local topology

    (P, tau_P).

A coarse representation

    q : (P, tau_P) -> (Y, tau_Y)

would then be required to respect the physical structure, for example through continuity, quotient structure, or another appropriate morphism.

This picture is useful because it makes one principle precise:

    not every equivalence relation or coarse-graining is physically legitimate.

A legitimate coarse description should arise from, or at least preserve, the physically realized organization of possibility.

However, ordinary topology may not be sufficient. Quantum physics carries structures that are metric, algebraic, symplectic, causal, operational, and compositional as well as topological. The current use of topology should therefore be understood as a family resemblance: the theory needs a notion of physically generated locality and continuity, not necessarily one specific topological formalism.


3. CANDIDATE PHYSICAL STRUCTURES

Several mature mathematical structures may contribute to the final object.

3.1 Hilbert and projective geometry

Pure quantum states are rays in Hilbert space rather than ordinary vectors with physically meaningful global phase. Projective Hilbert space carries natural geometric structures, including the Fubini-Study metric in finite-dimensional settings. Mixed states admit related geometries such as Bures distance and quantum Fisher information.

These structures encode notions of proximity and distinguishability intrinsic to quantum state space. They are natural candidates for some aspects of local geometry, particularly when the relevant object is a state or family of states.

3.2 Operator-algebraic structure

Quantum observables form noncommutative algebras. In quantum field theory, local algebras associated with spacetime regions can encode locality more fundamentally than a global tensor factorization. Operator norms, weak and strong operator topologies, states on algebras, commutation relations, and inclusion maps may therefore be part of the correct local structure.

This is especially important because subsystem decomposition in interacting quantum field theory can be subtle. A final Perspective theory should not assume a naive global tensor-product factorization where the physics does not supply one.

3.3 Operational metric structure

States, channels, and multi-time processes carry operationally meaningful discrimination metrics. Examples include trace distance for states, diamond norm for channels, and strategy/process discrimination norms for higher-order quantum objects.

These metrics tell us how strongly two physical conditions can differ under allowable interaction. They may characterize one part of the geometry of consequence, although the current physical program does not treat an analyst-chosen set of tests as ontologically primary.

3.4 Causal structure

Relativistic physics supplies a causal partial order or related spacetime structure. Which events can influence which others, and which regions are spacelike separated, constrain any physically meaningful notion of Perspective and continuation.

A useful local geometry may therefore have an explicitly causal component rather than being purely metric.

3.5 Boundary and process composition

General-boundary and process-based approaches treat regions and interfaces compositionally. A physical boundary can carry the data needed to connect one region to another without assigning the whole universe to one preferred time slice.

For Perspective, this may be more natural than an instantaneous subsystem state. The relevant local object may be a boundary state, process slice, local algebra, or record-conditioned process interface.

3.6 Sheaf-like and local-to-global structure

In theories where information is defined locally and must be consistently glued across overlaps, sheaf-like or presheaf structures may be useful. Contextuality itself is naturally expressed in such language. The project does not currently adopt this formalism, but local-to-global consistency is likely to be important if Perspective is built from overlapping physical access regions rather than sharply isolated objects.


4. PHYSICS DETERMINES THE STRUCTURE

The central conceptual principle is:

    physical structure determines the neighborhood and continuity relations;
    measurements and observables reveal or characterize them.

This reverses the temptation to define consequence by first selecting a favorable set of questions. A test can show that two conditions are distinguishable. It should not be treated as the metaphysical act that makes those conditions physically distinct.

Where a sufficiently rich family of observables F separates points or states, their response maps can help recover a topology. Mathematically, one can form the initial topology

    tau_F = the coarsest topology making every f in F continuous.

This provides one rigorous connection between observables and topology. But the intended direction is that F is physically determined: it arises from the actual observables, interactions, and composition laws of the theory.

If fundamental physics supplies a richer object than a topology, observables should likewise be used to reconstruct or probe that richer structure rather than replacing it.


5. REPRESENTATION INVARIANCE

A central criterion is invariance under changes that alter description but not physics.

Examples include:

- coordinate changes;
- basis changes;
- global phase;
- unitary equivalence where appropriate;
- gauge transformations;
- relabeling of physically identical degrees of freedom;
- different mathematical presentations of the same channel or process;
- compatible changes of foliation in a relativistic formulation;
- equivalent coarse representations that preserve the same physical structure.

A consequence geometry that changes arbitrarily under such transformations is not yet physical.

This is one reason a simple Euclidean volume in a hand-chosen feature space is inadequate. Coordinate volume is not invariant under general reparameterization. If a later notion of possibility volume is to be physically meaningful, the underlying geometry must be supplied or canonically constrained by physics.

The same principle applies to coarse-graining. If two coarse descriptions preserve the same physical local and continuation structure, they should not produce fundamentally different claims about consequence merely because their coordinates differ.


6. LOCALITY, ACCESSIBILITY, AND PERSPECTIVE

Perspective is expected to be a local structure within the universal physical geometry.

"Local" here is not restricted to spatial locality. It can include:

- causal locality;
- algebraic locality;
- access through a boundary or interface;
- entanglement-conditioned support;
- record locality;
- control locality;
- computationally available state variables.

A Perspective may therefore be represented by a region P together with the physical structure inherited from Omega. Its accessible observables, records, and continuations are not arbitrary subsets; they should follow from the couplings and locality of the physical system.

This provides the basis for the later distinction between universal possibility and situated possibility. The universal structure may contain distinctions that are physically real but locally inaccessible. A Perspective inherits only the portion of structure that can interact with, condition, or continue through its physical boundary.


7. CONTINUITY AND ORIENTED CONTINUATION

The project is ultimately interested not just in static neighborhoods but in oriented continuation.

A physical local geometry should support questions such as:

- From this situated condition, what continuations are possible?
- Which changes are infinitesimal or nearby in the relevant structure?
- Which continuations are dynamically connected?
- Which distinctions persist under evolution?
- Which alternatives can interfere or recombine?
- Which routes are blocked by causal or dynamical constraints?
- Which transitions alter the set or structure of later possibilities?

This suggests that the final object may combine topology with dynamics or category-like composition. One might need a space of states plus allowed morphisms, a process category, a causal graph with quantum weights, a path groupoid, a bundle of local state spaces over spacetime, or another construction that captures both local structure and oriented transformation.

The project therefore treats "topology" as shorthand for local organization, while the eventual mathematics may be closer to a structured dynamical or process geometry.


8. COHERENCE AS PART OF LOCAL STRUCTURE

Quantum coherence provides a specifically nonclassical relation among alternatives.

Two alternatives are coherent when relative phase remains capable of influencing some physically allowed continuation. This is stronger than merely being close in a conventional metric. Coherence can connect histories or state components that are distinct in a classical representation but not independently probabilistic.

The decoherence functional

    D(alpha,beta)

is one way of representing such relations among histories. If the cross term is non-negligible, the alternatives cannot simply be treated as independent classical branches.

The local geometry of possibility must therefore retain enough structure to distinguish:

- proximity;
- interference capability;
- decohered separation;
- causal separation;
- operational indistinguishability;
- exact identity.

These are different relations and may require different mathematical components.


9. RECORD FORMATION AS GEOMETRIC STABILIZATION

Stable records are important because they turn transient quantum correlations into persistent structures that constrain later continuation.

In geometric language, record formation may be understood as the emergence of robust regions or sectors whose internal distinctions remain stable under environmental interaction while interference with alternative sectors becomes strongly suppressed.

Such record-bearing sectors can support:

- a stable effective past;
- local object persistence;
- classical-looking trajectory structure;
- repeated access to the same coarse facts;
- conditional future prediction;
- coordination among multiple local systems.

This gives the local-geometry program a direct empirical anchor: any viable formulation of Perspective must explain why macroscopic records are stable and why they support effective classical continuity despite the underlying quantum structure.


10. COARSE DESCRIPTIONS

Coarse description is unavoidable for bounded physical systems, but the project distinguishes physical coarse-graining from arbitrary semantic abstraction.

A physically grounded coarse map should preserve structures relevant to the declared level of description. Depending on context, this may mean preserving:

- transition probabilities;
- causal accessibility;
- process response;
- record structure;
- symmetries;
- conservation laws;
- conditional future statistics;
- local algebraic relations;
- topological adjacency.

Exact preservation may be expressed through quotient constructions or lumpability. Approximate preservation may require error bounds or distortion metrics.

The project already has formal examples showing that a coarse-graining can preserve one property while destroying another. Exact dynamical lumpability, for example, can erase directional evidence. This motivates a general rule: no coarse representation should be treated as globally faithful merely because it preserves one selected observable or transition structure.


11. RELATIVITY AND THE ABSENCE OF A PREFERRED FOLIATION

A universal "present slice" is not assumed. Any physical local geometry used for Perspective must be compatible with relativistic locality.

Possible approaches include:

- local algebras assigned to spacetime regions;
- causal diamonds;
- boundary-state formulations;
- process composition across arbitrary spacetime regions;
- covariant histories;
- observer- or system-relative proper-time organization.

The perspectival present can then be local: a record/access frontier associated with a situated system rather than a global simultaneity surface.

This is not merely a philosophical preference. A preferred foliation would add physical structure that standard relativistic quantum theory does not normally supply. The program therefore seeks a formulation whose essential claims are foliation-independent or whose foliation dependence is explicitly physical rather than coordinate-induced.


12. QUANTUM CONTEXTUALITY

Quantum contextuality places additional constraints on a classical picture of local possibility.

One cannot generally assign globally consistent preexisting values to all observables while preserving the empirical predictions of quantum theory. This means a local geometry of possibility may need to be context-sensitive without becoming arbitrary.

A sheaf-like or algebraic description can encode which sets of observables are jointly measurable and how local contexts overlap. For the project, the important lesson is that "all physical detail" does not necessarily mean one giant classical configuration containing simultaneous values for every question.

Perspective may instead be a coherent local organization of compatible records and observables embedded in a noncommutative global structure.


13. THE ROLE OF METRICS

Topology tells us which structures are continuous and which neighborhoods exist. Metrics add quantitative distance.

Several different quantum distances may be relevant depending on the object:

- Fubini-Study distance for pure-state rays;
- trace distance for density operators;
- Bures distance and quantum Fisher geometry;
- diamond distance for channels;
- strategy/process norms for multi-time processes;
- Wasserstein-like distances in some open-system or semiclassical settings;
- information-geometric divergences.

The project does not assume that one universal scalar metric governs all relevant objects. The correct geometry may be Finsler-like, normed, partially ordered, multi-metric, or higher categorical.

What matters is that quantitative comparison derive from the physics of the object rather than arbitrary coordinates.


14. FROM LOCAL STRUCTURE TO CONSEQUENCE

The eventual consequence relation is expected to depend on oriented continuation within the physical geometry.

A difference between X and Y becomes consequential relative to a Perspective when that difference leads to distinguishable or structurally different continuation in the local physical process. The relevant notion of "different" should already be constrained by the local geometry.

This creates a hierarchy:

    physical difference
        -> physically structured relation
        -> situated accessibility
        -> oriented continuation
        -> consequential difference.

The hierarchy allows microscopic differences to remain physically real without requiring every microdifference to count equally in later consequence geometry.

For example, two gas microstates may be far apart microscopically yet converge rapidly to the same residual process at a given local interface. Conversely, one microscopic detector event may be amplified into a durable record that changes a large downstream region. The local geometry and continuation dynamics determine which case occurs.


15. CURRENT RESEARCH TARGETS

The immediate mathematical tasks are:

1. Identify the minimal physical structures required to define local quantum possibility without assuming a classical state space.

2. Determine how state-space geometry, operator algebra, spacetime causality, and process composition fit together.

3. Define the situated local object corresponding to a Perspective.

4. Specify invariance under physically equivalent representations.

5. Characterize record formation and decoherence as structures within the local geometry.

6. Express oriented continuation and compatibility of histories in representation-independent terms.

7. Determine when coarse maps are physically legitimate and when they destroy relevant structure.

8. Connect the physical local geometry to downstream consequence-preserving compression.

The objective is a physically generated geometry rich enough to support Perspective and consequence, yet general enough to apply beyond one specific quantum formalism.
