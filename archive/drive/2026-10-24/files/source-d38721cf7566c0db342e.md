**Date:** 16 October 2026.
**Status:** a working outline for discussion. v4.1 is written from scratch
(ruling 23). The v4 chapters, ledger r9 and the program review are sources of
content, not texts to edit.

**Length.** About **160,000–200,000 words** of main text in 33 chapters, plus
25,000–35,000 words of appendices. For comparison, v4's main text (chapters

provenance. Most of the added length is new material from the experiments, and
it lands in Parts II, III and VI.

**Changes from r4 (16 October):** chapter contents follow the foundation

  - each result argued once;
  - proofs, adapter details and the claim table go to the appendices;
  - the closing chapters are kept short.

## Governing discipline

1. **Headline** (adapted from Sol): the formal architecture remains intact.
   Several of its physical mechanism claims now have substantially stronger
   support. The central thesis is argued and testable, and its decisive tests
   are named.
2. **Every substantive claim carries a status,** recorded in the working
   files and visible in the prose wherever it matters:

   | Label | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | D | definition |
   | T | theorem, with assumptions and proof |
   | M | model result: shown in a test-bed adapter, within its scope |
   | E | empirical correspondence from the published literature |
   | N | normative premise, named |
   | C | thesis (asserted and defended) or conjecture (proposed); either can lose |

   No sentence moves between these categories silently.
3. **Do not weaken Omega because a toy model surprises us, and do not
   strengthen it because a toy model agrees with us** (Sol).
4. **Firewalls:**
   - between each model result and the thesis it bears on;
   - between the subject distinction and the move to ethics.
5. **Depth, not repetition:**
   - each result is argued once, in the chapter where it does work, and other
     chapters refer back to it;
   - the extra length goes to nuance, scope, objections and worked examples;
   - proofs and evidence rows go to the appendices.
6. **Voice:** one voice, channelling Parfit:
   - arguments explicit, and numbered where they carry weight;
   - premises named;
   - objections stated at full strength before they are answered;
   - concrete physical claims;
   - austere formal sections;
   - worked examples instead of analogies.

   Standing rules: "program", "manuscript", author-year citations, an
   "In words:" line after every boxed equation, and only the approved
   aphorism.

**Decisions still pending, marked where they bite:**
- [P1] three tiers for compounding (ruling 7);
- [P2] a confirmatory protocol for central-thesis tests (ruling 12);
- [P3] impartiality as a named premise (rulings 2 and 3);
- [P4] the "valuer" term (ruling 10).

## Part II — The measure (5 chapters, 26,000–32,000 words)

### 4. Lushness
- Definition: finite-history entropy of the physical process; the chain rule;
  uniqueness within the class (D, T).
- Breadth versus its explanations: generativity, persistence and control
  explain breadth but are not terms in it.
- Everything counts:
  - randomness, gas included (ruling 5);
  - stochastic costs need no separate term;
  - repeated labels are not outcomes.
- *Sources:* v4 chapters 4.1–4.4 and 12.2–12.3; A1, A10, B12.

### 5. Horizon, rate and offset
- Lushness is cumulative; rate and offset as summaries; the reducible-chain
  identity (T).
- **The offset theorem and its scope** (T): within a shared finite irreducible
  generator, roots share a rate. Growing or nonergodic systems can differ.
- **Preparation, process and mechanism** (D): which comparisons the theorem
  decides in advance.
- **Crossover horizon** T× = −Δb/Δr (T, asymptotic), with a worked example.
- **The profile** (T, A.3.9): the comparison is the profile Δ_n(a : b). Rate is
  its slope, offset its limiting level, the crossover its zero. Verdicts
  concern everything that follows; expected persistence is a reading, not a
  choice.
- **Persistence and the generative window** (T, after audit): why a finite
  irreducible world shares its final rate, and why rate in practice means the
  rate during the window.
- Ruin with a residual process keeps its rate. Short-termism and its mirror.
- *Sources:* v4 chapters 4.5–4.6 and 12.5; A2, A11, A13, A17, B8; rate-first
  analysis r3.

### 6. Resolution and grain
- Cadence as part of the declared resolution.
- The temporal expansion: why rankings flip, and the crossover resolution.
- **The resolution corridor:** admissible grains, sign stability, and the
  difference between small differences and flips.
- Dominance across resolutions: the compounding loop as a worked case.
- State grain: hidden registers, conditioning bounds, unresolved overlaps.
  Bank resolution as a worked check: the self-against-whole conflict of
  chapter 22 is grain-stable.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 4.7; B3; temporal calibration; fix note r2.

### 7. Subjects of the measure
- The marginal histories of a subsystem; one-observation and deeper bounds
  (D, T).
- The self and the whole field: one objective, different subjects (rulings
  1–3).
- Intertwining measured: mutual information between histories.
- **The self–whole gap** (T, A.7.6): self and whole differ by exactly one term,
  the change in the rest of the field given the subject. The model case from
  chapter 22.
- **Subjects that lose nothing** (T, A.7.7).
- Why private rankings are often unresolved, and what tighter queries would
  need.
- **Firewall:** this chapter shows that subject-relative rankings can differ.
  Which subject ethics takes is derived in chapter 16.
- *Sources:* C5; B1; the policy-search reports.

### 8. What the measure compresses
- **The compression boundary:** equal lushness can hide different structure.
  Whether the differences reappear bears on the central thesis.
- **Mixtures and risk** (T, after audit): the lushness of a physical gamble is
  the probability-weighted lushness of its outcomes, plus at most H(p).
- Valence is detectable only by its structural impact (ruling 8). Stated as a
  limit of the measure's current reach, not an escape from refutation.
- Neighbouring objectives: maximum occupancy, empowerment, causal entropic
  forces (E). The program's controllers as maximum-occupancy agents.
- *Sources:* A14, A19, A23; rate-first analysis r3.

## Part IV — Value (5 chapters, 24,000–30,000 words)

### 16. From measure to value
- **The link:** value requires valuers, the link from the physical to the
  moral; not a moral axiom. What it gives directly: future value needs future
  valuing (persistence as the metavalue); no free-floating disvalue.
- **The valuer** (D, A.7.1): anything that physically instantiates a frame
  relative to which its possible continuations are defined; every physical
  system, gas included; the whole world as the ultimate frame; summaries of
  the field are not valuers. Why disvalue is then a registered contraction.
  Dependence: under a hedonic definition, negative levels return.
- **What makes a frame physically instantiated** (open): candidate criterion
  and the lattice program (local cluster, open controller, composite, global
  parity, hash, conserved local excitation, moving boundary, noise region).
  The same question for admissible grains and quantum records.
- Commitment carries to conditions: a principle of practical reason, not an
  axiom of the ethics. The vortex.
- **The unindexed comparison** (D, A.7.8) and **its subject** (T, A.7.9):
  sufficiency gives the whole field; unaffected independent parts cancel
  (A.7.10); causal reach, not dependence; no ledger of subjects.
- **Why expect ethical judgement to track the whole** (part of the thesis):
  the two comparisons mostly agree (intertwining); the self has no privileged
  boundary; the standard others can use.
- Misappropriation by power-seeking controllers, and the impartial standard
  the whole-field reading gives others (ruling 3).
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 5.1–5.3; B1, B9; rulings 1–3, 10, 25, 26, 28 and 32;
  Appendix A r9.

### 17. Protection
- **Channels, not stock** (M; ruling 9): keep the channel open. Criticality is
  a statement about being, not about ethics.
- Dependence produces maintenance (M). The structure of harm, not raw hazard,
  sets policy (M).
- **Precaution in two layers** (ruling 22):
  - option value;
  - a margin from the actor's model error, which correction shrinks.
- Precautionary protection, restated. Strict protection now follows from the
  largest contraction (A.9.4), with no valuing premise.
- *Sources:* A4, A5, C14, C15; v4 chapter 5.4; rate-first analysis r3.

### 18. Harm, recovery and irreversibility
- Harm as a physical signature; the verdict belongs to lushness.
- **Contraction and expansion** (D, A.9.2): a comparison between options, not
  a level. Harm and benefit are their names under the central thesis.
  Material injury is a third thing; the funded-extraction arm is the test.
- **Foreclosure** (D, A.9.3), reported beside contraction. The largest
  contraction (T, A.9.4).
- Recoverable against irreversible harm. A rate-lowering harm eventually
  outweighs any finite offset, but the crossover can be late (M).
- **The cost of an early end** (T, A.4.9): regeneration shortens the loss;
  irreversibility makes it largest.
- Recovery, redemption, and turnover across scales.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 6.1–6.7; B2; C23.

### 19. Suffering
- Structural disvalue: what the measure can register. Suffering is bad
  insofar as it contracts the field relative to the same world without it,
  not relative to no world.
- **Firewall:**
  - the model result (M): in the tested family, matched, obstructed suffering
    lowers lushness relative to the matched comparator;
  - the universal statement (C), in the originator's final wording (ruling 8).

  A reversal in an adequate model would count against the thesis.
  The resolution limit states reach; it is not an escape hatch.
- Register variation is not futile struggle (the correction to E9).
- Signals: tracking that cannot act; hijacked signals; signal design (open).
- *Sources:* A22–A25, C16, C20, C21; register controls; v4 chapter 6.8.

### 20. The central thesis
- **The statement:** a correspondence. What we recognize as good corresponds
  to widening of the whole field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to
  narrowing. Not a definition (ruling 32).
- **The proof procedure:** one class of cases at a time, each with positive
  and negative cases, matched controls, compression cases and resolution
  tests, and no hidden value terms.
- Why it is a thesis and not a theorem; why the program expects it to hold
  (persistence and uncertainty).
- **What would decide it:**
  - the compression suite and equal-breadth pairs;
  - **the factory-farming objection,** the named adversarial target (open);
  - plural ecology against monoculture.
- What failure would mean, including lushness's version of the repugnant
  conclusion.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 5.5–5.6; A14, A22, A28, C6.
