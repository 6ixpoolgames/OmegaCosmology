CONSCIOUSNESS AND PHENOMENAL REALIZATION
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

The consciousness branch asks how phenomenal presence relates to the physically situated structures developed elsewhere in the program.

The physical program can describe a Perspective as a locally situated, record-conditioned structure inside universal quantum possibility. It can describe persistence, identity, agency, valuerhood, memory, control, and consequence. Consciousness adds a further question:

    Which physically realized perspectives are lived from within,
    and what relation connects physical organization to phenomenal presence?

The project uses awareness for minimal phenomenal presence and consciousness for organized or elaborated phenomenal life involving some combination of integration, memory, cognition, self-modeling, valence, and control.

The central placeholder is a correspondence relation

    kappa
        subseteq
    P_phys x E,

where P_phys is the class of physically situated perspectives and E is a space of phenomenal occurrences.

The relation is written as a relation rather than a function because subject individuation, uniqueness, overlap, and multiplicity remain part of the research problem.


1. THE PHENOMENAL FACT

The epistemic starting point is minimal:

    awareness occurs.

This fact does not determine the ontology of awareness.

Possible accounts include:

- physical identity;
- emergence;
- dual-aspect structure;
- fundamental psychophysical law;
- panpsychist or panprotopsychist structure;
- idealist reconstruction;
- other correspondence theories.

The present program does not require one of these to be selected before the physical architecture is developed.

Its aim is to specify the structures that any successful consciousness account must connect to.


2. PHYSICAL PERSPECTIVE AND PHENOMENAL PERSPECTIVE

A physical Perspective is a situated structure inside physical possibility.

It is characterized by such things as:

- current physical support;
- available records;
- local causal access;
- accessible interactions;
- present conditioning;
- continuation structure.

A phenomenal Perspective is the occurrence of experience from a bounded locus.

The working question is therefore not whether perspective exists only when consciousness exists.

Physical perspective is broader.

The question is which physical perspectives, or which structures within them, instantiate phenomenal presence.


3. THE CORRESPONDENCE PROBLEM

Let

    P_phys

denote physically realized situated perspectives.

Let

    E

denote phenomenal occurrences.

Then

    kappa
        subseteq
    P_phys x E

records the unknown relation between physical and phenomenal structure.

Different theories constrain kappa differently.

Identity theory:
    the physical and phenomenal descriptions pick out the same underlying event.

Emergentism:
    phenomenal structure arises when physical organization enters an appropriate regime.

Dual aspect:
    physical and phenomenal properties are two presentations of one underlying reality.

Psychophysical-law approach:
    additional lawful structure connects physical states and phenomenal states.

Panpsychist approaches:
    phenomenal or proto-phenomenal structure is more widely distributed and composition becomes central.

The notation does not decide among these.

It localizes the question.


4. CONSCIOUSNESS IS NOT REQUIRED FOR PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCE

The physical program does not depend on consciousness to define:

- possibility;
- local physical structure;
- Perspective;
- records;
- consequence;
- compression;
- Lushness;
- agency.

A nonconscious process can have deep physical consequences.

A nonconscious agent can control continuation.

A nonconscious valuer may also be possible if endogenous continuation-sensitive organization exists without phenomenality.

This means consciousness can be studied as a downstream realization problem rather than inserted into fundamental physics by definition.


5. CONSCIOUSNESS AND VALUERHOOD

The active structural conjecture is that consciousness may provide a sufficient route to valuerhood.

The argument is based on the architecture normally associated with conscious control.

A conscious system integrates:

- sensory information;
- bodily state;
- memory;
- affect;
- prediction;
- competing action tendencies;
- future consequence.

This supports an architecture of the form:

    distributed signals
        -> integrated temporoperceptual state
        -> differentiated anticipated continuations
        -> action selection
        -> changed future input.

If phenomenality occurs only in systems with this kind of integrated future-oriented control, then conscious systems already satisfy the core conditions of valuerhood.

This motivates:

    C(P) -> V(P)

as a candidate structural implication.

The converse is not assumed.


6. TEMPOROPERCEPTUAL COHESION

A conscious system acts on information arriving through different physical channels and at different delays.

Its lived present is therefore not a literal instantaneous global time slice.

It is an integrated control surface.

A schematic mapping is:

    distributed causally available signals
        -> integrated temporoperceptual state.

This state can combine:

- recent sensory input;
- bodily regulation;
- short-term memory;
- affective state;
- inferred environmental state;
- predicted outcomes.

The resulting experienced present is a temporally thick, action-relevant organization.


7. THE PERSPECTIVAL PRESENT

The physical present is special relative to a Perspective because current physical access is concentrated there.

The phenomenal present is the lived organization associated with that situated support.

This suggests a layered picture:

    universal quantum possibility
        -> record-conditioned physical Perspective
        -> integrated present support
        -> phenomenal realization.

The conscious present therefore need not correspond to a universal cosmic now.

It is local, situated, and physically constructed.


8. MEMORY AND EXPERIENCED TIME

Memory is present physical organization carrying information about earlier structure.

The experience of having a past can therefore be represented without requiring an awareness-token to move through a second metaphysical timeline.

The current state contains:

- records interpreted as past;
- continuity relations;
- anticipated future;
- ongoing control processes.

In a block-like cosmology, all local states can belong to the completed physical structure while each situated phenomenal occurrence contains an asymmetric organization of memory and anticipation.

This yields:

    physical completion
        compatible with
    internal experienced succession.

The remaining phenomenal question is why this organization is accompanied by lived passage rather than merely representing it.


9. COMPLETED ESSENCE, ONTOLOGICAL ACTUALITY, AND PHENOMENAL ACTUALITY

The broader project distinguishes three structures.

Completed essence:

    the full identity-compatible trajectory or possibility structure of a subject.

Ontological actuality:

    the physically realized continuation within universal reality.

Phenomenal actuality:

    the continuation lived from within by the subject.

In a branching quantum cosmology, many physically realized continuations may exist.

The phenomenal question is then how lived subjecthood relates to that branching structure.


10. BRANCHING SUBJECTHOOD

Several models remain conceptually available.

Branch-local subject model:
    each suitably realized branch contains its own phenomenal continuation.

Branching subject model:
    one prior subject has several successor subjects after branching.

Measure-weighted experience model:
    phenomenal occurrences across branches carry physically or psychophysically determined measure.

Stronger selection model:
    a phenomenal relation selects or privileges one lived continuation relative to a wider physical structure.

The physical quantum theory alone does not settle which description of subject identity is correct.

The issue combines:

- personal identity;
- quantum branching;
- phenomenal correspondence;
- measure.


11. PHENOMENAL ACTUALITY AND SELF-LOCATION

Even if all relevant branches are physically actual, a situated subject experiences one local continuation at a time.

This creates an indexical problem:

    Why this experience here?

A minimal answer treats the question as self-location inside a larger completed structure.

A stronger answer adds a phenomenal selection or coupling principle.

The project currently preserves both possibilities at the level of the research architecture.


12. IDENTITY AND SUBJECT CONTINUITY

Structural persistence is not automatically phenomenal continuity.

Let

    I_S

denote an identity relation or identity invariant for subject S.

This can represent continuity of:

- organization;
- memory;
- causal history;
- embodiment;
- policy;
- self-model.

Phenomenal subject continuity asks a stronger question:

    Are successive phenomenal states given to the same subject?

The two may coincide in ordinary biological cases without being conceptually identical.


13. THE SOUL INVARIANT

The theological branch uses the term soul invariant for the inward identity-conferring continuity by which changing embodied sections belong to one creature.

This is not defined as an immaterial substance.

Its structural role is:

    changing embodiment
        + world-supported identity relation
        -> one continuing creature.

The consciousness problem adds:

    does that structural continuity also bind one phenomenal subject?

A satisfactory account of consciousness and personal identity must eventually connect these.


14. FISSION

Fission cases make identity difficult.

Suppose one subject-like physical process divides into two successors, each preserving substantial memory and organization.

Several relations can hold simultaneously:

- both are continuous with the predecessor;
- neither is uniquely identical under a one-to-one identity criterion;
- each becomes a distinct future locus of experience.

This suggests that continuity and numerical identity may need to be separated.

The consequence framework can preserve what matters across fission even before metaphysical identity is fully resolved.


15. PHENOMENAL VALENCE

Valence is a particularly important interface between consciousness and value.

Examples include:

- pain;
- pleasure;
- hunger;
- fear;
- attraction;
- aversion;
- relief;
- reward.

These states appear to encode asymmetric orientation among possible continuations.

A schematic relation is:

    phenomenal valence
        -> endogenous orientation
        -> action / learning
        -> altered continuation.

This makes valence a strong candidate marker of valuerhood.

It does not follow that all consciousness must be valenced.


16. QUALIA

Qualia refers to phenomenal character:

    what an experience is like.

A complete physical account of information processing can describe:

- discrimination;
- report;
- memory;
- action;
- integration.

The qualia problem asks why any of this has a particular phenomenal character.

The project currently treats that mapping as unresolved.

In notation:

    physical organization
        -> kappa
        -> phenomenal character.

The main task is to constrain kappa enough that it becomes explanatory rather than merely a name for the gap.


17. THE WAKE CONCEPT

The wake refers to the formal and causal dynamics produced as signals pass through a recurrent, self-maintaining subject organization.

It includes:

- internal state changes;
- recurrent processing;
- predictive updating;
- environmental effects;
- feedback.

The wake is the physically describable processing structure.

Awareness is the phenomenal fact associated with some such structures.

Qualia is the character of that phenomenal occurrence.

Thus:

    wake != awareness != qualia

as conceptual roles, even if a final theory identifies some of them more tightly.


18. VORTICAL BINDING

A candidate architecture for subject unity uses recurrent cross-boundary organization.

A vortical subject:

- receives signals;
- integrates them;
- acts;
- alters its environment;
- receives the consequences of those actions;
- reconstructs itself through the loop.

The attractive idea is that the same organization that sustains identity may also define the bounded locus within which information is integrated phenomenally.

This supplies a candidate bearer or binding architecture.

It does not yet explain why the bearer is conscious.


19. SUBJECT BOUNDARIES

Consciousness requires an account of why some physical information belongs to one subject rather than another.

Candidate boundary features include:

- causal integration;
- recurrence;
- predictive closure;
- common control;
- shared memory;
- common action;
- information bottlenecks;
- organismic boundaries.

No single criterion is currently sufficient.

The subject boundary may be graded or scale-dependent.


20. DISTRIBUTED AND OVERLAPPING SUBJECTS

Complex systems can contain nested organizations.

Examples include:

- split-brain systems;
- colonies;
- multi-agent AI;
- social organizations;
- symbiotic organisms.

This raises possibilities of:

- multiple subjects in one body;
- overlapping subjects;
- higher-level subjects;
- no higher-level subject despite strong coordination.

The theory therefore cannot equate physical integration automatically with one conscious subject.


21. THE STRICT P-ZOMBIE LIMIT

Under a strong emergent identity view, a physically and functionally identical duplicate of a conscious human would also be conscious because the relevant physical organization is the conscious organization.

Under other accounts, physical identity may not be enough.

The project uses this thought experiment to distinguish theories of kappa.

It does not rely on philosophical zombies as empirical entities.


22. AWARENESS MEASURE

If many phenomenal occurrences exist across physical possibility, a theory may require a measure over experiences.

One generic form is:

    mu(S)
        =
    Tr(rho A(S)),

where A(S) is an awareness-associated positive operator or effect.

This supplies a mathematical way to assign measure to sets of experiences.

It does not by itself provide:

- subject continuity;
- transition law;
- identity;
- agency;
- moral weight.

Those require further structure.


23. PHYSICAL RECORDS AND AWARENESS

A physical record projector and an awareness operator need not be identical.

A system can contain a record without that record being consciously experienced.

Conversely, awareness may depend on integrated organization not captured by one explicit record variable.

The relation among:

- physical accessibility;
- global availability;
- phenomenal presence;
- reportability

is therefore an empirical and theoretical question.


24. UNIVERSAL HABITATION

One broad conjecture is that every physically admissible perspective satisfying the consciousness correspondence is phenomenally inhabited.

On a branching view, this means phenomenal presence is not allocated by one external moving present.

Each qualifying situated structure carries its own local phenomenal actuality.

The strength of the conjecture depends entirely on the criterion for qualifying perspectives.


25. CONSCIOUSNESS AND QUANTUM THEORY

The project does not require consciousness to collapse the wavefunction.

Quantum measurement, decoherence, records, and branch-relative physical structure can be described without awareness.

The relevant quantum-consciousness question is instead:

    how does phenomenal realization map onto a quantum-supported Perspective?

Any proposed psychophysical influence would need to specify what changes in quantum statistics or dynamics.

Without such a prediction, consciousness remains a realization relation rather than a modification of quantum mechanics.


26. PHENOMENAL STEERING

A stronger speculative possibility assigns phenomenal directedness a role in lived continuation.

Let

    Succ_E(x_n)

denote admissible successors from state x_n within a subject's completed possibility structure.

Let

    W_S(n)

denote phenomenal will or directedness.

Then schematically:

    sigma_S(W_S(n), x_n)
        =
    x_{n+1}^Phi
        in
    Succ_E(x_n).

There are several readings.

Compatibilist:
    sigma describes the same causal process under phenomenal language.

Dual-aspect:
    phenomenal and physical descriptions are co-referential aspects.

Interactionist:
    phenomenal directedness contributes additional causal selection.

Only the third introduces new psychophysical dynamics.

The present research program does not depend on it.


27. CONSCIOUSNESS AND AGENCY

Consciousness may improve control by creating an integrated state in which heterogeneous information can be compared and acted upon.

Possible functions include:

- temporal integration;
- conflict resolution;
- counterfactual simulation;
- flexible policy switching;
- social modeling;
- compressed action selection.

This makes consciousness potentially important for high-level agency even if agency in general does not require consciousness.


28. CONSCIOUSNESS AND LUSHNESS

Consciousness enters Lushness indirectly.

A conscious subject contributes:

- its own phenomenal continuation;
- endogenous valuation;
- learning;
- agency;
- generativity;
- social and ecological interaction.

But Lushness is not defined by counting conscious subjects.

Physical continuation geometry comes first.

Consciousness identifies one important class of value-bearing loci within it.


29. CONSCIOUSNESS AND MORAL STANDING

Phenomenal experience is a major candidate basis for moral standing, especially where valence is present.

However, standing is broader than the consciousness problem.

Questions remain about:

- nonconscious valuers;
- degrees of consciousness;
- distributed subjects;
- uncertain consciousness;
- copied subjects;
- brief versus persistent experience.

The ethical framework therefore needs a standing theory in addition to a consciousness theory.


30. CURRENT RESEARCH QUESTIONS

The consciousness branch is currently organized around the following technical questions:

1. What physical structures qualify as situated Perspectives?
2. Which of those instantiate phenomenal presence?
3. Is kappa identity, emergence, dual aspect, or additional law?
4. What physical architecture binds one subject?
5. How does subject identity behave under branching and fission?
6. What determines phenomenal measure across possible experiences?
7. How does valence relate to endogenous orientation?
8. Can consciousness be sufficient for valuerhood without being necessary?
9. Does phenomenal will add anything beyond physical agency?
10. Which aspects of experienced time follow from records and temporal integration, and which require a further phenomenal account?

The physical program can progress without resolving all ten.

A complete account of lived reality cannot.
