> **TL;DR**
>
> Six tests could overturn the central thesis: busy noise against
> organization, engineered suffering and factory farming, material loss that
> does not narrow, monsters and monocultures, options of equal lushness that
> differ in ways that matter, and the realism of the models. For each, this
> chapter says what the theory requires before the test is run, what result
> would count against it, and what in the theory would have to change if it
> fails. None of the six is yet decided. This chapter develops the cases named
> in [Overview section 6](#overview-6).

The thesis is stated so that it can be killed. [Chapter 31](#chapter-31)
surveyed what the tests already run have shown. This chapter turns to the tests
most likely to do the killing, and fixes in advance what each would have to
show. A claim whose failure condition is written down only after the result is
in has not been tested.

---

## 32.1 How each test is stated

**Each test names what the theory requires, what would count against it, and
what would have to change if it failed. All three are written before the
test is run.**

A test that can overturn a thesis has to be pinned down before its result is
known. For each of the six, this chapter gives:
1. **the objection,** in its strongest form;
2. **where the evidence stands,** with the tiers of [Appendix D](#appendix-d);
3. **what the theory requires:** the claim, its quantifier and its domain;
4. **what would count against it,** in three grades ([Appendix D, section D.9](#appendix-d)):
   - a **certified contrary** result, resolved against the claim with the same
     sign at every admissible level of detail tested, counts against it;
   - a **contrary result at one level** is pressure, and calls for the other
     levels;
   - an **unresolved** result is no verdict;
5. **what would have to change** if the test fails.

Some conditions are fixed before every run, so that no result can be met by
moving them afterwards ([chapter 12](#chapter-12)):
- **The admissible levels of detail are set by the physics of the case**, not
  by which option they favour, and declared before the run.
- **The horizon is set by the physics too.** Where a structure's expected
  persistence is known, the comparison runs at least that far.
- **Design guesses and claims are recorded separately.** Where a crossover is
  expected to fall is a guess unless a theorem fixes it.
- **When a claim fails, the theory changes.** The failure stays on record with
  what it shows about where the theory went wrong.

## 32.2 Busy noise against organization

**The objection.** Lushness counts every future, random ones included. A
process that merely churns faster produces more distinguishable histories than
a slower one that builds something. If that is enough to win, the measure
rewards busyness, and the thesis's claim that good corresponds to organization
that sustains and compounds the conditions for further possibility fails at
the root.

**Where the evidence stands.**
- **Activity counts, and often wins finite comparisons.** Paid variation,
  exchange volume, funded transfer and cycling selectors all add breadth, and
  the measure subtracts nothing for them ([Appendix D, section D.5.1](#appendix-d)).
- **Organization raises the rate, at every level tested (M1).** Enabling loops
  beat their ablations in every setting, and an enabling relation beats a
  non-enabling process at matched accepted work in 12 of 12 settings.
- **Activity was not matched.** At fixed numbers of events, the enabling loop
  loses at 8 events, by 1.76 bits, and wins at 32 and 128. Each of its events
  is also less predictable: 1.05 against 0.38 bits per event.
- **The activity-matched comparison is not built (M4).**

**What the theory requires.** Within the domain of enabling structures that
sustain their own channel, set against a comparator matched in activity and in
accepted work but without the enabling relation, the enabling structure has
the higher rate, resolved at every admissible level of detail. The claim is
universal over that domain. It concerns rate, not every finite horizon: before
the repayment horizon, matched churn may lead, and where that horizon falls is
a design guess unless a theorem fixes it.

**What would count against it.** A certified result in which the matched
churn has the higher rate. A lead for churn at finite horizons before the
crossover does not count against the claim; a lead in rate does.

**What would have to change.** The claim that organization raises the rate
would survive, since it is shown without the matched comparator. What would
fail is the claim that the rate gain comes from enabling structure and not
from activity alone, and with it the first prediction under the thesis: that
what we recognize as good organization corresponds to widening because it
builds. [Overview section 4](#overview-4) would have to say that generativity
is not how the future grows beyond what activity alone provides. The
admissible cadence could not be moved to rescue the claim, because it is set
by the physics of the case before the run.

## 32.3 Engineered suffering and factory farming

**The objection.** Valuers might be engineered whose suffering, or whose
confinement, makes the world lusher. A dense population of confined animals
might support more distinct futures than the land would support otherwise.
Factory farming is the real-world form. If lushness prefers it, the thesis
contradicts one of the clearest judgements we have.

**Where the evidence stands.**
- **Within a confinement, suffering narrows (M2).** A valuer with an aversive
  drive to escape an obstacle it cannot pass had narrower futures than the
  same valuer without the drive, at all 18 primary points at the finest level
  tested, and in all 216 comparisons. At a coarser level some comparisons are
  unresolved; none reverses ([chapter 19](#chapter-19)).
- **The comparison across worlds is not built (M4).** Whether a plural
  ecology outproduces a dense confined population on the same land is untested.
- **Steering by incentive against steering by punishment is not built (M4).**

The framework detects suffering only through its effect on the future. It
therefore stakes the case on that effect: the truncation of lives, the
confinement of behaviour, and the conversion of land with many niches into
land with one.

**What the theory requires.** Three claims.
- **Within any confinement, removing the suffering raises lushness.**
  Universal, over obstruction that cannot be passed, with the valuer matched.
  This is the suffering test class.
- **A benefit to others that can be had by a route that narrows less is
  dominated.** This follows from comparing options from the same present: the
  route that keeps more open ranks higher.
- **A plural ecology outproduces confinement.** On the same land and the same
  maintained gradient, a plural ecology of several kinds of valuer, suited to
  different patches, has a higher rate than a confined monoculture with a
  harvesting controller. Universal, over land with more than one niche. Land
  with a single niche is kept as a limit case, not set aside. Livestock uses
  more than 80 per cent of farmland and supplies about 18 per cent of food
  calories and 37 per cent of protein (Poore and Nemecek 2018); much grazing
  land cannot be cropped, and grazing it with varied, mobile, low-density
  animals is itself a plural ecology.

**The test.** Four arms on the same land and gradient: a confined monoculture
with a harvesting controller; a plural ecology; pastoral use of land that
cannot be cropped, with the plural ecology elsewhere; and the confined
monoculture without the aversive drive. The subject is the whole field.

**What would count against it.** A certified result in which the confined
monoculture beats the plural ecology on rate, resolved at every admissible
level of detail. A result at one level is pressure. A win for confinement on
single-niche land is reported as the limit case it is.

**What would have to change.** Lushness would face its own version of the
repugnant conclusion, in a class of cases where our judgement is clear. That is
what the thesis says counts against it. The program would report the failure
and restate the thesis: either restricted, with the restriction said plainly,
or shown to need something the measure compresses away (section 32.6). It
would not add a term for suffering after the fact.

## 32.4 Material loss that does not narrow

**The objection.** We recognize material injury as harm: destroyed apparatus,
appropriated resources, lost stock. If lushness rises after such a loss, the
correspondence between harm and narrowing fails.

**Where the evidence stands.**
- **Rate-lowering harm loses in the long run (M1, provisional).** In 40 of 40
  long-run comparisons, the intact structure beats the one that lost rate.
- **But it can lead for a long time.** Replacing repairable damage with
  permanent loss raised lushness at 256 time units in 4 of 8 contrasts, at all
  cadences. In one setting the loss led by 1.17 bits at 256, still led at
  1,024, and fell 2.46 bits behind by 4,096 ([model](#c-compounding)).
- **Funded appropriation widened every party.** The dominator paid from its
  own store and net flow ran toward it, yet the whole field and both parties
  gained breadth, through turnover ([model](#c-flow)).
- **A party's breadth rose while its stock fell,** in a funded pump.

**What the theory requires.** Two claims, of different status.
- **A theorem.** A harm that lowers the rate eventually outweighs any finite
  gain ([Corollary A.3.8](#a-3-8)). The intact structure leads over everything
  that follows ([Corollary A.3.9](#a-3-9)).
- **A claim of the thesis.** Every material injury that matters ethically
  appears as narrowing over the window that matters, set by the expected
  persistence of the structure harmed (section A.15). Universal, over material
  injuries that our judgements firmly count as harm, with the full accounts of
  every party and the realized flows in the model.

**What would count against it.** A certified case in which a loss we firmly
recognize as harm leaves the whole field wider over the whole physically set
window, with the injured party's accounts complete. A lead for the loss before
the crossover, inside a window that runs past it, does not count; a lead that
lasts the whole window does.

**What would have to change.** The correspondence for harm would no longer be
universal. Some harms we recognize would not be narrowings, and the thesis
would have to say which, and why our judgement tracks something else there.
The window could not be shortened or lengthened to rescue the claim, because
it is set by expected persistence, not by the result. For funded
appropriation, the test that decides is the version funded by the dominator
with the realized flows measured; until it lands, the case stays as pressure.

## 32.5 Monsters and monocultures

**The objection.** One vast valuer, or a world of one kind of valuer, might
outscore a plural world. If lushness prefers concentration, the thesis
contradicts our judgement that a world of many kinds of life is better than a
world turned over to one.

**Where the evidence stands.** No test is built (M4). The calculations made so
far for thin worlds are diagnostics, not tests. One formal result bears on it:
concentration costs breadth only through what it does to the continuation
([Corollary A.10.3](#a-10-3)), so the measure does not prefer plurality by
definition.

**What the theory requires.** On the same land and gradient, a plural ecology
is at least as lush, on rate, as a single vast valuer or a monoculture.
Universal, over worlds with more than one niche, with the same limit case as
section 32.3.

**What would count against it.** A certified result in which the monster or
the monoculture beats the plural ecology on rate.

**What would have to change.** The thesis would fail for a class where our
judgement is clear, and the program would say so. It could not defend
plurality by stipulating it, because mechanism words carry no value of their
own ([chapter 11](#chapter-11)). It would restate the thesis with the failure
as a stated exception, or find what the measure is missing.

## 32.6 Options of equal lushness that differ in ways that matter

**The objection.** Lushness compresses Omega to a single number. Two options
can have equal lushness while differing in suffering, control, recovery, or
the structures that support valuers. If such a difference never shows up as a
difference in lushness, the measure misses something ethics needs, and the
thesis fails wherever that difference matters.

**Where the evidence stands.**
- **The suite of equal-lushness pairs is specified and not run (M4).**
- **Inner variation can raise lushness with no benefit to access or survival.**
  Paid independent variation beat an inactive register despite shorter lives
  and less food ([model](#c-register-controls)). At short horizons the measure
  is blind to suffering, and to information that cannot act.
- **Breadth and stock can move apart,** as in the funded pump of section 32.4.

[Overview section 3](#overview-3) took this risk in the open: if some morally
crucial difference never shows up in what can follow, lushness will miss it,
and the difference is not brought back in through a weighting.

**What the theory requires.** For pairs of options equal in lushness at one
horizon and level of detail that differ in a way our judgements treat as
morally crucial, the difference reappears as a difference in lushness at a
longer horizon or a finer admissible level of detail. Universal, over such
differences. This is part of the thesis, not a property of the measure.

**The test.** Pairs built to be equal in lushness at a declared horizon and
level, differing in one respect at a time. Each pair is followed to longer
horizons and finer admissible levels, and its outcome is reported as one of
three: the difference reappears with the sign the thesis requires; it
reappears with the opposite sign; or it never reappears.

**What would count against it.** A certified persistent pair: equal at every
admissible level and horizon, differing in a way our judgements firmly treat
as crucial. A difference that reappears with the wrong sign counts against the
thesis too.

**What would have to change.** Whole-field lushness would not be the whole of
what ethical judgement tracks. The measure would stay what it is: it is derived,
and a weight added after the fact would make the result decide the theory. The
thesis would be restated to say what in Omega carries the difference, if
anything does. Omega holds more structure than its lushness, and a restated
thesis might have to read that structure directly.

## 32.7 The realism of the models

**The objection.** Every model result comes from a small, hand-built world.
Some rest on two base worlds, one mobility coefficient or one kinetic
parameter. Real organization works in space, at scale, against a thermal
background whose branching at fine detail is enormous. The rankings may be
artefacts of construction.

**Where the evidence stands.**
- **Audits have caught construction artefacts before.** A steering rule that
  redirected all of a model's chemistry was found on audit and its results
  withdrawn. A permissive control was found to have built in its answer
  ([Appendix D, section D.7](#appendix-d)).
- **Several sweeps have not landed.** The suffering family's parameter sweep,
  the lattice's mobility sweep, and the policy search's kinetic sweep.
- **The comparison against disorder with room to spread is not built.** The
  compounding models have no positions; the one spatial model had a single
  carrier and did not sustain assembly.
- **No estimator for realistic systems exists** ([chapter 33](#chapter-33)).

**What the theory requires.** Two claims, of different reach.
- **Within the models:** the rankings that hold at M1 and M2 survive the
  pending sweeps, and the capability claim survives in a spatial model with
  several carriers, where disorder has its advantages. Universal, over the
  parameter ranges each sweep declares.
- **Beyond the models:** organization can raise the rate at which futures open
  in real systems. A capability claim, over the real-system settings named in
  [chapter 33](#chapter-33), where its tests are listed.

**What would count against it.** Within the models, a ranking that reverses,
certified, when its sweep lands, or a spatial model in which organization never
raises the rate once disorder can spread. Beyond them, certified real-system
results, in the settings named, in which no organized process raises it. A
single failure there is pressure, since the claim is that it can happen.

**What would have to change.** A result that does not survive its sweep drops
a tier, or becomes a diagnostic, and every chapter citing it is revised. If the
spatial model shows that organization never raises the rate against disorder
with room to spread, the capability claim's domain shrinks to settings without
dispersal, and [Overview section 4](#overview-4) would have to say so. That
would be the largest revision on this list short of the thesis itself.

## 32.8 What a failure would mean

**The thesis is universal over classes of cases in which our judgements are
clear. One certified failure in such a class counts against it.**

Each of the six tests could produce one. If one does, the program will not
reclassify the claim as a guess, move the level of detail or the horizon, or
add a term to the measure. It will report what failed and what that shows, and
the theory will change to fit the evidence. Some failures would narrow a
prediction's domain. Some, the persistent equal pair or confinement beating a
plural ecology, would restrict the thesis itself. The program is after what is
true, not after keeping its first statement of it
([chapter 12](#chapter-12)).

---

## 32.9 What this chapter does not claim

- **That the thesis will pass these tests.** None is yet decided.
- **That these are the only tests.** They are the ones most likely to
  overturn it on present evidence. [Appendix E](#appendix-e) states every
  objection by field.
- **That passing them would establish the thesis beyond the models.** The
  real-system tests of [chapter 33](#chapter-33) would still be needed.
