What has the program shown, and how strongly? This chapter surveys the
evidence behind [Overview section 6](#overview-6), one class of cases at a
time, and answers by status: what is derived, what holds in models and how
far, what is observed in the world, and what is still open. Every claim here
has a row in [Appendix D](#appendix-d), every model is described in
[Appendix C](#appendix-c), and nothing here claims more than its row allows.
[Chapter 32](#chapter-32) then states the tests that could still overturn the
thesis.

## 31.1 How to read the evidence

**Every claim has a status, and every model result has a tier that says how
far its ranking holds.**

A claim is a definition, a theorem, a model result, an empirical
correspondence or a thesis ([chapter 12](#chapter-12)). The manuscript assumes
no normative premise. For model results, a single status is not enough.
[Chapter 12](#chapter-12) set the rule: of two faithful levels of detail, the
finer governs, and a ranking holds when it is resolved at the level that
governs, either computed there or certified from a coarser level. Results
meet that rule to different degrees, so each carries a tier.
- **M1:** resolved at the finest faithful level of detail tested, where two or
  more levels were tested.
- **M2:** resolved at the one level tested, or with the finer levels only
  partly tested.
- **M3:** unresolved at the finest level tested.
- **M4:** not yet built.

A ranking that a finer level reverses is corrected by it, and the coarser
result is kept as a record of where the difference comes from. One limit
applies to every tier for now. No model has yet derived its finest faithful
level from physics, so the finest level tested governs provisionally.

Three kinds of result stand outside the tiers because they make no claim on
the principle: operating ranges, which map how a mechanism behaves;
pressure, which the manuscript has to answer; and diagnostics, which fail one
of chapter 12's five conditions and are kept apart in
[Appendix H](#appendix-h).

## 31.2 What is derived

**The measure, its readings over time, the subject of the comparison with no
index, and the elementary facts about information and harm are theorems.**

[Appendix A](#appendix-a) contains eighty-three numbered results, each with
one status. Sixteen are established in the literature, forty-seven are proved
there, three rest on named physical premises, one is a decision model with
stipulated payoffs, and sixteen are definitions. None rests on a normative
premise.

The results the argument leans on most:
- **The measure is unique.** Four requirements on any count of distinct
  futures leave one measure ([Theorem A.2.3](#a-2-3)).
- **Time has an exact structure.** Starting points under one shared finite law
  differ only by a head start ([Theorem A.3.5](#a-3-5)), and over everything
  that follows, rate decides first and head start second
  ([Corollary A.3.9](#a-3-9)).
- **Persistence has an exact price.** Lasting is worth what it keeps open
  beyond its replacement ([Proposition A.4.8](#a-4-8)); an early end costs most
  when the world never rebuilds what was lost ([Proposition A.4.9](#a-4-9)); a
  fragile gain must clear a floor set by its expected life
  ([Corollary A.4.6](#a-4-6)).
- **A coarser description can be checked against a finer one.** Refining a
  description adds to each option exactly the uncertainty of the new detail,
  so a coarser lead survives whenever it exceeds the most that detail could
  add ([Proposition A.6.6](#a-6-6)).
- **Parts and the whole differ by one term** ([Proposition A.7.6](#a-7-6)), and,
  given that the comparison singles out no one, its subject is the whole field
  ([Corollary A.7.9](#a-7-9)). That ethical judgement tracks this comparison
  is part of the thesis, not of the theorem.
- **Information pays only through action** ([Corollary A.8.4](#a-8-4)), and the
  advantage of informed action splits exactly into three parts
  ([Proposition A.8.3](#a-8-3)).
- **The largest narrowing leaves a determined future**
  ([Proposition A.9.4](#a-9-4)).

None of this shows the central thesis. The theorems fix what the measure
ranks. Whether that ranking is the one our moral judgements track is tested
case by case, and section A.15 lists what the formal core does not contain.

## 31.3 Organization

**In the models, organization raises the rate at which futures open, at the
finest level of detail tested. Whether it outproduces disorder doing as much
is not yet tested.**

Five results hold at M1.
- **A locked source.** A law under which organization can release a locked
  energy source beats the same law with that path removed, by 0.260, 0.229
  and 0.197 bits per unit time at three cadences
  ([model C.1](#c-locked-source)). Within the capable law, starting points tie
  in rate, as the offset theorem requires.
- **A route that apparatus speeds up.** Where apparatus accelerates a route
  that stays open without it, an organized start leads an unorganized start of
  equal energy by 4.5 to 7.8 bits at 64 time units, and the acceleration
  raises the rate ([model C.2](#c-corrected-catalysis)). The lead is a head
  start, and the organized start trails before it.
- **An enabling loop.** A loop that captures energy and builds apparatus
  retains apparatus near capacity, 30.79 of 32 at the largest, while each
  single ablation retains at most 27 per cent of capacity. Its rate gain grows
  with capacity, from +2.37 to +6.65 bits per unit time
  ([model C.6](#c-capacity)).
- **Matched work.** At matched accepted work, the enabling relation wins in
  12 of 12 settings at every cadence ([model C.7](#c-matched-work)).
- **Three links.** Three successive paid enabling links compound with retained
  expansion in all 12 shape settings, and the full mechanism beats the unbound
  start, the version with all assistance off and the cut loop at every cadence
  in all 40 settings ([model C.8](#c-compounding)).

The locked source is an access result: the law without the path cannot
release the source. The others show organization raising a rate or a head
start. A rate gain does not by itself show that futures otherwise unreachable
were reached.

Two limits apply.
- **Activity was not matched.** The claim that organization can raise the rate
  does not need activity to be matched, and these results show it. A further
  question does: whether a rate gain reflects enabling structure rather than
  activity alone. Accepted work was matched; activity was not. At fixed
  numbers of events, the loop loses early, by 1.76 bits at 8 events, and wins
  at 32 and 128. The activity-matched comparison is the one
  [chapter 16](#chapter-16) tests, and it is not yet built.
- **Disorder had no room to spread.** The compounding models count molecules
  without positions, so they lack what makes disorder the default. On a
  spatial lattice, an organized seed beat the full thermal mixture and lost to
  an unbound start, whose free monomers moved faster; at the finest cadence
  tested it lost in all 14 laws ([model C.3](#c-lattice)). That model's
  feedback did not sustain assembly, so it maps an operating range and makes
  no claim either way. The spatial model with several carriers is next.

Real systems offer correspondences: photosynthesis raising the rate of
free-energy capture, and bacterial populations adapting without a plateau
(Kleidon 2012; Wiser, Ribeck and Lenski 2013). They motivate the hypothesis
that organization raises the rate at which futures open. They do not test it
([chapter 15](#chapter-15)).

## 31.4 Information through action

**Under matched register conditions, information without leverage does not
outperform free variation. Informed action can outperform it, when information
and action operate together.**
- **Without leverage.** A register that tracks the world but cannot act on it
  lost to one that varied at random in 16 of 16 cases at every horizon at the
  finest cadence tested ([model C.4](#c-paid-sensing)). This is
  [Proposition A.8.2](#a-8-2) at work.
- **With leverage and repair.** Where information could be used to act and the
  structure using it rebuilt, informed action had the higher rate in 57 of 57
  comparisons, across 19 laws and three cadences
  ([model C.5](#c-paid-renewal)).
- **Only together.** Informed action had a higher rate than random action in
  all 57. Information without action, and action without information, each had
  a lower rate than a random register that cannot act, in all 57. The whole
  advantage lies in the third part of the split.
- **Time matters.** Informed action won no law at 64 steps, 5 of 19 by 256 and
  13 of 19 by 1,024. In short-lived valuers, living about 7 time units, it
  never repaid its cost. Both are what the robustness floor predicts.

## 31.5 Harm and protection

**The policy that keeps the most open protects channels, not stock, and the
structure of a harm sets policy more than its raw hazard. Three of these
results rest on one level of detail so far.**
- **Channels, not stock (M2).** The winning policy keeps 4.8 to 9.2 per cent
  less working apparatus than a service-biased control while the channel stays
  open ([model C.9](#c-channels)). The complete policy search ran at one
  cadence.
- **Dependence (M2).** When switching needs working apparatus, maintenance
  becomes the policy that keeps the most open, with no bonus for protection
  ([model C.10](#c-dependence)). It was run at one cadence.
- **The structure of harm (M2).** Irreversible loss of hardware that has
  substitutes did not lead the winner to preserve it, at every cadence tested,
  while raw hazard moved policy little ([model C.11](#c-harm-sweep)). The
  hazard comparison ran at one cadence.
- **Horizon (M1).** Lengthening the horizon increased servicing in 17 of 20
  and 22 of 24 adjacent steps, and decreased it in none; at the lower hazard
  the winners never moved away from service at any cadence
  ([model C.11](#c-harm-sweep); [model C.12](#c-reserve)).
- **Reported, not yet verified.** In 40 of 40 long-run comparisons, the
  intact structure beat the one that lost rate, and all 78 productive
  configurations beat their non-conducting arm. The records of both are not
  yet in the test-bed archive.

The pressure here is timing. Raising the irreversible share of the same
damage raised lushness for a while in one model: +1.17 bits at an
intermediate horizon, before falling 2.46 bits behind at a much longer one
([model C.8](#c-compounding)). The account predicts the reversal. The
crossover came late, and [chapter 17](#chapter-17) takes it up.

## 31.6 Suffering

**The suffering prediction holds at every point tested, at the level of detail
that governs.**

In a family of models of a valuer facing an obstacle it cannot pass, the valuer
with an aversive drive to escape had narrower futures than the same valuer
without the drive, in all 216 comparisons
([model C.13](#c-register-controls)). At one point it held at every cadence
from 0.5 down to 1/512 of a time unit. Removing the futile drive added 0.42 to
0.54 bits.

That is M1. The comparisons were made at two levels of detail. One reads the
valuer's inner signal; the other merges the signal's states. The first draws
a distinction that physics registers and the second leaves out, so the first
governs. At the merged level, 44 comparisons are unresolved and none reverses.
One limit remains: the family has two base worlds and nine upkeep levels, and
a parameter sweep is pending. [Chapter 19](#chapter-19) states the result with
that limit.

The merged level does reverse a neighbouring result. Paid neutral variation,
a signal that flickers on its own, beats an inactive signal at the level that
reads it, and loses at the merged level, where only its cost remains visible.
That reversal says where the advantage comes from. It does not undo the
ranking.

## 31.7 Constraint and domination

**Constraint is judged by what it does, and blocking narrows the whole while
widening the blocker, at every level tested.**
- **Consequences (M1).** In the first map, from 64 steps onward at every
  cadence and in four environments, steering a subsystem away from a trap
  widened the whole field by 9.5 to 13.2 bits, while blocking its access to
  resources narrowed it by 6.3 to 8.4 ([model C.14](#c-domination)). The
  constrained party's lushness moved with the whole field's in all 20 cases.
- **The self–whole conflict (M1).** In the corrected model, blocking access
  narrowed the whole field by 13.64 bits, widened the blocker's own futures by
  4.85 to 14.06 bits, and narrowed the blocked party's by 14.18 to 25.78. The
  same resolved signs held at every cadence and horizon tested, on two grains
  five times apart, and when the restriction acted on movement only.
- **The converse (M1).** Where the field was conservative, the constraining
  party lost while the constrained party and the whole gained, at every
  cadence.
- **Production against domination (M1, at long horizons).** At 256 and 1,024
  time units, at every cadence, productive hardware gave the dominator more
  than blocking could. At shorter horizons the comparison is unresolved.

Two results press on the account. Funded appropriation widened every party
through turnover, although the flow ran toward the dominator
([model C.16](#c-flow)). And constraint won every finite comparison through
1,024 steps in a model where most constrained laws had a lower rate; the
estimated reversals fall between about 7,200 and 36,000 time units
([model C.15](#c-opportunity), [chapter 21](#chapter-21)).

## 31.8 Choosing inside the structure

**The exact predictions about crossovers check out where they have been
checked, and the best policies are state-dependent.**

The test-bed archive holds one exact check: in a historical steering case
the crossover estimate was 3,256 time units, and the exact profile changes
sign between 3,093 and 3,256 ([model C.15](#c-opportunity)). A wider analysis
reports exact crossovers within one step of the horizon
[Proposition A.3.7](#a-3-7) predicts wherever transients are short, and
ranking by rate agreeing with the full comparison exactly when a structure is
expected to persist past the crossover. Policy searches report that the whole
field's best policy depends on the state and the horizon, and that in one
integrated world the best controller cycled reversibly among its modes,
corresponding to maximum-occupancy agents (Ramírez-Ruiz et al. 2024). The
records of the wider analysis and of the policy searches are not yet in the
archive ([model C.17](#c-policy-search), [chapter 26](#chapter-26)).

## 31.9 What the evidence does not yet show

**Nineteen tests are specified and not built. Four of them bear directly on
whether the thesis survives.**
- **Busy noise at matched activity.** Whether a rate gain reflects enabling
  structure rather than activity alone ([chapter 16](#chapter-16)).
- **Options of equal lushness.** Whether options that score the same can
  differ in ways that matter, and whether the difference reappears at longer
  horizons or finer faithful levels of detail.
- **Plural ecology against confinement.** Whether a plural ecology outproduces
  a dense confined population on the same land. This is the factory-farming
  objection in testable form.
- **Real systems.** Whether organization raises the rate at which futures open
  outside the models ([chapter 33](#chapter-33)).

The others extend what is already supported: precaution under uncertainty,
signals that fire without a hazard, steering by incentive against punishment,
bounded agents, composition across scales, and estimators for realistic
systems. All are listed in Appendix D, section D.4.

## 31.10 What it adds up to

**No completed test has established a counterexample to the central thesis
under the program's criteria. Several results press on it, and the tests most
likely to overturn it are still to come.**

| Tier | Model claims |
|---|---|
| M1, resolved at the finest level tested | 19 |
| M2, resolved at one level so far | 3 |
| M3, unresolved at the finest level tested | 0 |
| Reported, record not yet in the archive | 3 |
| M4, not yet built | 19 tests |

Some rankings were corrected at the finest cadence tested, none of them a
claim of the thesis: on the spatial lattice, 2 of 14 laws favoured the seed at
the coarsest cadence and not at the finest, and a reserve policy's lead over
constant servicing reversed at the finest cadence. A finer cadence favours the
busier option, so these corrections stay provisional until each model's
finest faithful cadence is derived from physics ([chapter 7](#chapter-7)).

The pressure is real, even where it is not a counterexample.
- Permanent loss raised lushness for a while before the intact structure
  overtook it ([section 31.5](#315-harm-and-protection)).
- Funded appropriation widened every party, although the flow ran toward the
  dominator ([section 31.7](#317-constraint-and-domination)).
- Constraint won every finite comparison through 1,024 steps in a model where
  most constrained laws had a lower rate.

The account predicts the first and third reversals, and they came late. The
second needs [chapter 17](#chapter-17)'s treatment of material injury and
narrowing.

Three things follow.
- **What is strong.** Where organization, information, suffering and
  constraint have been tested, the results hold at the finest level of detail
  tested, in families of laws rather than single cases.
- **What is thinner.** The protection results rest on one level of detail so
  far, and three reported results await their records.
- **What is missing.** The tests most likely to overturn the thesis have not
  been run.

So the evidence supports continuing the program, and it does not yet decide
the thesis. A survey that found no counterexample among the tests already run
says less than one that included the hardest tests. Every model result holds
within its model. The scope sentences in Appendix D say how far each bears.

## 31.11 What this chapter does not claim

- **That the thesis is shown.** The results reported here do not yet
  establish the central thesis, least of all beyond the models in which they
  were obtained.
- **That the finest level tested is the finest faithful level.** It governs
  until each model's finest faithful level is derived from physics.
- **That diagnostics count.** Results from models that fail one of chapter
  12's five conditions are kept in Appendix H, on either side.
