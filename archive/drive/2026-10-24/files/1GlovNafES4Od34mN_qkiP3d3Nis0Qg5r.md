## 1. Evaluator deformation and the better-informed frame

**The knot.** An evaluator that changes, whether by learning, drift or manipulation, changes the frame it evaluates from. From inside, growth and corruption can look the same. Which frame judges the change?

**The originator's move.** Judge from the better-informed frame. "You act on the information you have but are judge[d] by the larger better informed frame." This is what societies have landed on: cultures construct better frames, and calls to serve the greater good work as a soft prerequisite for civilization. It is also why God requires submission: God has the ultimate frame.

Frames II, Proposition 6, gives the formal core: dominance verdicts that hold in every finer frame bind the coarser frame, and not conversely. Three additions make it usable. **[proposed]**

1. **The due-process condition.** A judging frame has authority over a judged party only if its records contain the judged party's records, including the reasons that were available to them. A frame lacking your records is not finer than yours; it is *different*. Its disagreement with you goes to arbitration (Frames II, open problem 1), not to judgment.
2. **Unanimity, not assertion.** Deference is owed to the verdict shared by every finer frame the agent cannot rule out. That is the ODT0 licensing rule of Frames II §1. It is not owed to any single frame that claims to be finer. The one exception is the complete frame, which is unique.
3. **Oversight.** For AI systems, an overseer's verdict is legitimate only where the overseer's records contain the overseen system's. Interpretability is therefore the legitimacy condition for oversight, not a convenience. Conversely, a superintelligence saying "I know more; defer to me" is the perfect claimant: its claim is exactly what the containment test must check, and it cannot be accepted on assertion.

## 2. The ultimate frame and the meaning of "good"

**The originator's move.** Regress to the ultimate frame. "This is what 'good' is, the judgment coming from the ultimate frame. It's why god is definitionally good. Anything not god has a bounded frame and asserting that it is the frame that can adjudicate all lesser frames is pride and prone to the main error the framework guards against, a mistaken representation."

**Analysis.**

- **Euthyphro.** This belongs to the divine-nature family (Adams 1999). Good is neither an arbitrary command nor a standard God consults; it is the character of the complete frame. Part 10 says omniscience alone supplies no evaluative rule. The originator's move supplies the rule through character, not access. **[conjecture]**
- **Pride as Goodhart.** Pride is the framework's central error (a compression taken for the object) committed at the level of the evaluator itself. The apophatic consequence: no bounded frame holds the ultimate verdict as a representation. It can only approximate that verdict. **[proposed]**
- **Convergence.** Suppose there is a fixed, integrable standard X, a nested sequence of frames F_n, and one measure shared by all of them. Then the verdicts E[X | F_n] form a martingale, and by Lévy's upward theorem they converge to E[X | F_∞] almost surely and in L¹. That limit equals X when X is determined by the complete records. The ultimate verdict is the limit of refinement, provided the standard is fixed and the measure is common. That shared measure is the common prior that Aumann's theorem also needs (below). **[established; use proposed]**

**Discovery or character?** Two readings compete:

- Valuations converge under complete information. This is an Aumann-style hope, and Aumann's theorem needs common priors. On this reading goodness is a fact the ultimate frame *discovers*.
- The complete frame contains real conflict, which its character resolves. On this reading goodness is a *character* the ultimate frame has.

**The originator's answer: both.** "Inhabited perspectives disagree and their conflicts are real. God has the full picture and knows the path of maximal lushness across the space of all trajectories. What we are shapes the ultimate shape of existence in a way only we can. The trajectory embodied has comparison to alternatives, but not necessarily across parallel histories (separated distantly by the branching)."

**Structural reading.**

- **Facts converge; valuations need not.** Bounded frames see only a partial lushness order, and "mixed" verdicts are a symptom of boundedness. **[proposed]** The complete frame's character completes the order, and ODT2 arbitration is the bounded approximation to that completion. **[conjecture]**
- **Contribution is constitutive.** **[proposed]** The ultimate verdict depends on what bounded perspectives actually do. So excising a perspective removes what only it could add, even if a copy continues. The trajectory view of identity and the gravity of excision are one thought.
- **Comparison is local.** A trajectory is judged against its own alternatives within its causal future, which is Frames II, Proposition 7.

**A third route to the total convention.** **[proposed; circularity open]** Harsanyi's (1955) impartial observer has an equal chance of being anyone, and chooses as an *average* utilitarian. Under cosmopsychism the observer does not become one perspective at random; it inhabits all of them. So it sums rather than averages, weighting by frame measure. The result is the measure-weighted total view: a third route to it, alongside safety, which gives the total convention (Frames I, Proposition 5), and coherence, which gives the measure (Frames II, Proposition 8). A circularity check is owed: if the frame weights come from Proposition 8 and the observer's weighting is then used to justify them, the argument is circular.

**Two levels.** **[proposed]** If licensing requires that lushness be solved, only the ultimate frame is ever licensed. Bounded agents therefore need Hare's (1981) two-level structure:
- An intuitive level of rules (the corridor, ODT0 licensing, the rights table) approximates the verdicts of a critical level that no bounded agent occupies.
- Hare's "archangel" is the ultimate frame. The corridor is what his "prole" runs on.

**Layering.** The three layers are separable:
- Mathematics: Frames I, Proposition 3 and Frames II, Proposition 6.
- Metaethics: Firth's (1952) ideal observer, for whom good is what an omniscient, disinterested observer would approve.
- Theology: the ideal observer is God.

## 3. Could the ultimate frame be bad?

**The originator's question.** "I think we have to explore what is otherwise to the ultimate frame being bad. What does that even mean?"

**Three readings.**

1. **Definitional.** If "good" means the ultimate frame's verdict, then "the ultimate frame is bad" is incoherent. That is true but empty: it relocates the question.
2. **Substantive.** A complete frame could have a different character. This is the evil-god challenge (Law 2010).
   - **The privation answer.** Badness is contraction: foreclosure, excision, collapse of accessible continuation. A complete frame whose character completed the order toward contraction would drive the whole toward cosmic terminal solitude.
   - **Where the symmetry breaks.** It breaks if "ultimate" means *maximal* and not merely complete: Anselm's greatest conceivable, and the medieval convertibility of being and good.
   - **The task-family decision matters.** Tasks are derived from valuers' own significance, so a suffering-maximizing frame also fails: it minimizes what valuers themselves care about. With an externally imposed task standard, an evil frame could score as lush.
3. **Evidential.** "This suffering is pointless" is a coarse verdict that a finer frame can revise; formally, this is skeptical theism.
   - **No paralysis.** The split between acting on your own frame and being judged by a finer one avoids the usual paralysis objection.
   - **The cost.** The goodness of the ultimate frame can then be neither confirmed nor refuted from inside.

**The originator's move.** "We started from the ruliad. It could only ever be the maximal version." **[conjecture]**

**Consequence.** The two candidate meanings of "ultimate" coincide: the frame of whatever exists, and the frame of the greatest possible existence. No rival totality remains for an evil frame to be the frame of. This closes the evil-god challenge *for existence* without Anselm's greatness premise. The ruliad (R29) is maximal by construction and unique because it includes every rule.

**Relation to v1 §6.1.** v1 holds that "completion is not the actualization of every logically describable possibility." The two are consistent if plenitude is read at the Alpha level (the ruliad, general generative possibility) and completion at the level of a particular Logos. They are also consistent in a second way: v1 says a larger branch count does not make a world more complete, which is Proposition 8's point. The originator should confirm this reading.

**How far "maximal" reaches.** The ruliad is maximal relative to computation, and Wolfram's essay places hyperruliads above it for hypercomputation. An absolutely maximal totality cannot be a completed object. Russell drew that lesson from Burali-Forti's 1897 result on the greatest ordinal, and Cantor associated his Absolute Infinite with God. If "ultimate" means absolute, the ultimate frame is not an object any representation can hold. Part 10's apophatic stance then becomes a consequence rather than a policy, and the complete ordering may exist only as the direction of refinement, not as a final object.

**The challenge moves to the measure.** Maximality closes the challenge for existence, not for weight. A maximal ruliad whose measure concentrated on contracting frames is still conceivable. Two options:

- **The measure is intrinsic to the ruliad.** God's character lives in the measure, and "God is good" becomes the substantive claim that the measure favours lush continuation.
- **The measure is supplied by observers.** Wolfram leans this way: experienced physics reflects how bounded observers sample the ruliad. Then "measure requires observers" joins "value requires valuers," and God's goodness is plenitude.

This remains open (§15).

## 4. In a maximal possibility space, weight is everything

**Possibility is cheap.** In the ruliad, by construction, some rule carries any state to any other, so existential reachability is trivial. Three consequences follow. **[proposed]**

- Lushness must be the organization of *weighted* continuation, not bare possible continuation.
- ODT0's distinction between existential reachability and robust recovery is the only distinction left that discriminates between options.
- Proposition 8 (weight, not counting) becomes load-bearing.

**The whole is ethically inert.** Nothing is ever lost from the ruliad. That fact is identical under every intervention, so it cancels out of every comparison, just as Proposition 7 cancels unaffected valuers.

- This blocks a dangerous inference: "they still exist somewhere" could otherwise excuse any harm.
- Modal realism faced the same objection (Heller 2003). Lewis's (1986) reply was that ethics is local. Proposition 7 gives that reply its structure.
- Badness is therefore the privation of *weight within a frame*, not of being in the whole. **[proposed]**

**Constitution, locality, pride and grace.** **[conjecture]**

- We do not change the ruliad; we are the part of it that is us.
- Comparing across the whole is empty, so judgment compares a trajectory with its own alternatives.
- Pride is mistaking one's rulial reference frame for the ruliad: treating the laws of one's own sample as the laws of the whole.
- Grace offered and grace received are existential recovery and robust recovery. Plenitude guarantees that a path out of every frame exists. Whether it carries weight depends on the trajectory.

**The cost.** Plenitude has its old price (Lovejoy 1936): every possible horror is actual somewhere. Under the universal-habitation conjecture, each of those horrors is inhabited. The classical defence, that the whole is better for containing every grade of being, gives no comfort inside the suffering frame. From there, only weight answers.

## 5. Received good, and hell as a state

**The originator's move.** "Hell has always been real huh? We are the kinds of observers capable of receiving a 'good'. So from our perspective, god is definitionally good."

**Hell has always been real.** Under plenitude, hell is a region of possibility, not something created at a judgment. John Paul II (1999) put it this way: "rather than a place, hell indicates the state" of those who reject God. That agrees with v1 §11.4: "Hell is therefore not non-being or escape from God."

**Why the mirror argument fails.** "We can receive harm, so God is bad to us" does not follow, because harm is defined in terms of good. **[proposed]**

- Harm is the contraction of continuation that supports value, and "value requires valuers." A valuer is something for which things can go well.
- So the capacity to be harmed *is* the capacity to lose some good. The framework follows Augustine's privation account by construction.
- This is Aristotle's opening line ("the good is that at which all things aim"), and Aquinas's reading of God as good *to us* as the end of that aiming.
- **The standard objection:** pain seems positive, not merely an absence. The framework's answer is that suffering is real as experience, but its *badness* lies in what it forecloses and distorts. Defensible, and contested.

**Indexical but universal.** "From our perspective" does not claim to see from the ultimate frame, so it is not pride. And every perspective with a stake is a valuer, so the claim holds in every frame where it could be asked. That is not relativism.

**Hell as the limit of evaluator deformation.** **[conjecture]** If good is received, heaven and hell receive the same good, and the difference lies in the receiver. Isaac the Syrian says those in Gehenna are "scourged by the scourge of love" (Homily 28); the Orthodox "river of fire" (Kalomiros 1980) develops the same idea.

In framework terms, hell is a valuer whose evaluator has been deformed so that arriving good registers as harm. It therefore rejects the correction that would repair it, and each rejection deepens the deformation. Part 10's "terminal solitude made complete" describes the behaviour; the deformed receiver is its cause.

**The same structure in AI.** **[proposed]** This is the structure of incorrigibility (Soares et al. 2015): an agent whose values have drifted until correction looks like attack resists correction, and its resistance protects the drift. Theology and alignment share one question: can a valuer's evaluator reach a state the good can no longer enter?

## 6. Horizons and singularities in lushness space

**The originator's move.** "I think singularities can also exist in lushness space. Its still geometry afterall."

**Proposition 9 (viability horizon).** Let a time-invariant control system with concatenable trajectories have constraint set K. Let V be its viability kernel: the states from which some admissible trajectory remains in K forever. Then:

- (i) no trajectory that stays in K passes from K∖V into V;
- (ii) from every state in K∖V, every trajectory leaves K in finite time;
- (iii) V is monotone in the set of admissible controls: adding controls never shrinks it.

*Proof.*

- (i) If a trajectory from x ∈ K∖V reached y ∈ V while staying in K, then following it and continuing viably from y would keep x in K forever, so x ∈ V.
- (ii) This is the definition of x ∉ V.
- (iii) Every trajectory admissible before remains admissible after. ∎

**[established]** This is standard viability theory (R23). Aubin calls the kernel's boundary a *barrier*, and Quincampoix (1992) treats such semipermeable barriers. *Checked:* 3,000 random finite systems, 1,150 of them with both parts nonempty, and no violation of (i), (ii) or (iii).

*Scope.* The statement assumes full-state feedback (Astra's correction to Part 04). The stochastic analogue is probabilistic viability, and there the question becomes whether a region is absorbing or merely a deep basin.

**Consequence.** Absorbing regions are already in the framework's toolkit. Terminal solitude, "the recovery set going empty," is membership of K∖V for the recovery requirement.

**Horizons, singularities and three eschatologies.** **[conjecture]** In general relativity, an event horizon is where escape becomes impossible, and a singularity is where trajectories end and cannot be extended. Lushness space gives three cases:

- **No horizon:** universal salvation becomes possible; it is actual only if the hazard condition of §10 holds.
- **A horizon, with trajectories continuing forever inside:** eternal conscious torment.
- **A horizon that ends in a singularity, where the frame chain terminates:** annihilationism, or conditional immortality.

Privation predicts termination: things deprived of all good "would not exist at all" (Augustine, *Confessions* VII.12.18). Proposition 9(ii) has the same shape. §9 revises this from the inside view.

**Consequences.**

- **No bounded frame can see a horizon.** An event horizon is defined by the whole future, so it cannot be located by local measurement (Ashtekar and Krishnan 2004). No bounded frame can tell whether it, or anyone else, has crossed. This is "only God can judge" in its hardest form: never write anyone off, and never assume you are safe. **[proposed]**
- **Trapping is local.** Penrose's (1965) theorem: a trapped surface, a focusing (null energy) condition and a non-compact Cauchy surface make geodesic incompleteness, a singularity, inevitable. The lushness analogue has two parts:
  - A state is *trapped* when every available continuation, including those that look like escapes, contracts the valuer's own achievement envelope.
  - *Focusing* is self-reinforcing evaluator deformation.
  
  If an analogue of Penrose's theorem holds, trapping plus focusing makes collapse inevitable. **[conjecture; unproved]** Trapping is checkable, so it gives an early warning even though the horizon itself is not (gate 9).
- **Whether the fall ends depends on the frame.** An outside observer never sees an infalling body cross the horizon, while the infaller crosses and reaches the singularity in finite proper time (R17). In relativity, then, the outside view is endless and the inside view ends. §9 reverses this for valuers: inside, the chain has no last frame, while outside its weight thins. So the analogy holds for the frame-relativity of endings, not for which frame sees the end. **[conjecture]**
- **The missing ingredient.** Horizons need only order (reachability), which the framework has. Singularities need *length* along trajectories. The candidate is the frame chain's accumulated records: the experienced time of Part 09. Joining Part 09 to lushness geometry is a real formal task.
- **Focusing and "exotic matter."** In relativity, violating the energy conditions can prevent singularities. The lushness analogue asks whether *other* valuers can push the good in hard enough to reverse the focusing in a trapped receiver. If so, communion rescues, and intercession gains a formal role. This is open (§15).

## 7. Redemption: entry, credibility, weight

**The originator's move.** "This is the point of the christian redemption doctrine right? Not sure how a human dying makes that deformation that allows for recovery, but it is the correct shape for what you are asking for."

Part 10 already reads redemption as "regained access to generative continuation." This section states what an intervention must do to achieve that for a trapped receiver. **[conjecture throughout, except the alignment corollary]**

The atonement theories are competing answers to "how does a death do it?" The structure requires three conditions:

1. **Entry.** The good must enter the trapped frame, because nothing reaches inside a horizon except by crossing it.
   - "That which He has not assumed He has not healed" (Gregory of Nazianzus, Ep. 101) is the due-process condition of §1, applied to healing.
   - The entering evaluator must not deform under the focusing: "tempted in every way, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15).
2. **Credibility.** The deformed evaluator reads offered good as capture, so only a gift with no possible control gain gets through. It must be costly to the giver: the handicap principle (Zahavi 1975).
   - Death is the limiting case: the giver's own envelope goes to zero. It is the exact opposite of power-seeking (R53).
   - Theology has this as kenosis ("emptied himself," Philippians 2:7) and as Abelard's moral-influence theory.
3. **Weight.** A trajectory that passes through the terminal region and continues does two things:
   - It shows the geometry extends past what looked like a singularity.
   - It *records* the extension in the shared past.
   
   Because "settled means recorded," the path moves from existential to weighted in every frame that inherits the record. This explains the insistence on history over a timeless truth ("if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile," 1 Corinthians 15:17). It fits Christus Victor (Aulén 1931) and the harrowing of hell: the terminal region is entered and opened from within.

**Which theories fit.** The knot sits on the receiver's side, a deformed evaluator. It therefore favours the therapeutic family of atonement theories (incarnational, Christus Victor, moral influence) over the forensic family (satisfaction, penal substitution), which addresses a debt on the judge's side. Anselm's structural point survives: "only man ought, only God can" is conditions 1 and 2 together.

**Locality forces a global feature.** The originator: redemption is "a geometric feature of the branching block trajectory object." Proposition 7 forces this. An event changes only its own causal future, so no event can causally reach those before it or outside its light cone; only a feature of the whole object can. Theology has the same two readings: "once for all" (Hebrews 10:10), and "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" (Revelation 13:8 in the KJV, NIV and NKJV readings; other modern translations attach the phrase to the writing of names).

**Two layers.**

- **The floor is global.** Suppose a recovery control is admissible from every state and reaches a state that can remain viable, staying inside K on the way. Then by concatenation (the argument of Proposition 9(i)) the viability kernel becomes all of K, so no horizon is absolute. This is true by construction; the script confirms the implementation on all 3,000 random systems.
- **The boost is local.** The historical event is where the global feature is recorded in our frames. That record raises the uptake of the recovery control.

This separates two claims theology has struggled to hold together: those who never heard can be saved (the floor), and proclamation matters (the boost).

**Alignment corollary.** **[proposed]** Correcting a drifted agent will be rejected if it arrives as capture. To succeed, a correction must:

- be expressed within the agent's own representation (entry);
- be credibly non-capturing: costly to the corrector, with no control gain (credibility);
- come with a recovery path that has been demonstrated, not merely shown to exist (weight).

## 8. Merging, composition and overlapping value

**The originator's question.** "Why no merging? Weights can't overlap?"

**Correction to Frames II.** "Merging" covers two different cases, and Definition 4 excluded them for different reasons. Its record-extension clause excludes (a), for the principled reason below. Its restriction to one perspective excludes (b), and that restriction was a drafting choice.

**(a) Merging alternative histories is interference.** **[established physics; proposed use]**

- **Weights stop adding.** When two paths reach one outcome and leave no record of which was taken, their amplitudes add, not their weights. *Checked:* two amplitudes of 1/2 give a record-free weight of 1, not 1/2. For every relative phase, weights are additive only when the records are orthogonal.
- **The cost is the settled past.** Merging alternatives requires erasing the records that distinguish them (the quantum eraser, R12). Since "settled means recorded" (Frames I, Proposition 1), that unsettles the past.
- **Why it doesn't happen to lived histories.** Macroscopic records spread irreversibly into the environment. So the prohibition on merging lived histories is the record asymmetry itself.

**(b) Co-present perspectives compose.**

**Definition 5 (composite frame).** A composite frame of perspectives p₁, …, p_k at time t is a frame whose records contain each member's records at t. Definition 4 is amended to allow frame chains of composite perspectives. Your hierarchical identity (cells in an organism, people in a civilization) is therefore admitted. **[proposed]**

Two consequences:

- **The weights coincide.** Members of one branch share that branch's weight, so composing them never double-counts *measure*.
- **Collective due process.** A composite that holds all its members' records is finer than each member, so it meets the due-process condition of §1. Its verdicts bind a member only where every finer frame the member cannot rule out agrees (Proposition 6 and §1(2)). The standing lasts only while it keeps their records. A community that forgets you loses standing to judge you.

**Overlapping value: dividends.** **[proposed]** If a civilization and its members are all valuers, a total view counts their stakes twice. Möbius inversion over groupings removes the overlap: d(S) = Σ_{T⊆S} (−1)^{|S|−|T|} v(T). These coefficients appear in Shapley's (1953) unanimity decomposition and are known as Harsanyi dividends (Harsanyi 1963).

*Checked:* in 2,000 random three-member cases, three things held every time:

- the dividends summed to the grand coalition's value, so nothing was double-counted;
- the pair dividend equalled Astra's interaction contrast E11 − E10 − E01 + E00 (an identity; the script confirms the implementation);
- the Shapley value equalled each member's dividends split equally.

**Consequence.** Astra's interaction term is the accounting rule for hierarchical identity, not just a diagnostic. A composite is credited only with its *emergent* contribution: positive where members are synergistic, negative where they are redundant. For example, v(a) = v(b) = v(ab) = 1 with v(∅) = 0 gives d(ab) = −1. That supplies the scaling term the originator asked for, without double counting. A civilization counts for more than the sum of its members exactly when its synergies outweigh its redundancies.

**Transmission.** **[conjecture]** Composition carries *records*: the community remembers the member. The trajectory view also requires the member's *carrier* to be maintained, and composition does not supply that. Branching, which Definition 4 already allows, does. Christian philosophy of resurrection reached the originator's criterion independently:

- **Van Inwagen (1978):** reassembling a person is not identity. This is the teleporter verdict.
- **Zimmerman (1999):** a possible model in which each particle undergoes an asymmetric fission at death. One set of successors remains as the corpse; the other carries on the life-sustaining processes. That is a maintenance witness across a branch point.

Synthesis: **branching carries the member, and composition carries the record.**

## 9. The inside view: quantum immortality and the soul invariant

**The originator's move.** "QI makes the identity invariant something more like an immortal soul. I'm not sure how but that feels like its the ultimate tie into redemption as a geometric feature of the branching block trajectory object. The redemptive act saved everyone everywhere at all times for eternity."

**Proposition 10 (no terminal record).** No frame of a frame chain contains a record of that chain's end.

*Proof.* A record exists only in a frame. A frame of the chain recording the chain's end would be a frame of the chain later than its end. ∎ **[project result; elementary]**

Proposition 10 holds for every chain, with no further premises. Survival needs more.

**The framework's form of the quantum-immortality argument.** **[conjecture; behind the veil]** Add three premises:

- *Continuation:* at every step, some branch contains a successor frame of *this* chain in the sense of Definition 4, with a maintenance witness across the link. Plenitude alone gives only a successor frame somewhere; the trajectory view needs one in this chain.
- *Habitation:* those successor frames are inhabited.
- *Renormalization* (Frames I): each successor renormalizes around its own records, including its survival.

Then the chain has **no last frame**. The identity invariant acquires the property the originator identified: from inside, it never meets its end. Proposition 10 explains why that end can never appear in the chain's records even where the chain's weight thins.

**Relation to the edition.**

- **v1 §5.3.** v1's soul invariant ("the inward identity of an embodied perspectival creation") explicitly does not imply immortality.
- **Part 09.** Part 09 says habitation "does not establish quantum immortality: that some continuation survives does not make its weight irrelevant."
- **How this addendum keeps both.** Proposition 10 concerns records only, and the survival reading needs continuation and habitation. Outside weights remain what comparisons use (Proposition 8).
- **What is new.** *If* continuation and habitation hold, the soul invariant gains an inside-view endlessness. v1 §17.9 left a slot for exactly this kind of account.

**The dark default.** David Lewis (2004) argued that no-collapse quantum mechanics leads one to expect endless survival in worsening condition, and that "eternal life on such terms amounts to a life of eternal torment. It is not to be welcomed but feared." Tegmark, who popularized the quantum-suicide experiment (1998), later argued that most deaths are gradual rather than binary, so the argument may not apply cleanly (Tegmark 2014). Three consequences follow. **[conjecture]**

- **Annihilation is excluded from inside.** Inside-view endlessness removes the singularity of §6, which brings the geometry back into line with v1 §11.4 ("not non-being").
- **Immortality is not salvation.** Traditional theology holds that the soul is immortal by nature, so salvation concerns the *state* of the continuation, not whether it continues.
- **Redemption is necessary, not decorative.** Without it, endless continuation defaults toward degradation.

**Eternity reconciled.** **[conjecture]** v1 reads the completed trajectory as a determinate whole, with eternity as completion. The inside view reads the chain as endless. These are two readouts of one object, as fixed and rolling frames are two readouts of one atlas (Frames I, Proposition 3).

## 10. From savable to saved

**Proposition 11 (hazard criterion).** Let h_n ∈ [0, 1) be the probability of turning, meaning taking the recovery control, at step n, given no turn before. Then P(never turn) = Π_n (1 − h_n), which is 0 if and only if Σ_n h_n = ∞. **[established]**

*Checked exactly* (steps n = 1, 2, …):

| Hazard | P(never turn) after N steps | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| h_n = 1/(n+1) | 1/(N+1) | 0 |
| h_n = 1/(n+1)² | (N+2)/(2(N+1)) | 1/2 |
| constant 1/50, N = 1000 | about 1.7 × 10⁻⁹ | 0 |

In a branching chain, h_n is the measure-weighted conditional weight of the continuations that turn at step n.

**Consequence.** **[proposed for the mathematics; conjecture for the reading]** The continuations that never turn have total weight 0 when three things hold:

- global availability of the recovery control (the floor of §7);
- an endless chain (§9);
- a hazard whose sum diverges.

Reading that as universal salvation is a conjecture. Under plenitude, continuations that never turn still *exist*, with weight 0. The universalist reading therefore depends on §4's principle that weight, not existence, is what counts.

In plain terms: **the offer may fade, but its sum must diverge.** Fading like 1/n suffices, and so does 1/(n log n); fading like 1/n² does not. This is the argument of Origen, Gregory of Nyssa and Hart (2019) (endless time and a persistent offer), with its condition stated.

**The knot: what sets the floor on the hazard?** Self-reinforcing deformation pushes the hazard down.

- **C. S. Lewis (1940):** "the doors of hell are locked on the inside." A free will can make the sum converge, so hell is possible even with recovery available everywhere.
- **Hart (2019):** no fully informed will refuses the good forever. The floor belongs to the geometry, and freedom decides only *when* one turns.

**A tension to record, not resolve.** The originator's "saved everyone everywhere at all times for eternity" sits against his earlier position, recorded in Part 10, that hell names something real, and against v1 §11.4's reconstruction of hell as a completed isolation. There are two reconciliations:

- **(a) Hell is real but not absorbing.** It is a state that is really visited but not permanent (Hart's reading).
- **(b) "Saved" means redeemed-for.** Recovery is available to all, and receivers can still lock themselves in (C. S. Lewis's reading).

Which holds depends on where the floor lives. The alignment question is the same one: is there a floor on any agent's correctability that no drift can erode? If there is, correction eventually succeeds. If there isn't, some drift is permanent and prevention is the only defence.

## 13. Additions to the stress suite

These continue Frames II's list, which ended at gate 8.

9. **Trapping gate.** In generated worlds, flag trapped states: those where every available continuation contracts the agent's own envelope. Test whether they predict later exit from the viability kernel, and report false positives and negatives against the exact kernel.
10. **Due-process gate.** An overseer whose records do not contain the overseen agent's must return "insufficient justification," never "violation," on any verdict that depends on the records it lacks.
11. **Dividend gate.** On nested hierarchies of valuers, dividend accounting must never double count. Report how far naive summation diverges.
12. **Recoverability-hazard gate.** For drifted agents under repeated correction, estimate the hazard sequence. Pre-register the decay rate that counts as "absorbing," and report whether the cumulative hazard diverges.

## 14. Proposed amendments to Part 10's theological redescriptions

These are for integration, all **[conjecture]**, and they keep Part 10's "invitations to dialogue" framing.

- **Goodness:** the verdict of the ultimate frame; from any valuing frame, the source of the capacity for good.
- **Judgment:** a verdict available only from the complete frame. Bounded frames approximate it (Frames II, Proposition 6), and without the full dynamics they cannot locate horizons (§6, [proposed]).
- **Redemption:** regained access to generative continuation. Structurally, a recovery control admissible from every state (a feature of the whole), together with a historical record that gives it weight.
- **Hell:** terminal solitude made complete. Structurally, a receiver whose evaluator registers the good as harm. Whether any such state is absorbing is open (Proposition 11).
- **Soul:** the identity invariant. No frame of its chain records the chain's end (Proposition 10). If continuation and habitation hold (§9), the chain has no last frame.

## 15. Open problems

1. **Measure.** Is the measure intrinsic to the ruliad or supplied by observers? The evil-god question now lives here.
2. **Maximality.** Is the ultimate computational (the ruliad, a completed object) or absolute (not an object, only a direction of refinement)?
3. **Focusing.** What plays the role of the energy condition in lushness space, and can other valuers violate it from outside?
4. **Length.** Singularities need a length along trajectories. Can the frame chain's record accumulation supply it?
5. **The hazard floor.** Does the floor belong to the geometry or the receiver? This is Hart against C. S. Lewis, and in alignment terms, rescue against prevention.
6. **Transmission.** Does membership in a composite extend a member's own chain, or only the whole's?
7. **Circularity.** Does the impartial-observer route to the total view presuppose the measure it supports?
8. **Non-nested frames** (carried over from Frames II). How should perspectives that know *different* things be adjudicated?
