What has the program shown, and how strongly? This chapter answers by status:
what is derived, what holds in models and how far, what is observed in the
world, and what is still open. Every claim here has a row in
[Appendix D](#appendix-d), and nothing here claims more than its row allows.
[Chapter 32](#chapter-32) then states the tests that could still overturn the
thesis.

## 31.1 How to read the evidence

**Every claim has a status, and every model result has a tier that says how
far its ranking holds.**

A claim is a definition, a theorem, a model result, an empirical
correspondence or a thesis ([chapter 12](#chapter-12)). The manuscript assumes
no normative premise. For model results, a single status is not enough.
[Chapter 12](#chapter-12) set the rule: a ranking holds only when it is
resolved, with the same sign, at every admissible level of detail. Results
meet that rule to different degrees, so each carries a tier.
- **M1:** resolved, with the same sign, at every level of detail tested.
- **M2:** resolved at one level of detail, or a few, and not yet shown at the
  others.
- **M3:** unresolved, or the sign changes between admissible levels.
- **M4:** not yet built.

One limit applies to M1 for now. For most models, the levels of detail tested
are recorded, usually three cadences, but an admissible range has not been
declared. Until [Appendix C](#appendix-c) declares one for each model, M1
means every level tested, not every admissible level. Where the sources do not
record the levels tested at all, the tier is provisional.

Three kinds of result stand outside the tiers because they make no claim on
the principle: operating ranges, which map how a mechanism behaves;
pressure, which the manuscript has to answer; and diagnostics, which fail one
of chapter 12's five conditions and are kept apart in
[Appendix H](#appendix-h).

## 31.2 What is derived

**The measure, its readings over time, the subject of the comparison with no
index, and the elementary facts about information and harm are theorems.**

[Appendix A](#appendix-a) contains seventy-six numbered results, each with one
status. Fifteen are established in the literature, forty-three are proved
there, two rest on named physical premises, one is a decision model with
stipulated payoffs, and fifteen are definitions. None rests on a normative
premise.

The results the argument leans on most:
- **The measure is unique.** Four requirements on any count of distinct
  futures leave one measure ([Theorem A.2.3](#a-2-3)).
- **Time has an exact structure.** Starting points under one shared finite law
  differ only by a head start ([Theorem A.3.5](#a-3-5)), and over everything
  that follows, rate decides first and head start second
  ([Corollary A.3.9](#a-3-9)).
- **Persistence has an exact price.** Lasting is worth what it keeps open
  beyond its replacement ([Proposition A.4.8](#a-4-8)); an early end costs most
  when the world never rebuilds what was lost ([Proposition A.4.9](#a-4-9)); a
  fragile gain must clear a floor set by its expected life
  ([Corollary A.4.6](#a-4-6)).
- **Parts and the whole differ by one term** ([Proposition A.7.6](#a-7-6)), and
  the comparison that singles out no one has the whole field as its subject
  ([Corollary A.7.9](#a-7-9)).
- **Information pays only through action** ([Corollary A.8.4](#a-8-4)), and the
  advantage of informed action splits exactly into three parts
  ([Proposition A.8.3](#a-8-3)).
- **The largest narrowing leaves a determined future**
  ([Proposition A.9.4](#a-9-4)).

None of this shows the central thesis. The theorems fix what the measure
ranks. Whether that ranking is the one our moral judgements track is tested
case by case, and section A.15 lists what the formal core does not contain.

## 31.3 Organization

**In the models, organization raises the rate at which futures open, at every
level of detail tested. Whether it outproduces disorder doing as much is not
yet tested.**

Four results hold at M1.
- **A locked source.** A law under which organization can release a locked
  energy source beats the same law with that path removed, by 0.260, 0.229
  and 0.197 bits per unit time at three cadences
  ([model](#c-locked-source)). Within the capable law, starting points tie in
  rate, as the offset theorem requires.
- **An enabling loop.** A loop that captures energy and builds apparatus
  retains apparatus near capacity, 30.79 of 32 at the largest, while each
  single ablation retains a quarter or less. Its rate gain grows with
  capacity, from +2.37 to +6.65 bits per unit time ([model](#c-capacity)).
- **Matched work.** At matched accepted work, the enabling relation wins in
  12 of 12 settings at every cadence ([model](#c-matched-work)).
- **Three links.** Three successive paid enabling links compound with retained
  expansion in all 12 shape settings, and the full mechanism beats the unbound
  start, the version with all assistance off and the cut loop at every cadence
  in all 40 settings ([model](#c-compounding)).

Two limits apply.
- **Activity was not matched.** Accepted work was matched; activity was not.
  At fixed numbers of events, the loop loses early, by 1.76 bits at 8 events,
  and wins at 32 and 128. Whether organization outproduces busy noise doing as
  much is the comparison [chapter 16](#chapter-16) tests, and it is not yet
  built.
- **Disorder had no room to spread.** The compounding models count molecules
  without positions, so they lack what makes disorder the default. On a
  spatial lattice, an organized seed beat the full thermal mixture and lost to
  an unbound start, whose free monomers moved faster
  ([model](#c-lattice)). That model's feedback did not sustain assembly, so it
  maps an operating range and makes no claim either way. The spatial model
  with several carriers is next.

Real systems offer correspondences: photosynthesis raising the rate of
free-energy capture, and bacterial populations adapting without a plateau
(Kleidon 2012; Wiser, Ribeck and Lenski 2013). They motivate the hypothesis
that organization raises the rate at which futures open. They do not test it
([chapter 15](#chapter-15)).

## 31.4 Information through action

**Information without action loses, and informed action wins, at every level
of detail tested. The advantage comes from the two together.**
- **Without leverage.** A register that tracks the world but cannot act on it
  lost to one that varied at random in 16 of 16 cases
  ([model](#c-paid-sensing)). This is [Proposition A.8.2](#a-8-2) at work.
- **With leverage and repair.** Where information could be used to act and the
  structure using it rebuilt, informed action had the higher rate in 57 of 57
  comparisons, across 19 laws and three cadences ([model](#c-paid-renewal)).
- **Only together.** Informed action beat random action in all 57; information
  alone and action alone each beat the idle version in none. The whole
  advantage lies in the third part of the split.
- **Time matters.** Informed action won no law at 64 steps, 5 of 19 by 256 and
  13 of 19 by 1,024. In short-lived valuers, living about 7 time units, it
  never repaid its cost. Both are what the robustness floor predicts.

## 31.5 Harm and protection

**The policy that keeps the most open protects channels, not stock, and the
structure of a harm sets policy more than its raw hazard. Two of these results
are not yet shown across levels of detail.**
- **Channels, not stock (M1, provisional).** The winning policy keeps 4.8 to
  9.2 per cent less working apparatus than constant servicing while the channel
  stays open ([model](#c-channels)), and all 78 productive configurations beat
  their non-conducting arm.
- **Dependence (M1, provisional).** When a system physically depends on
  maintenance, maintenance becomes the policy that keeps the most open, with
  no bonus for protection ([model](#c-dependence)).
- **The structure of harm (M2).** Irreversible loss of hardware that has
  substitutes did not lead the winner to preserve it, while raw hazard moved
  policy little ([model](#c-harm-sweep)). A check across cadences is pending.
- **Horizon (M2).** Lengthening the horizon increased servicing in 17 of 20
  and 22 of 24 adjacent steps, and decreased it in none. The check at other
  cadences is pending.
- **Rate-lowering harm (M1, provisional).** In 40 of 40 long-run comparisons,
  the intact structure beats the one that lost rate.

The pressure here is timing. Permanent loss raised lushness for a while in
one model: +1.17 bits at an intermediate horizon, before falling 2.46 bits
behind at a much longer one ([model](#c-compounding)). The account predicts
the reversal. The crossover came late, and [chapter 17](#chapter-17) takes it
up.

## 31.6 Suffering

**The suffering prediction holds at every point tested, at the finest level of
detail. It is not yet shown at coarser levels, where some comparisons remain
unresolved.**

In a family of models of a valuer facing an obstacle it cannot pass, the valuer
with an aversive drive to escape had narrower futures than the same valuer
without the drive, at all 18 primary points ([model](#c-register-controls)).
The drive-free valuer won all 216 comparisons, and removing the futile drive
added 0.42 to 0.54 bits.

That is M2, not M1. At a coarser level of detail, which merges the signal's
states, 44 and 43 cases across the sweep are unresolved. None reverses. Two
things would raise the result to M1: resolving those cases, or showing that
the coarser level is not admissible for this question. A third limit is
breadth: the family has two base worlds and nine upkeep levels, and a
parameter sweep is pending. [Chapter 19](#chapter-19) states the result with
all three limits.

## 31.7 Constraint and domination

**Constraint is judged by what it does, at every level tested. That blocking
narrows the whole while widening the blocker is grain-stable at one cadence.**
- **Consequences (M1).** In the first map, from 64 steps onward at every
  cadence and in four environments, steering a subsystem away from a trap
  widened the whole field by 9.5 to 13.2 bits, while blocking its access to
  resources narrowed it by 6.3 to 8.4 ([model](#c-domination)). The
  constrained party's lushness moved with the whole field's in all 20 cases.
- **The self–whole conflict (M2).** In the corrected model, blocking access
  narrowed the whole field by 13.64 bits, widened the blocker's own futures by
  4.85 to 14.06 bits, and narrowed the blocked party's by 14.18 to 25.78. The
  pattern held at three grains over a fivefold refinement, at one cadence.
  Other cadences are not reported.
- **The converse (M1).** Where the field was conservative, the constraining
  party lost while the constrained party and the whole gained, at every
  cadence.
- **Production against domination (M2).** At one certified point, productive
  hardware gave the dominator more than blocking could.

Two results press on the account. Funded appropriation widened every party
through turnover, although the flow ran toward the dominator. And constraint
won every finite comparison through 1,024 steps in a model where most
constrained laws had a lower rate, so the reversal, if it comes, comes late
([chapter 21](#chapter-21)).

## 31.8 Choosing inside the structure

**The exact predictions about crossovers check out, and the best policies are
state-dependent.**

Where transients are short, exact crossovers fall within one step of the
horizon [Proposition A.3.7](#a-3-7) predicts. Ranking by rate agrees with the full
comparison exactly when a structure is expected to persist past the crossover.
In policy searches, the whole field's best policy depends on the state and the
horizon, and in one integrated world the best controller cycled reversibly
among its modes. That is the first instance in the test bed of the
correspondence with maximum-occupancy agents (Ramírez-Ruiz et al. 2024). It
maps an operating range and is sensitive to one kinetic parameter
([chapter 26](#chapter-26)).

## 31.9 What the evidence does not yet show

**Eighteen tests are specified and not built. Four of them bear directly on
whether the thesis survives.**
- **Busy noise at matched activity.** Whether organization outproduces
  disorder doing as much ([chapter 16](#chapter-16)).
- **Options of equal lushness.** Whether options that score the same can
  differ in ways that matter, and whether the difference reappears at longer
  horizons or finer levels of detail.
- **Plural ecology against confinement.** Whether a plural ecology outproduces
  a dense confined population on the same land. This is the factory-farming
  objection in testable form.
- **Real systems.** Whether organization raises the rate at which futures open
  outside the models ([chapter 33](#chapter-33)).

The others extend what is already supported: precaution under uncertainty,
signals that fire without a hazard, steering by incentive against punishment,
bounded agents, composition across scales, and estimators for realistic
systems. All are listed in Appendix D, section D.4.

## 31.10 What it adds up to

**No claim has gone against the thesis. The strongest support is in
organization, information and the consequences of constraint. The tests most
likely to overturn it are still to come.**

| Tier | Model claims |
|---|---|
| M1, every level tested | 19, of which 9 provisional |
| M2, one level or a few | 5 |
| M3, unresolved or sign-changing | 0 |
| M4, not yet built | 18 tests |

Three things follow.
- **What is strong.** Where organization, information and constraint have been
  tested, the results hold across the levels of detail tested, in families of
  laws rather than single cases.
- **What is thinner.** Two of the four classes in
  [Overview section 6](#overview-6) rest on M2 results: the self–whole conflict
  under domination, and suffering. Both hold where tested. Neither is yet
  shown across levels.
- **What is missing.** The tests most likely to overturn the thesis have not
  been run. A survey that found no failure among the tests already run says
  less than one that included them.

So the evidence supports continuing the program, and it does not yet decide
the thesis. Every model result holds within its model. The scope sentences in
Appendix D say how far each bears.

---

## 31.11 What this chapter does not claim

- **That the thesis is shown.** No model result, and no number of them, shows
  a correspondence that is tested one class at a time.
- **That M1 means every admissible level of detail.** It means every level
  tested, until Appendix C declares the admissible ranges.
- **That diagnostics count.** Results from models that fail one of chapter
  12's five conditions are kept in Appendix H, on either side.
