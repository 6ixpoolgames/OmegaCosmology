PERSPECTIVE AND SITUATED PHYSICAL ACCESS
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

Perspective is one of the central physical objects in the current program. It is intended to describe a bounded, situated organization within the universal physical structure: a local locus of access, records, uncertainty, and continuation. Perspective is not defined by consciousness and is not an external observer's choice of variables. The research target is to derive it from physical locality, interaction, record structure, and quantum conditioning.


1. WHAT PERSPECTIVE MEANS

A universal physical description contains all physically realized structure, but a bounded system occupies only a local position within it. It interacts through specific channels, carries specific records, is entangled with particular environments, and has direct access to only part of the total structure.

Perspective names this situated physical condition.

A Perspective is expected to include some combination of:

- a local physical support or region;
- a boundary or interface through which interactions occur;
- a record structure encoding prior correlations;
- a set of directly accessible observables or local algebras;
- environmental couplings that stabilize some distinctions and suppress others;
- a conditional state or process relative to the local records;
- a structured family of future continuations available from that condition.

The central idea is that the Perspective is in the universe. It is not a God's-eye partition imposed on the universe from outside.


2. PERSPECTIVE IS NOT CONSCIOUSNESS

The physical concept is deliberately broader than conscious subjecthood.

A detector can have a situated interaction boundary and stable record without being conscious. A cell can maintain a local internal state and respond to its environment without possessing humanlike awareness. A distributed control system can have operational access to some signals but not others.

These cases can instantiate perspectival structure in the physical sense.

Consciousness is a later question: whether some physically constituted Perspectives are phenomenally realized, and what additional relation would account for that fact.

Separating the concepts is important because it allows the physical theory of situated access to be tested independently of a theory of experience.


3. RECORD-CONDITIONED QUANTUM SUPPORT

A useful working scaffold begins with a universal or larger-system density operator rho and a physical record structure R_r.

Conditioning on that record can be represented schematically as

    rho^(r) = R_r rho R_r / Tr(R_r rho),

when R_r is an appropriate projector and the idealized assumptions of the expression hold.

More generally, the conditioning could be represented by an instrument, completely positive map, local algebra state, process-tensor update, Bayesian quantum state update, or another physical conditioning operation.

The significance is conceptual:

    Perspective is associated with the conditional physical structure supported by actual local records.

The conditional state does not remove quantum uncertainty. It organizes it.

Some facts are stably recorded. Some quantities remain distributed. Some alternatives remain coherent. Some incompatible observables cannot all be sharply represented at once. The Perspective is therefore better pictured as a coherent organization of uncertainty around stable physical constraints than as one exact classical microstate.


4. STABLE RECORDS

A record is a physical correlation that persists long enough to influence later interaction.

Examples include:

- a detector memory;
- a neural memory trace;
- a molecular configuration preserved by feedback;
- a fossil or written record;
- environmental redundancy encoding a pointer state;
- a computer register;
- a macroscopic measurement apparatus state.

A stable record can constrain which histories are compatible with the present local state and can guide future action or prediction.

For Perspective, records perform several roles:

1. They provide the Perspective's effective past.
2. They stabilize coarse distinctions against immediate quantum interference.
3. They support persistence of local identity and world-model structure.
4. They constrain conditional future continuation.
5. They allow different local systems to coordinate around shared facts.

A theory of Perspective must therefore connect records to the underlying quantum dynamics rather than merely assuming a classical memory register.


5. DECOHERENCE AND SITUATION

Decoherence provides a physical mechanism by which certain alternative structures become approximately autonomous.

Environmental interactions can correlate a system with many external degrees of freedom, making interference between macroscopically distinct alternatives practically inaccessible. Some observables become robust pointer variables, and their values can be redundantly recorded in the environment.

The current picture distinguishes:

    decoherence stabilizes sectors;
    conditioning on records situates a Perspective relative to a sector.

This distinction is especially important in Everettian or no-collapse interpretations. Decoherence explains why branch-like sectors become dynamically independent to high precision. It does not by itself assert that exactly one sector remains real.

A Perspective can therefore be branch-relative or record-relative without adding an external collapse rule to the universal dynamics.


6. THE PERSPECTIVAL PRESENT

The present of a Perspective is provisionally understood as the local physical boundary between accumulated record and conditional continuation.

A compact representation is:

    record-compatible past
        | present support |
    conditional continuation.

The present support can be called a record frontier or present membrane.

It is not necessarily one exact state or one global time slice. It may be a local boundary state, process interface, algebraic region, causal diamond boundary, or other physically defined support.

Pastward of the frontier, correlations have been stabilized in the Perspective's records.

Futureward of the frontier, the universal process supplies conditional continuations that are not yet encoded as that Perspective's records.

This gives a local physical meaning to presentness even in a globally completed or block-like universe.


7. DIRECT AND INDIRECT ACCESS

A Perspective has direct physical access only through its current couplings and support.

Remote past events are not directly re-experienced. Their relevance is mediated by surviving records, traces, correlations, and reconstructed models.

Remote future conditions are not directly present. Their relevance is mediated by conditional dynamics, propagators, process tensors, planning models, or backward representations of future effects.

This suggests three layers of access:

- direct present support;
- record-mediated past access;
- continuation-mediated future access.

The exact boundary among these layers may depend on the physical scale and system, but the distinction helps organize the program without requiring a universal temporal cut.


8. PHYSICAL BOUNDARY

A Perspective requires a non-arbitrary boundary or interface.

In simple models, this may be a subsystem/environment split. In realistic quantum field theory, a local algebra associated with a spacetime region may be more appropriate. In process formalisms, it may be a set of input/output ports. In a biological system, it may involve several nested membranes, sensory channels, control pathways, and environmental couplings.

The boundary need not be perfectly isolated. A Perspective can be open and dynamically constituted.

The relevant questions are:

- Which interactions cross the boundary?
- Which degrees of freedom are internally available?
- Which records persist inside the boundary?
- Which external variables are coupled strongly enough to matter?
- Which conditional futures can be affected from within?
- Which changes to the boundary preserve the same situated organization?

A mature theory should derive the boundary from physical coupling and organization rather than from ordinary-language object labels.


9. NESTED AND OVERLAPPING PERSPECTIVES

Perspectives need not be disjoint.

A cell is embedded in an organism. An organism is embedded in a social and ecological environment. A detector is embedded in a laboratory. Local quantum regions overlap through shared fields and causal relations.

This suggests a nested or partially overlapping structure of physical perspectives.

Several questions follow:

- When does a larger Perspective contain the information of a smaller one?
- When are two Perspectives compatible but non-nested?
- Can one physical process participate in multiple Perspectives?
- How do records synchronize or conflict across boundaries?
- How does coarse-graining change the apparent boundary?

These questions later become important for agency, valuerhood, and standing, but they already arise at the physical level.


10. PERSPECTIVE AND UNCERTAINTY

A Perspective is not simply an ignorance state about an underlying exact classical configuration.

Quantum uncertainty can be intrinsic. If observables A and B fail to commute,

    [A,B] != 0,

then their dispersions satisfy a relation such as

    Delta A Delta B >= (1/2) |<[A,B]>|.

The Perspective must therefore support a structured pattern of sharp and unsharp quantities, compatible and incompatible observables, and record-conditioned uncertainties.

This motivates the description:

    Perspective is a coherent organization of quantum uncertainties around stable physical records and access constraints.

The word coherent here refers to mutual physical consistency, not necessarily quantum phase coherence among all components.


11. PERSPECTIVE AND HISTORY

A present Perspective generally supports many compatible microscopic pasts.

Let H^-(P) denote schematically the set or family of history contributions compatible with the current Perspective. The current records may rule out many histories while failing to select one exact microscopic chain.

This is not necessarily mere epistemic ignorance. Quantum alternatives can remain coherent or can differ only below the resolution stabilized by present records.

A robust effective past can nonetheless emerge because many compatible fine histories share the same coarse record structure.

Thus:

    many microscopic histories
        -> one stable record-compatible effective trajectory.

The Perspective's past is therefore a structured equivalence or compatibility class rather than automatically one exact path.


12. PERSPECTIVE AND FUTURE

The Perspective's future is the conditional continuation of the universal process relative to its current support and records.

This continuation may include:

- ordinary stochastic outcome distributions;
- coherent alternatives;
- decohering branches;
- controlled interventions;
- feedback loops;
- memory-bearing process structure;
- inaccessible possibilities that remain part of the universal process but cannot influence the local system within the declared scope.

A key downstream problem is to determine which details of the present condition must be retained to preserve this continuation. That leads to residual-process representations and predictive sufficiency.

But the Perspective itself is prior to that compression. It is the physical situation from which continuation is defined.


13. CONTROL AND ACTION

Perspective does not require agency, but agency requires perspectival access.

To act, a system must have some local state, some distinction among possible conditions, some coupling to future outcomes, and some physically realizable means of changing continuation.

The physical Perspective therefore supplies the substrate for later concepts such as:

- policy;
- control;
- planning;
- self-maintenance;
- error correction;
- recovery;
- learning.

An agent is not merely a Perspective. But a situated physical organization of access and continuation is a prerequisite for agentive structure.


14. INDIVIDUATION

One of the hardest physical questions is when a local pattern deserves to be treated as one Perspective rather than an arbitrary collection of degrees of freedom.

Candidate indicators include:

- recurrent internal interaction;
- stable causal boundary;
- information bottlenecks;
- record integration;
- feedback and control closure;
- self-maintaining organization;
- persistent conditional independence structure;
- coherent local algebra or subsystem decomposition;
- robust behavior across coarse-graining.

No single criterion is currently privileged across all scales.

The final individuation principle may be dynamical rather than static: a Perspective may be a persistently regenerated local organization, not a fixed material set.


15. RELATIVISTIC FORMULATION

A Perspective's present must be local enough to avoid requiring a preferred universal simultaneity surface.

A relativistically suitable formulation may use:

- proper-time evolution along a world tube;
- causal diamonds;
- local spacetime regions;
- boundary data;
- local operator algebras;
- covariant process networks.

Records and continuation can then be defined relative to a local physical region. Different Perspectives need not share exactly the same present boundary.

The task is to recover the ordinary experience of temporal succession and shared macroscopic facts from compatible local record structures rather than from one cosmically privileged now.


16. WHAT A SUCCESSFUL FORMALIZATION SHOULD DELIVER

A successful Perspective construction should make it possible to derive, for a specified physical system:

1. its physically justified boundary or local support;
2. the records available at that support;
3. the conditional quantum state or process relative to those records;
4. the set of accessible observables or interactions;
5. the local past-compatibility structure;
6. the conditional future process;
7. the transformations that preserve the same Perspective;
8. the conditions under which the Perspective persists, splits, merges, or ceases.

This would convert Perspective from a conceptual placeholder into a physically calculable object.


17. MAIN OPEN QUESTIONS

The current frontier includes:

- deriving physical boundaries without arbitrary subsystem choice;
- formulating record-conditioned support in quantum field theory;
- handling nested and overlapping Perspectives;
- relating decoherence, records, and branch-relative situation;
- expressing Perspective without a preferred foliation;
- distinguishing persistent Perspective from transient local state;
- determining how much process memory belongs to the Perspective itself;
- connecting Perspective to consequence extraction without inserting semantic relevance;
- characterizing the transformation group under which the same Perspective is represented differently.

These questions define the physical individuation problem at the center of the current program.
