# 13 — References

The Omega program is a synthesis. Almost every piece of machinery it uses was
built by someone else, often over decades, for purposes that had nothing to do
with ethics. What the program adds is an organization of that machinery around
one question: how physical organization supports further possibility, and what
follows for value. This chapter acknowledges the work the argument stands on,
says what each field contributes, and then lists every work cited in the
manuscript.

---

## 13.1 What the program owes

**Information theory and probability.** The measure at the centre of the
manuscript is Shannon's entropy (Shannon 1948), and its uniqueness within the
adopted class is the theorem of Faddeev (1956) and Khinchin (1957). The chain
rule, the data-processing inequality and the coding theorems come from the same
tradition (Cover and Thomas 2006; Hamming 1950). The theory of finite Markov
chains supplies the recurrences, mixing bounds and absorption calculations
behind every worked example (Kemeny and Snell 1960; Norris 1997; Levin, Peres
and Wilmer 2009; Williams 1991). Ecology and language modelling reached the
same quantity independently, as the Hill number of order one and as perplexity
(Hill 1973; Jost 2006; Jelinek et al. 1977), and similarity-sensitive diversity
(Leinster and Cobbold 2012) marks the natural extension.

**Physics.** The physical possibility structure is standard physics read as a
whole: unitary quantum mechanics and its universal state (Everett 1957; Wallace
2012), decoherence and the emergence of classical records (Joos et al. 2003;
Zurek 2003, 2009), decoherent histories and their set-selection problem
(Griffiths 1984; Gell-Mann and Hartle 1993; Dowker and Kent 1996), quantum
field theory and its local algebras (Weinberg 1995; Haag 1996; Yngvason 2005),
and the process-tensor description of multitime quantum processes (Pollock et
al. 2018). The bounds on information and its cost come from thermodynamics and
gravity (Landauer 1961; Bennett 1982; Bekenstein 1981; 't Hooft 1993; Susskind
1995; Bousso 2002). The entropy gap that generativity spends, and the clumping
of self-gravitating matter that begins it, are established astrophysics (Egan
and Lineweaver 2010; Lynden-Bell and Wood 1968; Burbidge et al. 1957). The
causal-set program supplied the cleanest form of Alpha (Bombelli et al. 1987;
Rideout and Sorkin 2000), and constructor theory a close neighbour for
possibility and its realization (Deutsch 2013; Deutsch and Marletto 2015).

**Chemistry and the origin of organization.** Autocatalytic sets, chemical
organization theory, self-replicating molecules and dissipative structures
(Kauffman 1986; Hordijk and Steel 2004; Dittrich and Speroni di Fenizio 2007;
von Kiedrowski 1986; Nicolis and Prigogine 1977) are the empirical and
mathematical ground for chapter 3's account of generativity. The program's
reaction probes adapted published lattice-gas chemistry (Boon et al. 1996).

**Evolution, ecology and biology.** The Price equation and multilevel selection
(Price 1970, 1972; Sober and Wilson 1998; Traulsen and Nowak 2006), the
mechanisms of cooperation (Hamilton 1964; Trivers 1971; Axelrod and Hamilton
1981; Nowak 2006), the major transitions (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry 1995) and
survival in fluctuating environments (Smith and Wilkinson 1969) supply the
structure of chapters 3 and 7. Senescence, programmed cell death and cancer as
cheating (Williams 1957; Kirkwood 1977; Kerr, Wyllie and Currie 1972; Aktipis
et al. 2015), resilience (Holling 1973), and metabolic scaling (West, Brown and
Enquist 1997; Brummer, Savage and Enquist 2017) shape chapters 6, 8 and 11.

**Cybernetics and control.** Ashby's law of requisite variety and the good
regulator theorem (Ashby 1956; Conant and Ashby 1970) give the program its
account of suppression. Autopoiesis and viability theory (Maturana and Varela
1980; Aubin 1991) describe self-maintenance and continuation under constraint.

**Economics, decision and games.** Option value under irreversibility (Arrow and
Fisher 1974; Henry 1974) is the logic the central wager extends. Dynamic
programming (Bellman 1957), the distinction between conditioning and
intervention (Pearl 2009), causal and functional decision theory (Lewis 1981;
Yudkowsky and Soares 2017), Newcomb's problem (Nozick 1969), the Kelly
criterion (Kelly 1956), bargaining and proportional fairness (Nash 1950; Kelly
1997), overtaking in optimal growth (von Weizsäcker 1965), the rejection of pure
time discounting (Ramsey 1928), optimum population (Dasgupta 1969) and the
governance of commons (Hardin 1968; Ostrom 1990; Binmore 2005) are all used
directly.

**Philosophy and ethics.** The comparative account of harm (Feinberg 1984), the
hypothetical imperative (Kant 1785), Hume's gap (Hume 1739), conatus (Spinoza
1677), virtue as disposition (Aristotle), population ethics and personal
identity (Parfit 1984; Nozick 1974, 1981), negative utilitarianism and its
critics (Smart 1958), existential risk (Bostrom 2013), the hard problem
(Chalmers 1995) and the cosmopsychist literature (Shani 2015; Goff 2017;
Chalmers 2016; Miller 2018) set the questions chapters 5 to 8 answer.
Relativity's block picture (Putnam 1967), the statistical account of time's
arrow (Albert 2000), Everettian probability and branching (Deutsch 1999;
Vaidman 1998; Saunders and Wallace 2008), and the mathematical-universe and
ruliad proposals (Tegmark 2008; Wolfram 2021) frame chapter 10.

**Psychology and neuroscience.** Affect as a progress signal, learned
helplessness, attentional narrowing, the smoke-detector principle, central
sensitization, self-stimulation and congenital insensitivity to pain (Carver
and Scheier 1990; Seligman and Maier 1967; Maier and Seligman 2016;
Easterbrook 1959; Nesse 2005; Woolf 2011; Olds and Milner 1954; Cox et al.
2006), together with postdiction, the global workspace and interoceptive
accounts of presence (Eagleman and Sejnowski 2000; Dehaene, Kerszberg and
Changeux 1998; Seth, Suzuki and Critchley 2012), are the evidence behind the
functional account of suffering and consciousness.

**AI safety.** Instrumental convergence and power-seeking (Omohundro 2008;
Bostrom 2012; Turner et al. 2021), corrigibility and the off-switch game
(Soares et al. 2015; Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017; Carey 2018), side-effect
measures (Krakovna et al. 2019; Turner, Hadfield-Menell and Tadepalli 2020),
reward hacking and Goodhart's law (Amodei et al. 2016; Goodhart 1975;
Strathern 1997; Manheim and Garrabrant 2018), and Pascal's mugging (Bostrom
2009) define the problems the alignment framework addresses.

**Philosophical and theological traditions.** Where chapter 10 maps the program
onto older ideas — the Logos (Heraclitus), procession from a first principle
(Plotinus), eternity (Boethius; Aquinas), the apophatic way (Pseudo-Dionysius),
substance monism (Spinoza 1677), the calculus of distinction (Spencer-Brown
1969) and the pattern that connects (Bateson 1972) — the weight is carried by
those traditions, and the program notes only where its own structure meets
them.

## 13.2 How to read the citations

A citation identifies borrowed machinery or a point of contact. It does not
imply that the cited authors endorse the program, or that the program accepts
the rest of their views. Where the program extends a result, the chapter says
which part is borrowed and which is its own.

The manuscript is a draft, and this list was assembled from the chapters' own
reference lists. Entries have not yet been individually checked against their
sources, and details such as page ranges may change in later editions. The
bracket after each entry gives the chapters that cite it.
