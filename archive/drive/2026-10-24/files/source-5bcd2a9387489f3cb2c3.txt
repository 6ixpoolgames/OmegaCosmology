FORMAL RESULTS AND PROVEN CONSTRAINTS
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

This document collects the project's strongest formal and finite-model results that remain relevant to the current research program.

The results fall into three broad classes:

    theorem-level results
        consequences proved from explicit finite definitions and hypotheses;

    exhaustive finite witnesses
        counterexamples or separation examples showing that one property
        does not imply another;

    structural constraints
        lessons that any later physical, consequence, compatibility,
        or ethical formalization must respect.

The formal program is strongest in finite continuation, abstraction, recovery, and compatibility. These results do not by themselves solve the newer quantum-Perspective problem. They constrain what a successful solution may look like.


1. EVIDENCE TYPES

Formal result:
    A mathematical consequence of explicitly stated definitions and hypotheses,
    proved in the formal development or by complete finite enumeration.

Finite counterexample:
    An explicit model showing that a proposed implication fails.

Reproducible computational result:
    A result produced by an implemented finite instrument or exhaustive search.

Definition:
    A precise project object whose adequacy is a separate question.

The important distinction is:

    a valid theorem establishes what follows from a model;
    it does not establish that the model is the uniquely correct representation of reality.


2. PRIMITIVE SEPARATION RESULTS

Early formal work separates several concepts that are easily conflated.

Finite witnesses establish that one may have:

    relation without the relevant notion of distinction;

    distinction without continuation relation;

    relation and distinction without asymmetry;

    local asymmetry without global reachability irreversibility.

Conversely, sufficiently typed asymmetry entails at least the distinctions and relations required for the asymmetric comparison.

The enduring lesson is:

    distinction,
    relation,
    orientation,
    and irreversibility

must not be silently identified.

This matters downstream because consequence, time orientation, agency, and ethical loss rely on different combinations of these structures.


3. EXACT ABSTRACTION BY STRONG LUMPABILITY

For finite controlled Markov models, action-wise strong lumpability provides an exact abstraction criterion.

Let q partition concrete states into aggregate blocks.

If, for every action a, any two concrete states x and x' in the same aggregate block send identical total probability mass into each target aggregate block B,

    sum_{y in B} P(y | x,a)
        =
    sum_{y in B} P(y | x',a),

then the aggregate dynamics are well defined.

Under compatible initial conditions, target predicates, and policies, the abstract and concrete models agree on the corresponding aggregate finite-horizon behavior.

This establishes a clean baseline:

    exact coarse dynamics are possible when the abstraction satisfies the appropriate transport condition.


4. EXACT DYNAMICAL FAITHFULNESS DOES NOT PRESERVE EVERY PROPERTY

A crucial negative result is that exact lumpability can preserve aggregate transition dynamics while erasing a directional property that exists in the finer model.

A canonical finite witness collapses a biased cycle to a coarser state space.

The aggregate process is dynamically valid at the declared abstraction level, yet a fine-grained forward/reverse directional statistic disappears.

Therefore:

    exact abstraction of dynamics
        does not imply
    preservation of every physically or conceptually relevant invariant.

This is one of the project's strongest general lessons.

A consequence-preserving abstraction must preserve the property under investigation, not merely produce valid coarse dynamics.


5. PRESENTATION SOUNDNESS

The formal program treats a presentation or coarse description as sound only relative to the facts it is intended to preserve.

A sound presentation may not merge states that a registered consequence context requires to remain distinct.

Schematically:

    if context C distinguishes x and y consequentially,

then a C-sound presentation must not identify them.

This is the formal ancestor of the present topology-first requirement that legitimate coarse descriptions respect physically inherited structure.


6. REPRESENTATION-DEPENDENT ARTIFACTS

Finite examples show that quantities may change under a representation even when the represented dynamics remain equivalent for some narrower purpose.

This motivates a general constraint:

    a physically meaningful observable must be invariant,
    covariant, or explicitly indexed under admissible changes of presentation.

The newer quantum work inherits this as the canonicality problem for Perspective and consequence geometry.


7. FEEDBACK AND EFFECTIVE IRREVERSIBILITY

Finite controlled systems show that feedback can create functionally non-invertible behavior even when primitive operations are individually reversible.

The controller's use of information can produce:

- state-dependent selection;
- loss of accessible alternatives;
- effective directional structure;
- persistent records.

Thus:

    primitive reversibility
        does not imply
    effective process reversibility.

This supports the broader view that time-directed or irreversible organization can emerge at a higher process level.


8. RECOVERY IS FACT-RELATIVE

Recovery is defined relative to:

- a registered fact or requirement;
- allowed repair operations;
- resource constraints;
- a horizon.

A state can count as recovered relative to one fact language and not another.

Therefore:

    recoverability is not a context-free binary property of a physical state.

This result is important for consequence and ethics because "reversible harm" must always specify what is being restored.


9. BOUNDED RECOVERY

Given a registered target fact F, repair class R, and horizon H, bounded recovery asks whether some allowed repair restores F within H.

Schematically:

    Recover_{F,R,H}(x)
        iff
    exists repair r in R
        such that F is restored within H.

This provides an exact finite predicate.

The complementary notion of nonrecoverable contraction occurs when a registered fact is lost and no allowed repair restores it within the declared frame.


10. PHANTOM RECOVERY

Finite witnesses show that an incorrect or overly coarse recovery model can report restoration that does not exist in the concrete system.

This is phantom recovery.

The general structure is:

    abstract or corrupted frame:
        recovery appears possible;

    concrete frame:
        required fact remains lost.

This motivates recovery reflection:

    a recovery claim made in an abstraction must be checked against the concrete system before it licenses action.


11. RECOVERY-FRAME REFLECTION

Under explicit bridge conditions between a believed recovery frame and the true recovery frame, a recovery-aware license can be transported soundly.

The theorem pattern is:

    if the abstraction faithfully reflects the registered facts,
    repair actions,
    and relevant transitions,

then a licensed recovery claim in the abstract frame corresponds to a valid concrete recovery claim.

This is a model of a broader methodological rule:

    justification must survive representation change.


12. LOCAL RECOVERY DOES NOT DETERMINE JOINT RECOVERY

Finite multi-agent examples establish that each member of a family can possess an individual recovery route while no jointly compatible recovery exists.

Thus:

    for every i, Recover_i

does not imply

    JointRecover({i}).

Repair resources or required actions can conflict.

This result carries directly into multi-valuer ethics.


13. MAY COMPATIBILITY

For a family F of continuation requirements, define its realization set

    Real(F).

May compatibility is:

    May(F)
        iff
    Real(F) != empty.

It is an existential property.

At least one joint realization must exist.


14. ROBUST COMPATIBILITY

Given an environment class E and policy class Pi, robust compatibility is:

    Robust_E(F)
        iff
    exists pi in Pi
        such that
    for every e in E,
        outcome(pi,e) belongs to Real(F).

It asks for one policy that secures the family across all declared contingencies.


15. ROBUST IMPLIES MAY

A formally established implication is:

    Robust_E(F)
        -> May(F),

assuming the environment class is nonempty in the intended finite setup.

If one policy secures the family in every environment, then a joint realization exists.

The converse fails.


16. MONOTONICITY UNDER ADDING REQUIREMENTS

If F is enlarged by adding more continuation requirements, its realization set cannot grow.

Schematically:

    F subseteq G

implies

    Real(G) subseteq Real(F).

Thus adding requirements can only preserve or shrink joint realizability.

This is a basic but important law of the compatibility structure.


17. MONOTONICITY UNDER ENVIRONMENT ENLARGEMENT

If the uncertainty environment is enlarged, the set of policies that robustly secure a family cannot grow.

If

    E subseteq E',

then

    Secure_{E'}(F)
        subseteq
    Secure_E(F).

Robustness is therefore harder, never easier, when more contingencies must be covered.


18. PAIRWISE MAY DOES NOT IMPLY JOINT MAY

The hollow-triangle counterexample establishes:

    May({A,B}),
    May({A,C}),
    May({B,C})

while

    not May({A,B,C}).

Compatibility is therefore non-flag.

A compatibility complex cannot in general be reconstructed from its graph of pairwise edges.


19. PAIRWISE ROBUST DOES NOT IMPLY JOINT ROBUST

An analogous finite witness establishes that every pair can be robustly securable while the full family is not.

Thus:

    pairwise robust compatibility
        does not imply
    higher-order robust compatibility.

This blocks any method that certifies collective viability solely from pairwise tests.


20. JOINT REALIZABILITY DOES NOT IMPLY JOINT ROBUST SECURABILITY

A family can satisfy:

    May(F)

while failing:

    Robust_E(F).

A standard witness uses indistinguishable environments requiring incompatible policies.

The family can coexist in each environment under some policy, yet there is no single policy that works before the environment is known.

This distinguishes:

    existence of a good realization

from

    existence of a robust controller.


21. LOCAL SUCCESS DOES NOT IMPLY GLOBAL POLICY

There are finite cases in which:

    for every environment e,
        exists policy pi_e that succeeds,

but:

    there is no one pi
        that succeeds for every e.

This motivates policy atlases.

The successful policy regions form a cover of the environment even when no global section exists.


22. POLICY COVERING NUMBER

Let

    S_pi =
        {e : policy pi secures F in e}.

Define

    kappa_F(E)
        =
    inf {
        |P| :
        E subseteq union_{pi in P} S_pi
    }.

Finite fixtures establish cases with:

    kappa = 1,
    kappa = 2,
    and no cover within the declared policy class.

The covering number measures complementary policy behavior under the specified interface.


23. POLICY COVERING DOES NOT IDENTIFY VALUER PLURALITY

The same controller may implement several conditional policies.

Therefore:

    kappa > 1

does not mathematically imply:

- multiple valuers;
- multiple conscious subjects;
- multiple authorities.

This is a typing constraint that prevents a structural property of policy coverage from being misread as a metaphysical or political result.


24. JOINT STRUCTURE IS NOT RECOVERABLE FROM MARGINAL SUMMARIES

Finite instruments establish examples where systems share the same individual or marginal descriptors but differ in joint behavior.

Matched summaries can include:

- individual recovery profiles;
- vector counts;
- marginal continuation attributes;
- span-like statistics;
- degree sequences.

Yet the systems differ in:

- joint compatibility;
- joint recovery;
- coupling structure;
- relational composability.

Therefore:

    joint structure contains irreducible information
    not recoverable from the list of individual profiles.

This is a recurring formal theme throughout the program.


25. NONREDUNDANCY AND SPAN

Finite covariance or feature-space constructions illustrate that repeated copies of one direction and several independent directions can have equal total mass while differing in rank and effective dimension.

If

    Sigma_narrow
        ~ M |v><v|

and

    Sigma_broad
        ~ sum_k (M/r) |u_k><u_k|,

then rank-sensitive, participation-ratio, or log-determinant summaries distinguish them.

This does not establish one canonical Lushness measure.

It establishes why raw multiplicity cannot recover nonredundant geometry.


26. IID NOISE AS A PREDICTIVE-COMPRESSION RESULT

For an IID process,

    P(X_future | h)
        =
    P(X_future)

for every history h.

Therefore all histories have the same exact predictive continuation.

An exact causal-state or predictive-sufficiency quotient collapses them to one predictive state.

This is a mathematically clean benchmark demonstrating that:

    high sequence entropy
        does not imply
    rich predictive continuation.

The result is standard information/process theory applied as a project constraint.


27. PERSISTENT MEMORY SEPARATES FROM IID NOISE

If IID outcomes are written into stable memory and later behavior depends on those bits, histories cease to be predictively equivalent.

Thus the addition of a persistent record changes the continuation quotient.

This is the simplest finite demonstration of the role of records in consequence.


28. PASSIVE PREDICTION DOES NOT DETERMINE INTERVENTION RESPONSE

Two controlled systems can have identical passive output distributions while differing under intervention.

Therefore a complete consequence representation for an agentive setting must generally preserve intervention response, not only uncontrolled prediction.

This is a standard controlled-process distinction and a load-bearing constraint on the project's residual continuation object.


29. NON-MARKOVIAN PROCESSES REQUIRE MEMORY

Finite processes can be constructed in which the naive current observable state is insufficient to predict the future.

A sufficient continuation state must retain hidden or historical information.

Thus:

    current appearance
        need not equal
    sufficient predictive state.

This motivates process-level residual continuation rather than assuming a simple Markov present.


30. COHERENT QUANTUM ALTERNATIVES CANNOT BE REPLACED BY CLASSICAL MIXTURES IN GENERAL

Standard quantum interference provides the relevant formal constraint.

Two quantum states or histories can share the same classical-looking marginal statistics while differing in relative phase.

If later operations can reveal interference, the difference remains physically consequential.

Therefore:

    coherence must be retained
    until the physical process renders phase information inaccessible.

This is not a new theorem of the project; it is a direct requirement inherited from quantum mechanics.


31. DECOHERENT HISTORY PROBABILITIES REQUIRE THE APPROPRIATE CONSISTENCY CONDITION

For histories alpha and beta with class operators C_alpha and C_beta,

    D(alpha,beta)
        =
    Tr(C_alpha rho C_beta^\dagger).

Ordinary additive probabilities over the history alternatives are justified only when the relevant interference terms vanish or are negligible under the appropriate decoherence condition.

Thus:

    fine-grained history possibility
        cannot generally be treated as
    an ordinary additive probability distribution.

This constrains any quantum Lushness or branch-count proposal.


32. PROCESS DISCRIMINATION INDUCES PHYSICAL DISTANCES

Standard operational quantum theory provides coordinate-independent distinguishability metrics.

Examples:

    trace distance for states;

    diamond-type distances for channels;

    strategy/process norms for interactive multi-time objects.

These establish a general tool:

    physical separation can be quantified by optimal discrimination advantage.

The current topology-first program uses these as possible probes of an already physical local structure.


33. DATA PROCESSING

Operational distances satisfy appropriate contraction principles under common physical transformations.

Schematically:

    D(Phi(X), Phi(Y))
        <=
    D(X,Y)

for suitable physical maps and distances.

This provides a powerful consistency requirement for consequence geometry:

    downstream processing should not manufacture distinguishability from nothing.


34. ODT0 LICENSE

Within the declared formal decision frame, ODT0 checks whether a proposed action satisfies registered justification and continuation-corridor requirements.

Formal results establish that, under the reflection hypotheses encoded in the model, an ODT0 license entails:

- preservation of the declared corridor;
- validity of the registered justification in the reflected frame.

The theorem is conditional on the supplied register and bridge conditions.


35. ODT1 PARTIAL DOMINANCE

ODT1 defines partial comparison relations over licensed options.

Formal properties include:

- reflexive/transitive preorder behavior under the supplied comparison rules;
- explicit witnesses when a dominance relation fails;
- preservation of incomparability rather than forced totalization.

This gives the decision layer a mathematically typed way to represent unresolved comparison.


36. ODT2 FINITE ARBITRATION

For a finite unresolved frontier and a registered natural-number violation score, a least-score candidate exists.

This is a simple finite minimization result.

It establishes:

    the arbitration procedure can return a least registered score.

It does not establish the normative legitimacy of the score.


37. STATIC CERTIFICATES CANNOT COVER GENUINELY NEW REGISTER FACTS

A fixed-domain static certificate cannot certify a later requirement that introduces a genuinely new fact outside its domain.

This is an important formal constraint on adaptive systems.

As the register grows, justification machinery must also grow or be recomputed.

One cannot infer future adequacy from a certificate that never represented the new distinction.


38. FORMAL RESULTS AS DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

The most durable constraints for the current v2.5 program are:

A. Coarse-graining must preserve the specific structure being used downstream.

B. Pairwise structure cannot substitute for higher-order joint structure.

C. Realizability and robust controllability are distinct.

D. Marginal summaries cannot in general reconstruct joint consequence.

E. Recovery claims require explicit fact, repair, and horizon semantics.

F. Operationally or physically equivalent descriptions should not produce different physical conclusions.

G. Quantum coherence must remain represented while it can affect continuation.

H. A predictive state may require memory and intervention-sensitive process structure.

I. Scalar summaries cannot be assumed to preserve the geometry from which they were compressed.


39. RELATION TO THE CURRENT PHYSICAL FRONTIER

The newest physical program asks for:

    universal quantum possibility
        -> intrinsic local structure
        -> Perspective
        -> consequence geometry.

The finite results do not yet derive this chain.

They provide requirements for it.

In particular, any proposed Perspective and consequence map should be tested for:

- representation robustness;
- correct treatment of coherence;
- preservation of non-Markovian memory;
- joint versus marginal structure;
- sound coarse-graining;
- downstream reflection;
- multiscale stability.

These are the proven constraints inherited by the next stage of research.
