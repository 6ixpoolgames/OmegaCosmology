CONSEQUENCE-PRESERVING COMPRESSION
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

Once a physically situated Perspective and its residual continuation are identified, the next problem is compression.

The goal is to remove descriptive redundancy while preserving every distinction that changes consequential continuation at the chosen physical scale of analysis.

The core question is:

    What is the smallest representation of the past or present that preserves the physically consequential future?

This problem connects the project to mature work in causal states, predictive state representations, sufficient statistics, process tensors, quantum strategies, rate-distortion theory, information bottlenecks, bisimulation, model reduction, and operational process discrimination.

These mathematical tools do not define the underlying ontology. They provide ways to represent and compress a consequence structure that is already physically grounded.


1. WHY COMPRESSION IS NECESSARY

A universal physical description contains vastly more detail than any local consequential description needs.

Examples of potentially redundant information include:

- microscopic labels with no effect on later dynamics;
- thermal fluctuations that rapidly mix away;
- alternative descriptions related by symmetry;
- historical details whose future effects have completely recombined;
- inaccessible environmental degrees of freedom;
- duplicate encodings of the same physical distinction.

If every microdifference were counted separately, any measure of possibility richness would be dominated by raw state-space size.

The program therefore seeks a representation that preserves what matters to continuation while eliminating distinctions that are future-equivalent.


2. THE RESIDUAL CONTINUATION MAP

Let h denote a physically realizable history or conditioning state.

Map it to its residual continuation process:

    h -> Upsilon_P^h.

This separates two kinds of information.

Historical information:

    what physically happened in detail.

Continuation information:

    what physical process remains possible after that history.

The project is primarily interested in the second when constructing consequence geometry.

Many histories may therefore map to the same continuation.


3. EXACT PREDICTIVE EQUIVALENCE

In an ordinary stochastic process, two histories h and h' can be called predictively equivalent when

    Pr(future | h) = Pr(future | h').

For a controlled system, the requirement must hold under every physically relevant future action policy pi:

    Pr(Y_future^pi | h)
        =
    Pr(Y_future^pi | h')

for all pi.

The equivalence classes define predictive states.

This construction is attractive because it does not group histories by superficial similarity. It groups them according to identical continuation.

In the project, the same idea is applied only after the physical Perspective and continuation structure are identified.


4. CAUSAL STATES

Computational mechanics provides a particularly clean uncontrolled example.

Define

    h ~ h'

iff

    Pr(X_future | h)
        =
    Pr(X_future | h').

The resulting equivalence classes are causal states.

They form a minimal exact predictive representation under standard conditions.

This is closely aligned with the project's desired compression principle:

    retain only distinctions in the past that make a difference to continuation.

Causal-state machinery is therefore a natural mathematical reference point for classical finite models.


5. PREDICTIVE STATE REPRESENTATIONS

Predictive State Representations extend the idea to controlled systems.

Rather than representing a system through an assumed hidden latent state, a PSR represents it through predictions of outcomes of future experiments or action-observation sequences.

A state can be represented schematically by

    s(h)
        =
    { Pr(test succeeds | h) }_tests.

This is useful for consequence compression because a physically situated system is defined partly through what continuation it supports under intervention.

The representation can therefore encode future behavior directly rather than reconstructing an arbitrary microscopic latent state.


6. QUANTUM PROCESS REPRESENTATIONS

The quantum analogue requires more than a probability distribution over future observations.

Multi-time quantum processes can contain:

- memory;
- temporal entanglement;
- intervention dependence;
- non-Markovian correlations;
- incompatible instruments;
- coherent alternatives.

Quantum combs, process tensors, and quantum strategies provide mature carriers for this structure.

The residual continuation can therefore be represented schematically as

    Upsilon_P^h
        =
    conditional quantum process after h.

Two histories can then be considered continuation-equivalent when they induce the same conditional process.

This is stronger than matching one measurement distribution. It preserves how the process would respond to the whole physically relevant range of later interactions.


7. MINIMAL SUFFICIENT REPRESENTATION

Let S_P(h) denote a compressed state of history h.

An exact sufficient representation should satisfy:

    S_P(h) = S_P(h')
        implies
    Upsilon_P^h = Upsilon_P^h'.

A minimal sufficient representation retains no distinction that can be removed without losing continuation information.

The ideal exact pipeline is

    h
        -> Upsilon_P^h
        -> S_P(h).

The first map is supplied by physical dynamics.

The second removes redundancy.


8. APPROXIMATE COMPRESSION

Exact equality is often too strict.

Real physical systems are noisy, continuous, large, and computationally inaccessible. Two continuation processes may differ only by a physically negligible amount.

This leads to approximate compression.

Let D_cont be a physically meaningful distance between continuation processes.

Then seek a compressed representation Z satisfying approximately

    D_cont(
        Upsilon_P^H,
        Upsilon_P^Z
    )
        <= epsilon

on average or with a suitable worst-case criterion.

One schematic optimization is

    minimize representation complexity

subject to

    expected continuation distortion <= epsilon.

An information-theoretic version may take the form

    minimize I(H;Z)

subject to

    E[D_cont(Upsilon^H,Upsilon^Z)] <= epsilon.

The exact objective need not be universal. The important principle is maximal compression subject to bounded loss of consequential continuation.


9. PREDICTIVE RATE-DISTORTION

Predictive rate-distortion theory studies precisely this tradeoff.

The past is compressed while preserving information relevant to the future.

As the allowed distortion approaches zero, the representation approaches exact predictive sufficiency.

As distortion tolerance increases, increasingly fine distinctions are merged.

This gives a natural multiscale family of consequence-preserving descriptions.

Rather than introducing an arbitrary ontological cutoff, the program can study how continuation structure simplifies as physically meaningful resolution changes.


10. INFORMATION BOTTLENECK

The information bottleneck provides a related template.

One seeks a representation Z of past information H that compresses H while retaining information about a relevance variable Y:

    maximize predictive relevance
    while minimizing retained past information.

For the present program, the relevance object is not merely a selected label Y. It is the physical residual continuation.

This makes the conceptual target a process-level or future-structure information bottleneck.


11. OPERATIONAL PROCESS DISTANCE

Approximate compression requires a distance or divergence between continuation processes.

For ordinary probability distributions, total variation or related statistical distances may suffice.

For quantum states, trace distance measures optimal single-shot discrimination advantage.

For quantum channels, diamond-norm-type distances capture discrimination under ancillary assistance.

For multi-time strategies or process tensors, strategy norms and interactive process discrimination provide higher-order analogues.

The general operational idea is:

    two continuations are close when no physically available interaction can make them behave very differently.

Within the current topology-first program, such distances are ways of characterizing physical separation in continuation space, not arbitrary choices of semantic relevance.


12. DATA PROCESSING

A sensible consequence compression should respect data processing.

If two continuation states are already close, applying the same downstream physical transformation should not create a larger distinguishability than the transformation itself physically permits.

This is captured by contraction properties of many operational distances.

Schematically,

    D(
        Phi(Upsilon),
        Phi(Upsilon')
    )
        <=
    D(
        Upsilon,
        Upsilon'
    )

for an appropriate physical map Phi.

Data-processing behavior is important because it prevents representation from inventing consequence that the physical process does not contain.


13. OVERLAP AS DERIVED STRUCTURE

Once a distance D_cont has been physically justified, an overlap measure need not be introduced independently.

One can define, for example,

    O(Upsilon,Upsilon')
        =
    Psi(D_cont(Upsilon,Upsilon'))

for a monotone decreasing function Psi.

Exact equivalence is the limiting case

    D_cont = 0.

Thus overlap, similarity, and partial redundancy can be treated as derived features of consequence geometry.

This is preferable to postulating a separate overlap axiom without connection to physical continuation.


14. RECOMBINATION UNDER COMPRESSION

Suppose two histories differ for a time but eventually induce the same residual continuation.

Then compression naturally merges them:

    h != h'

but

    S_P(h) = S_P(h').

This is effective recombination.

Nothing requires the universal physical history to be deleted. The compressed consequence state simply records that the old distinction no longer changes continuation.

This distinction between physical history and current consequence state is central to the program.


15. MEMORY AND NON-MARKOVIANITY

A compressed present need not be Markovian in the naive state-variable sense.

If the future depends on long-range historical information, then the sufficient predictive state must retain whatever memory is necessary.

This may be:

- a finite hidden state;
- a belief state;
- a recurrent predictive state;
- a process-tensor memory;
- a causal-state class;
- an infinite-dimensional sufficient statistic.

The compression principle does not assume that the world is memoryless.

It asks instead:

    what is the smallest state that makes the remaining continuation conditionally complete?


16. COMPUTATIONAL IRREDUCIBILITY

The exact consequence state may be well defined even when a bounded agent cannot calculate it efficiently.

This motivates distinguishing:

    physical consequence state

from

    computational approximation to the consequence state.

A finite agent may maintain

    Shat_P^C

under computational budget C.

As C increases, the approximation may improve without ever reconstructing the universal process in full.

This provides a natural place for bounded rationality, model uncertainty, and approximate control later in the program.


17. MODEL REDUCTION

Several mature reduction methods are relevant implementation tools.

Examples include:

- balanced truncation;
- Hankel singular-value methods;
- Mori-Zwanzig projection;
- state aggregation;
- bisimulation and approximate bisimulation;
- spectral model reduction;
- tensor-network compression.

These methods are useful insofar as they preserve continuation-relevant behavior.

The project does not require one universal algorithm. Different physical regimes may admit different efficient representations of the same general compression principle.


18. COMPRESSION AND CAUSAL STRUCTURE

A valid compression should preserve not only passive prediction but also the relevant intervention structure.

Two states can produce the same uncontrolled trajectory distribution while responding differently to intervention.

If those interventions are physically available, the states are consequentially distinct.

Therefore the appropriate compressed object in agentive or controlled settings is generally closer to an intervention-aware process state than to a passive forecast distribution.


19. COMPRESSION AND QUANTUM COHERENCE

A compression that replaces every coherent alternative by a classical mixture too early can destroy real future consequence.

If relative phase remains capable of affecting later interference, it must remain represented.

Only after the relevant coherence has become physically inaccessible or decohered can an approximately classical compression merge the alternatives without losing continuation information.

This is a central quantum constraint on the compression program.


20. COMPRESSION ACROSS SCALE

The same physical process may support useful consequence representations at several scales.

At fine scale, many microscopic distinctions remain.

At intermediate scale, fast-mixing details disappear while stable control variables remain.

At macroscopic scale, the compressed state may consist of objects, agents, records, institutions, or ecological variables.

A successful program should show how these descriptions relate through lawful coarse-graining rather than treating one level as uniquely fundamental for all purposes.


21. FROM COMPRESSION TO GEOMETRY

After continuation-equivalent or approximately redundant states are merged, what remains is a space of nonredundant consequence states.

Call this space schematically

    Gamma_P.

Gamma_P is not merely a set.

It inherits structure from:

- the physical topology of continuation;
- process composition;
- causal relations;
- operational distinguishability;
- conditioning dynamics;
- quantum interference and decoherence;
- physical weights.

The next question is:

    What geometry does this compressed consequence space carry?

That geometry is the subject of the next document.
