QUANTUM PASTS, TRAJECTORIES, AND FOCUS
v2.5 checkpoint
Current through 21 September 2026

CURRENT STATUS

The current program distinguishes configuration, trajectory direction, and remote focus as three different structures involved in organizing quantum history relative to a Perspective. The framework uses semiclassical mechanics, decoherent histories, pre/postselection mathematics, process theory, and boundary formulations as technical guides while seeking a representation-independent quantum account.

The main idea is that a present Perspective can be compatible with multiple quantum pasts and multiple future continuations, while still supporting stable effective trajectories at the scale where records and decoherence provide sufficient structure.


1. CONFIGURATION, TRAJECTORY DIRECTION, AND FOCUS

The three concepts are separated as follows.

Configuration:

    a possible physical arrangement or local support on a boundary or cut.

Trajectory direction:

    an oriented tendency of continuation through neighboring physical configurations or process stages.

Focus:

    a remote condition relative to which compatible history or trajectory contributions are being resolved.

A focus may lie in what the Perspective calls the future or past. It need not be an actually observed endpoint. It can function as a mathematical boundary condition, effect, query, or target condition used to organize the contributing histories.

The distinction is important because quantum theory does not generally identify a state with one classical path and velocity.


2. SEMICLASSICAL SUPPORT FOR THE DISTINCTION

In semiclassical mechanics, the analogy becomes precise enough to motivate the broader program.

Write

    psi(q) = R(q) exp(i S(q)/hbar).

In suitable regimes, the phase function S defines a local canonical momentum

    p(q) = grad S(q).

Thus the same configuration q can carry directional information through the phase gradient.

Now consider a propagator from present configuration q at time t to a remote configuration q_f at t_f:

    K(q_f,t_f;q,t)
        approximately
    sum_a A_a exp(i S_a(q_f,q)/hbar).

The stationary-action contributions a define a family of compatible classical or semiclassical paths. Their initial directions satisfy schematically

    p_t^(a) = - partial S_a / partial q.

This illustrates:

    one present configuration
        + one remote focus
        -> a family of compatible trajectory directions.

The project treats this as strong mathematical motivation, not a universal definition. Outside semiclassical canonical mechanics, "trajectory direction" may need to be represented through generators, process derivatives, currents, channel structure, or another object.


3. MULTIPLE COMPATIBLE PASTS

A present Perspective does not generally determine one unique microscopic past.

Let P_t denote the Perspective's current physical support. A schematic family of compatible past histories is

    H^-(P_t) = { alpha : alpha contributes nonzero amplitude through P_t }.

This expression is intentionally broad. In a histories formalism, alpha can label a sequence of quantum alternatives. In a path-integral formulation it can refer to a path contribution. In a process formalism it may correspond to an earlier instrument/outcome history compatible with the current process state.

The key point is that a present condition can be reached by many physically admissible contributions.

These are not automatically interpreted as ignorance about one hidden classical history. Quantum interference can remain physically relevant among them.


4. RECORD-COMPATIBLE PASTS

A Perspective normally possesses records that constrain the family of compatible pasts.

Suppose R_r is a stable present record. Then the relevant history family is restricted to histories compatible with R_r.

In a decoherent-histories description, one can use class operators

    C_alpha = P_{alpha_n}(t_n) ... P_{alpha_1}(t_1)

and a decoherence functional

    D(alpha,beta) = Tr(C_alpha rho C_beta^dagger).

If the histories associated with robust records decohere, then the Perspective can assign ordinary probabilities to the relevant coarse alternatives.

The effective past is therefore not arbitrary reconstruction. It is physically constrained by present records, the universal dynamics, and decoherence relations.


5. HOW MANY PASTS CAN FORM ONE TRAJECTORY

Many fine histories can agree on the same robust coarse sequence.

For example, microscopic environmental interactions may differ enormously while preserving the same macroscopic motion of a baseball. A biological organism's exact molecular history can vary while its recorded behavioral trajectory remains stable. A detector may have multiple microscopic environmental histories consistent with the same registered click.

This yields the structure:

    many microscopic histories
        -> one record-compatible effective trajectory.

The trajectory is therefore an emergent stable pattern across histories, not necessarily one exact microtrajectory.

This supports the broader program's treatment of identity and persistence as organizational rather than purely material.


6. FOCUS

A focus is a condition away from the present that helps resolve the set of compatible histories or directions.

A future focus may be represented by a positive effect E_F at time tau > t.

The effect can be propagated to the present:

    E_F(t) = U(tau,t)^dagger E_F U(tau,t).

For present state rho_t, the compatibility of the future condition can be evaluated as

    p(F | rho_t) = Tr(E_F(t) rho_t).

For a pure state |psi_t>, this becomes

    p(F | psi_t) = <psi_t|E_F(t)|psi_t>.

This representation shows how a remote condition can have a well-defined present mathematical image.

The focus need not be an event that actually occurs. It can be used as a probe of the structure of possibility.


7. PRE- AND POSTSELECTION MATHEMATICS

Time-symmetric quantum inference provides a mature formal neighborhood.

Suppose rho_t represents information propagated forward from earlier conditions, while E_t represents information propagated backward from a later condition.

For an intermediate measurement operation M_k, a two-sided conditional probability can take the schematic form

    p(k | past, future)
        proportional to
    Tr(E_t M_k rho_t M_k^dagger).

This shows that future boundary information can reorganize the relative weight of intermediate alternatives.

The project borrows the mathematics as a way of representing remote focus and two-sided constraints. It does not require the claim that the future physically causes the past or that the universe has a unique postselected final state.


8. FOCUSED HISTORY COHERENCE

A remote focus can be included in a histories-style interference diagnostic.

A candidate focused decoherence functional is schematically

    D_F(alpha,beta)
        = N Tr(E_F C_alpha rho C_beta^dagger),

where N is a normalization factor.

This object combines:

- an initial or present quantum condition;
- two history alternatives;
- a remote effect or focus;
- the interference cross term between those histories.

Its role in the program is exploratory. It demonstrates that focus-relative history coherence can be expressed within established quantum mathematics.

The larger research question is whether a representation-independent structure of focused history bundles can be defined without making one histories interpretation fundamental.


9. COHERENCE

The project uses a physically operational meaning of coherence:

    alternatives are coherent when their relative phase can still influence at least one physically admissible continuation.

This avoids defining coherence metaphorically as simply "pointing toward the same future."

Two history contributions can converge on the same record but remain coherent at one scale and decohere at another. Conversely, two apparently similar macroscopic histories can become permanently separated by environmental records.

Any trajectory/focus formalism must preserve these distinctions.


10. TRAJECTORY OUTSIDE CLASSICAL MECHANICS

The word trajectory becomes more difficult in fully quantum regimes.

Possible generalizations include:

- probability or amplitude currents;
- Bohmian velocity fields in a specific interpretation;
- sequences of decoherent projectors;
- paths in projective Hilbert space;
- process-tensor histories;
- channel composition paths;
- causal network paths;
- geodesic or variational directions in state space;
- semiclassical stationary-phase bundles.

The program does not currently select one as universal.

The physically important content may be weaker than a literal path: an oriented relation among local supports that composes through the universal process.

Thus "trajectory" may ultimately mean a stable oriented process structure rather than a continuously parameterized worldline in configuration space.


11. FOCUS AND UNCERTAINTY

The focus concept suggests a way to interpret some quantum uncertainty geometrically.

Past-resolving, present-resolving, and future-resolving questions can all be represented at the present boundary. Some of these questions correspond to noncommuting operators or incompatible process interventions.

If

    [A,B] != 0,

then one cannot simultaneously sharpen both resolutions arbitrarily:

    Delta A Delta B >= (1/2) |<[A,B]>|.

This motivates the hypothesis that quantum uncertainty can sometimes be understood as an obstruction to simultaneously sharpening incompatible ways of focusing the universal possibility structure around one Perspective.

This is an interpretive hypothesis, not a replacement for standard uncertainty relations.


12. SHARP PRESENT, DIFFUSE PAST DIRECTION

The current picture yields useful qualitative dualities.

A sharply specified present configuration can remain compatible with a spread of incoming trajectory directions.

A sharply specified trajectory or future focus can remain compatible with a spread of earlier configurations.

In semiclassical phase space this resembles position/momentum complementarity. In the broader program it suggests a triangular relation among:

- location/support;
- directional continuation;
- remote focus.

The mathematical task is to express this triangle without relying on a special coordinate system.


13. FUTURE CONTINUATION

From a present Perspective P_t, the universal process supplies a family of possible continuations.

These may include coherent alternatives initially. Environmental interaction can then produce decohered records, yielding approximately classical branch structure.

A remote focus F restricts or reweights the set of continuations relevant to a particular analysis.

One can therefore think schematically:

    P_t
        -> family of coherent continuations
        -> decoherence / record formation
        -> effective future alternatives.

This structure later feeds into consequence analysis, where the program asks which present differences lead to distinct residual future processes.


14. FOCUS IS NOT VALUE OR GOAL

The word "focus" does not imply purpose, preference, teleology, or an agent's goal.

A focus is a structural condition used to resolve histories or continuations.

An agent's goal can later be represented as one kind of focus if the physical policy actually conditions behavior toward a target. But the quantum concept is broader.

This separation is important for keeping the physical theory prior to the normative theory.


15. FOCUS AND COMPOSITIONAL BOUNDARIES

General-boundary formulations suggest that a remote focus need not be defined by a global time coordinate.

A focus can instead be associated with data on another boundary of a spacetime region.

The present Perspective occupies one local boundary; another region or boundary carries the remote condition. The amplitude associated with the intervening region links them.

This may provide a covariant route to the focus concept:

    present boundary data
        <-> quantum region/process <->
    remote boundary condition.

Such a formulation could avoid treating "future" as one globally defined half-space.


16. PATH INTEGRALS AND MULTIWAY POSSIBILITY

Path-integral language provides another useful intuition.

A propagator is constructed by summing amplitudes over many histories:

    K(q_f,t_f;q_i,t_i)
        = integral Dq exp(i S[q]/hbar)

schematically.

The physical amplitude is not obtained by selecting one path first. The family of paths jointly contributes, with constructive and destructive interference.

In the semiclassical limit, stationary-action paths dominate and classical trajectory structure emerges.

The project uses this as a model of a broader transition:

    universal multiway quantum possibility
        -> coherent history bundles
        -> decoherence / stationary structure
        -> quasiclassical trajectories.

This is closer to the current physical intuition than beginning from a classical branching graph.


17. RECORDS AND TRAJECTORY DIRECTION

Stable records can constrain not only position-like facts but direction-like facts.

A sequence of records can encode motion, momentum, orientation, causal tendency, or control state. A macroscopic trajectory becomes stable because present records contain information about earlier positions and transitions.

The trajectory direction of a Perspective is therefore partly inherited from record structure.

This may explain why a present state can possess a well-defined effective direction even though many microscopic histories remain compatible with it.


18. TRAJECTORY SPLITTING AND BRANCHING

When future alternatives decohere, one effective trajectory can split into multiple branch-relative successors.

The program avoids assuming that this produces a countable set of fundamental worlds. Rather, branch structure is an emergent organization of the underlying quantum process.

Questions of personal identity across such splitting are downstream. At the physical level, the relevant structure is:

- common record-compatible past;
- present support;
- multiple decohering continuations;
- branch-relative future records.

This is the physical geometry that later identity theory must interpret.


19. THE PRESENT AS THE INTERSECTION OF PAST AND FOCUS

A useful conceptual synthesis is that the present Perspective sits at the meeting point of:

- accumulated constraints from compatible pasts;
- current local support;
- possible directional continuations;
- remote focus conditions used to resolve those continuations.

The present is therefore not merely a point on a timeline. It is a structured interface at which multiple history contributions and future possibilities are organized into one local physical situation.

This is one reason Perspective is central to the program: the universal object alone does not tell us which past/future organization is locally instantiated without specifying the situated support and records.


20. MAIN RESEARCH QUESTIONS

The present technical questions are:

1. What replaces classical trajectory direction in a fully quantum, representation-independent formalism?

2. Can the configuration/direction/focus triangle be expressed using quantum information geometry, process structure, or boundary amplitudes?

3. What precisely defines the family of past histories compatible with a Perspective?

4. How do stable records select an effective quasiclassical trajectory from a coherent history bundle?

5. Can focused decoherence functionals be used without selecting one interpretation of quantum mechanics?

6. How should remote focus be represented in relativistic quantum field theory?

7. How are focus, postselection mathematics, and ordinary conditional prediction related?

8. When does a remote condition change only our description, and when does it identify a physically distinct conditional process?

9. How do multiple local Perspectives with different records reconstruct mutually compatible histories?

10. Which aspects of trajectory and focus survive abstraction to nonquantum possible worlds?

These questions define the program's current attempt to reconstruct history and temporal orientation from the quantum possibility structure itself.
