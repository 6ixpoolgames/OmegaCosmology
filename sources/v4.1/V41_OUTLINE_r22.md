## Part I — The problem

The reader learns why standards of good are stuck, what a way out would have
to provide, and why the way out starts from what can follow.

### 1. Who counts?
- **Question.** Ethics guides action: what to do, what to permit, what to
  protect and what to become. Whose lives, relations and possibilities enter
  that guidance, and why has no answer ever been settled?
- **Answer.** Every standard settles who and what counts before the inquiry
  begins, so every dispute about that starting point is a dispute about the
  whole standard. More argument cannot end it.
- **Builds.** A survey of the standards (welfare, preference, rights, virtue,
  contract, capabilities, biocentric ethics) and the starting points each
  depends on; the is–ought problem stated fairly.
- **Then.** Why artificial agents make the problem urgent: they need a standard
  that does not depend on whose preferences trained them, favours no group,
  and stays defined as their understanding grows.
- **Hands on.** Three requirements for a way out:
  - begin with no decision about who counts;
  - stay well defined;
  - be testable.
- *Primers:* moral philosophy; AI objectives.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* one response to the stalemate is to deny there is anything to settle. Chapter 30 answers it.

### 2. Possibility as a standard
- **Question.** What must a world support for anything to matter, including
  forms of value no one has anticipated?
- **Answer.** What can follow from its presents.
  - **Whatever can matter happens physically.** Acts, experiences,
    relationships, institutions and their consequences all occur through
    some physical development. Their conditions of possibility therefore
    belong to the physical structure of what can follow. This is the
    program's starting commitment about the world, made precise in chapter 9
    as value requires valuers.
  - **Histories, not end points.** A history includes the act itself, how it
    happened and the relations involved, not only where it ends. Building on
    what can follow does not reduce a duty or a virtue to its eventual
    consequences.
  - **What can follow presupposes no one.** It is defined by physics before
    anyone is chosen to count, it contains every candidate for what matters
    without choosing among them, it stays defined as understanding grows, and
    it can be tested: the three requirements of chapter 1.
  - **Earlier attempts.** Others have built on freedom, options or
    possibility, and stalled at four points:
    - **grain:** what counts as a different option;
    - **weighting:** how likely options count;
    - **subject:** whose options;
    - **busyness:** whether noise counts as possibility.

    Any proposal must resolve all four.
  - **What waits for Part III.** Why what can follow bears on value (value
    requires valuers; value still to come needs valuing to continue;
    uncertainty about where it will arise) is argued in chapters 9 to 11,
    after the object and its measure are in place.
- **Builds.**
  - Freedom of choice: Pattanaik and Xu.
  - Capabilities.
  - Option value: Arrow, Fisher and Henry.
  - Empowerment, causal entropic forces and maximum occupancy.
  - Open-endedness.
- **Hands on.** The four points become the agenda of Part II.
- *Status:* E (literature); the case for what can follow is argued from the
  starting commitment that whatever can matter happens physically.
- *Sources:* v4 chapter 5.4 ("The same uncertainty selects the object"); the
  progenitor's minimal physical ontology (Gradient Ethics draft, section 2).

## Part II — The object and its measure

Each chapter opens with the ethical problem its physical tool resolves. The
reader then meets the tool.

### 3. What follows from a present
- **Question.** Chapter 2 concluded that a standard should be about what can
  follow. What, physically, is that?
- **Answer.** It is built from the present outward. A present is a frame's
  state now: it holds the past only as traces, and the future only as what it
  can give rise to. An orientation gives it a before and an after, a frame
  makes it the present of something, and the law fixes what it can give rise to.
  Gathered from every present, that is Omega. No stock quantity of the
  present (energy, order, stored information) settles what follows; the
  differences lie in what can follow, and in what each part of the world can
  reach.
- **Order of the chapter:** the present; orientation; frames; the law,
  histories and futures; Omega; why present stocks do not determine what
  follows; access.
- **What it does not do.** It defends no comparison and establishes no
  ethical claim. The normative commitments and the central thesis are stated
  separately, in chapters 9 to 11.
- **Builds.**
  - **Present-rooted, not presentist.** Everything is assessed from a
    frame's present, the past acts only through traces, and futures are what
    the present supports. No metaphysical thesis that only the present exists
    is assumed; relativity singles out no universal present, and the
    interpretations of chapter 29 are handles on Omega.
  - Plain-English definitions: orientation, frame, present, history, Omega,
    access; the present is introduced informally first, then defined once
    what it needs is in place. "Future" is the plain name for the histories that can follow a
    present.
  - **The present:** a sufficient physical specification for the
    continuation law being assessed; for quantum frames, the state, dynamics,
    actual couplings and relevant environmental correlations. Conditioning
    on an exact hidden configuration is a separate construction.
  - **Omega's probabilities** belong to classical histories and to the
    records physical frames make; frames' record laws need not combine into
    one physically realizable history.
  - **Access** is the physically realized availability of continuation within
    the law: machinery, records, resources, couplings and repair set which
    developments occur and how likely each is. It acts through the law, never
    as a multiplier on the measure.
  - **Orientation** orders occurrences into before and after, partially
    where events are spacelike separated; a frame gives the standpoint from
    which what follows is defined. Chapter 28 gives the program's proposed
    account of where orientation comes from.
  - What Omega is not: a probability over beliefs, or a utility.
- *Primer:* probability and histories.
- *Formal:* A.1.
- *Drafted (chapter 3 r6).*

### 4. Counting futures
- **Question.** How much possibility does a present hold? The grain problem and
  the weighting problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** Physics decides what counts as the same, and probability decides
  how much there is. Four requirements on any count of futures leave one measure,
  the perplexity of the futures: lushness.
- **Builds.**
  - resolution;
  - the continuation structure;
  - lushness;
  - the branch-volume form: effective branches through any stage, times the
    geometric mean of the breadths within them (A.2.6);
  - horizons as later sections; the profile as the object where the field
    has no end;
  - why similarity weighting is excluded;
  - counting everything, randomness included;
  - what the count is called: the exponential of path entropy, the effective
    number of resolved continuation histories; the per-step perplexity of
    language modelling is 2^h, the rate, never lushness;
  - the compression boundary: what the measure cannot see.
- *Primers:* entropy and perplexity; lushness is not the entropy of heat.
- *Formal:* A.1.1, A.2.
- *Drafted (chapter 4 r7).* Its sequence: what can be distinguished, how much
  it amounts to, how breadth is generated, how it accumulates, and what the
  number leaves out.

### 5. Futures through time
- **Question.** Should a head start you can see now count for more than a lead
  that grows later?
- **Answer.** Compare the whole profile of the difference. Its slope is the
  rate, its level the offset, and its zero the crossover horizon. Over
  everything that follows, rate decides first and offset second.
- **Builds.**
  - the profile;
  - rate and offset;
  - the crossover horizon;
  - preparation, process and mechanism contrasts;
  - the offset theorem.
- *Primer:* Markov processes.
- *Formal:* A.3, A.3.9.
- *Drafted (chapter 5 r4).* Three worked examples, one per kind of
  comparison: a workshop that builds before acting (process), a robust and a
  fragile machine (mechanism), a full and an empty fuel tank (preparation).
  Only process comparisons are choices from one present.

### 6. How long things last
- **Question.** Should what exists be preserved? Is a fast gain worth taking if
  it may not last?
- **Answer.** In the long run, persistence is worth the rate it sustains
  beyond whatever would replace it.
  - A regime is a physical operating condition, not a law: its successor can
    branch by a different effective law under the same native law.
  - A gain must repay its cost over its expected life (the robustness floor).
  - An early end costs least when the world can rebuild what was lost, and most
    when it never can.
  - Long shots are bounded by a finite world, and a finite generative window
    caps the expected payoff of any rate gain.
- **Builds.**
  - random horizons;
  - what persistence is worth;
  - the cost of an early end and regeneration;
  - the generative window;
  - gambles and finite stakes.
- *Primer:* expected value.
- *Formal:* A.4, A.5.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* transience does not zero worth. What a finite regime adds is real.
- *Drafted (chapter 6 r3).*

### 7. Resolution: what counts as different
- **Question.** Rankings can flip with how finely or how often we look. Can any
  verdict be trusted?
- **Answer.** Declare the resolution. Let physics decide what counts as a
  difference: a distinction is faithful if physics can register it. Compare
  options at one resolution. Of two faithful descriptions, the finer
  governs once its extra detail is shown faithful, and the fullest faithful
  description, the judgement of the ultimate frame, is the target. A coarser
  ranking is certified when what it leaves out cannot reverse it, corrected
  when a finer level shown faithful reverses it, and a resolution-dependent
  reversal when a finer level of open faithfulness reverses it. Read finely enough, the
  busier option wins, so timing counts only as finely as the system's own
  physics can record it. A clock is physical: finer recording is a different
  process under the same native law, and can suppress what it records.
- **Builds.**
  - faithful resolutions and the governing description;
  - the joint grain of two faithful grains, for compatible histories;
  - the temporal expansion, for random waiting times;
  - the crossover resolution, and why timing needs a physical criterion;
  - certifying a ranking from a coarser description;
  - the physical development, its records and the analyst's sampling;
  - recording as a physical act: the quantum Zeno effect, and whether finer
    recording pays as an option comparison;
  - limited frames as approximations of the ultimate frame;
  - access limited by the law itself (absolute uncertainty in Bohmian
    mechanics), without a universal minimum cell or time step.
- *Formal:* A.6.
- *Drafted (chapter 7 r4).* Three worked examples: a box with a pocket
  (grain), a relay and a wandering molecule (cadence), and a recorded
  two-level system (recording).

### 8. Parts and wholes
- **Question.** Whose futures? The subject problem, from chapter 2.
- **Answer.** The measure applies to any part of the world or to the whole. A
  part never has more lushness than the whole. Self and whole differ by
  exactly one term: what an act does to everything else, given what it does to
  the part. Parts the act cannot affect drop out, and there is no ledger of
  subjects to keep.
- **Builds.**
  - projections and subjects;
  - bounds on a part's lushness;
  - intertwining;
  - the self–whole gap;
  - subjects can disagree;
  - unaffected parts cancel.
- *Primer:* mutual information.
- *Formal:* A.7.1–A.7.7, A.7.10.
- *Drafted (chapter 8 r3).* Four worked examples: a relay and a lamp
  (intertwining), a thermometer in a heated room (bounds), one supply and
  three machines (disagreement and the gap), a recorder and the weather
  (cancellation, and when it fails). The rest-given-the-part term fixes the
  gap between the two rankings; it is not by itself a cost to the rest.

## Part III — From physics to value

The turn from what there is to what matters.

### 9. Value requires valuers
- **Question.** Physics describes what can follow. Where does value enter?
- **Answer.** Value requires valuers: without something that values, nothing
  physical is good or bad. This is the step from the physical to the moral,
  not a moral rule.
  - A valuer is anything with a frame of its own, from which its futures can
    be projected.
  - Every physical system counts, gas included. The whole world is the
    ultimate frame.
- **Builds.**
  - valuer; the ultimate frame;
  - what makes a frame physically instantiated (the candidate, and the open
    problem);
  - persistence as the metavalue;
  - disvalue as narrowing;
  - the principle of practical reason;
  - why a hedonic definition would bring back negative totals.
- *Formal:* A.7.1; A.15.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* value is a relation within physics, not something projected onto it.

### 10. The comparison with no index
- **Question.** Self-interest and ethics: two objectives, or one?
- **Answer.** One measure, two comparisons. The indexed one, for the self, is
  what prudence tracks. The one with no index, for the whole, is what the
  thesis says ethical judgement tracks.
- **Builds.**
  - agent-neutral and agent-relative reasons;
  - why expect ethics to track the whole (intertwining, no privileged
    boundary, a standard others can use);
  - misappropriation by power-seekers, and the defence the whole-field
    standard licenses;
  - an infinite world: an infinite whole-field total need not prevent a
    well-defined comparison between localized alternatives, when the options
    leave the exterior unchanged.
- *Formal:* A.7.8–A.7.9, A.7.11.

### 11. The central thesis
- **Question.** What exactly is claimed?
- **Answer.** What we recognize as good corresponds to widening of the whole
  field's futures, and what we recognize as harm to their narrowing.
  - Why expect it: persistence and uncertainty together turn value requires valuers into
    breadth.
  - What it is not: a definition of good, or a rule to maximize entropy.
  - Mechanism words (organization, cooperation, domination) carry no sign.
- **Builds.**
  - narrowing, widening and foreclosure;
  - the largest narrowing;
  - the proof procedure: one class of cases at a time, each with positive and
    negative cases, matched controls, compression cases and resolution tests,
    and no hidden value terms;
  - what failure would look like.
- *Formal:* A.9.2–A.9.4; A.15.

## Part IV — Earning the thesis I: organization

The first test classes. Every result carries its model and its scope.

### 12. How the thesis is tested
- **Question.** What can small exact models show, and what can they not show?
- **Answer.** There are two layers:
  - the oracle layer, which discovers what lushness ranks;
  - the bounded-agent layer, which discovers what an agent inside the world
    can know and do.

  Every test is a comparison that states where it starts, the options, what is
  held fixed, the level of detail and the horizon. It reports the profile,
  narrowing, foreclosure, sign stability and whether it is resolved. A result
  counts only if its model contains what the claim is about; otherwise it is a
  diagnostic. A failed claim drawn from the principle changes the theory.
- **Builds.**
  - the five conditions and the diagnostic;
  - "the same valuer without X";
  - reading results: design guesses and claims; two-factor reporting; the
    nine questions;
  - the gas baseline: capability, not margin;
  - reproducibility;
  - the single-model ideal.
- *Drafted (chapter 12 r10).* Its one-line question: under a common physical
  law, a matched endowment and the same boundary conditions, how does
  organization change the weighted continuation?

### 13. Organization against disorder
- **Prediction 1.** Organized systems reach, within the time available,
  futures that disorder never reaches.
- **Builds.**
  - counterfactual access;
  - locked gradients;
  - gas and same-law comparisons;
  - capability, not proportion;
  - the uncatalysed control that was a more permissive law.
- *Status:* M, E.

### 14. Information pays through action
- **Prediction 2.** Knowing more widens the future only when the knowledge is
  used to act.
- **Builds.**
  - a register adds only its unpredicted switching;
  - "If your internal state cannot influence the world, might as well dream";
  - informed action;
  - the repayment horizon.
- *Status:* T, M.

### 15. Persistence and compounding
- **Question.** Can structure that lasts and builds on itself raise the rate
  at which futures open?
- **Answer.** The models show that it can, and how: retention, funding, and
  rate growing with capacity. Real systems offer physical correspondences
  (free-energy capture, sustained adaptation) that motivate the rate
  hypothesis. Whether organization raises the rate at which futures open in
  real systems is open, and needs tests of its own, named in chapter 33.
- **Builds.** Uptime and payoff; the enabling loop; three-link compounding;
  error thresholds; the split by regime, which makes uptime times excess
  branching exact up to a conditional-branching and an interaction term
  (A.3.10); where a gain sits is a hypothesis for the declared partition.
- *Status:* M, E, with three tiers [P1].
- *Primer:* dissipative and self-maintaining systems.
- *Nihilism, briefly:* compounding can raise the rate at which futures open. The structure is not indifferent between organization and disorder.

### 16. Activity is not breadth
- **Question.** If the measure counts everything, must it prefer noise and
  busyness to structure? The strongest objection, at full strength.
- **Answer.** Activity pays now and capacity pays later. The activity-matched
  comparator and the compression pairs decide it. Results are given if they
  have landed; otherwise the objection is stated as open.
- *Status:* M, open.

## Part V — Earning the thesis II: loss

### 17. Harm as narrowing
- **Question.** What does harm look like in the structure?
- **Answer.** Narrowing relative to what else could have been done, never a
  level below zero.
  - A harm that lowers the rate eventually outweighs one with a finite cost.
  - Irreversibility makes an early end most costly.
  - Material injury and narrowing are different things, and the
    funded-extraction case tests the gap between them.
- **Builds.**
  - offset and rate harms;
  - recovery;
  - foreclosure beside narrowing;
  - turnover across scales.
- *Formal:* A.9, A.4.9.

### 18. Protection: channels, not stock
- **Answer.** Keep channels open; do not maximize preservation. Dependence
  produces maintenance. The structure of a harm sets policy, not its raw
  hazard.
- **Builds.** Criticality as a statement about being, not ethics;
  precautionary protection; protection without a valuing premise; stronger
  protection around agents with high leverage, from the expected cost of
  irreversible error under uncertainty, not from status (A.9.5, A.3.11).
- *Status:* M, T.

### 19. Suffering
- **The test class.** A world of obstructed suffering is less lush than the same
  world, with the same obstruction and the same valuer, without the suffering.
- **Builds.**
  - the measure detects valence only through its effect on the future;
  - the factory-farming objection as the named adversarial target;
  - register controls.
- *Status:* M, C.

## Part VI — Earning the thesis III: many valuers

### 20. Cooperation and joint access
- **Answer.** Cooperation is joint access. Concentration of control costs
  breadth only through what it does to the continuation.
- **Builds.** Coupling at fixed marginals; division and concentration; control
  invariance; the commons.
- *Formal:* A.10, A.2.5.

### 21. Constraint and domination
- **Prediction 3.** Constraint that blocks others' access narrows the whole,
  even when it widens the futures of whoever imposes it.
- **Builds.**
  - domination as a setup, not a verdict;
  - where constraint turns from good to bad, located by a sweep;
  - the self–whole conflict measured;
  - the boundary in time.
- *Status:* M.

### 22. Justice, forgiveness and virtue
- **Answer.** Each is a dynamic of the continuation structure: coordination,
  repair and durable policy. Protection among the governed is the core of a
  rule of law.

### 23. Persons, populations and minds
- **Builds.**

## Part VII — Acting without knowing

### 24. Choosing inside the structure
- **Answer.** A bounded agent decides step by step. The decision recursion is
  consistent because the measure composes. It acts on finite horizons, with
  bounds on what it has not seen.
- *Formal:* A.13, A.12.

### 25. One uncertainty, many places
- **Answer.** Precaution, option value, deference, keeping mistakes correctable,
  and a margin for model error are one structure:
  - uncertainty;
  - information that may arrive;
  - what stays actionable;
  - how reversible a step is.

  Irreversibility is not a further value.
- **Builds.** The robustness floor in practice; anti-fanaticism; correction
  channels priced by regeneration; the identification problem: which agents
  will matter is seldom known in advance, so precaution scales with the
  leverage at stake, using the expected complete difference, not a constant
  excess rate.
- *Primer:* value of information.
- *Formal:* A.9.5–A.9.6, A.13.3.

### 26. Controllers that reshape the field
- **Builds.** What an embedded controller is physically; state-dependent
  optima; maximum occupancy.
  - **The second transition** (after the progenitor paper): the capacity to
    alter the processes that generate future agency, its own or others'.
  - **Leverage of a choice:** bounded, rate and superlinear, as consequence
    signatures of that capacity, not its definition. A self-enabling network
    can saturate and show only rate leverage; noise can grow faster than
    linearly with nothing that acts.
  - **A test:** whether a sharp threshold appears in a control parameter,
    such as the ratio of repair to error.
- *Status:* M, D, open.
- *Formal:* A.3.11–A.3.12.

### 27. Alignment
- **Answer.** What the framework offers artificial agents:
  - an evaluator;
  - a decision structure;
  - conditions for correction;
  - a test bed.

  Its risks: misappropriation by self-directed optimizers, risk neutrality,
  and the compression boundary.
- *Primer:* corrigibility.

## Part VIII — The whole

The account's setting. The argument of Parts I–VII does not depend on it, and
Part VIII says so in its first paragraph. Earlier chapters mention Alpha at
most in passing (orientation in chapter 3, irreversibility in chapters 6 and
17), each with a pointer here and a statement that their argument does not
need it. The cosmology gets its full treatment here (ruling 77).

### 28. Alpha, Logos and Omega
- **To develop in full** (ruling 77): the cosmology's own case, stated at the
  strength it deserves, separate from the ethics it does not support.
- **Builds.**
  - the hierarchy: Alpha, the generative dynamics; the adapter, a universe's
    native law as Alpha's dynamics express it; the Omega of that universe,
    its complete continuation structure. Different adapters, different
    Omegas. Instruments, clocks and observers are processes inside an
    Omega, under its law; the adapter is not an instrument;
  - the governing resolution as adapter-relative in this cosmological sense,
    not observer-relative (chapter 7);
  - distinction, relation and asymmetry;
  - the Logos;
  - the chain from asymmetry to frame, valuer, continuation and lushness;
  - irreversibility and directional asymmetry;
  - orientation darwinism.

### 29. Quantum frames
- **Answer.** A physical frame's recorded histories have an ordinary law.
  - Records made at spacelike separation do not depend on the slicing.
  - Comparisons are adjudicated from a frame above the branching.
  - Where incompatible records have no physical common refinement, their
    joint classical history remains unresolved. Alternative interventions
    remain comparable from their shared frame before the branch.
- **Builds.** Instruments and records; common refinement; extended Wigner's
  friend.
  - **Realizations of Omega, as handles on it.** Bohmian mechanics: the
    wavefunction, the configuration and the guidance law; a frame as an
    organized subsystem with its records and couplings, guided by its
    conditional wavefunction; empty branches as possibility carried but not
    occupied. Everett: the encompassing development and the emergence of
    records. Bohmian mechanics, Everett and standard quantum theory give the
    same record statistics, and so the same lushness from records; they
    differ about what is ultimately actual. Collapse theories put chance in
    the law and predict small departures, which experiments constrain.
- *Primer:* decoherence and records.
- *Formal:* A.14 (with the remark on the Bohmian realization at A.14.3).

### 30. Completion, choice and meaning
- **Cosmic nihilism, head-on.** Grant every premise: no value built in,
  nothing watching, everything ends.
  - Value is a relation within physics (chapter 9).
  - The structure is not indifferent: organization reaches futures that
    disorder never reaches (chapters 13 and 15).
  - Ending does not zero anything. A finite history still differs in total
    lushness (chapters 5–6).
  - The ultimate frame is a standpoint needing no observer.
  - The honest limit: the premises leave the ordering standing; whether it is
    value is the thesis.
- **Builds.**
  - what completion means;
  - choice in a completed whole;
  - the ultimate frame and the limit of description;
  - infinite totals and finite comparisons (chapter 10);
  - what the account means, and what would make it false.

### 33. The program ahead
- **Open problems:**
  - the criterion for physical frames (the lattice program);
  - tests of generativity's rate in real systems;
  - comparisons between frames that disagree about records;
  - estimators for realistic systems;
  - the matching rule;
  - attribution of a difference among interacting channels;
  - formalizing the second transition;
  - whether physics bounds every faithful resolution (finite energy and
    finite time with finitely many distinguishable alternatives), and the
    finest faithful resolution of each model;
  - how much timing information a given physical system can preserve or make
    consequential, given its native dynamics and finite resources, including
    the clocks needed to register it; whether it has a smallest faithful
    cadence, a bounded amount of recordable timing information without a
    sharp cutoff, or a more complicated structure;
  - whether the encompassing frame should ever condition on variables no
    record can fix (such as the Bohmian configuration), which makes every
    lushness 1 under a deterministic law; the record construction is the
    working one;
  - infinite fields: a joint limit in space and time;
  - the confirmatory protocol [P2].
- **Research directions:**
  - the feasibility frontier: under one law, endowment and boundary
    conditions, which combinations of extent, concentration, throughput and
    persistence the substrate can support;
  - the rival access reading, deferred until it is defined. Read as a
    capacity from a frame's state, it counts memory as well as influence; a
    usable version needs physically implemented interventions, their costs
    and the consequences they can distinguish. Once defined, it is computed
    alongside lushness, with the cases where the two disagree deciding
    between them.
- The order of work.
